# Packaged food and beverage in the United States: psychology, rules, competitors, foreign entry history, AI outlook (checked October 2026)

Scope: **foreign packaged food and drink brands entering the U.S.** (snacks, confectionery, noodles and pantry, dairy and plant-based, non-alcoholic drinks), with a short note on alcohol. Restaurants are in the separate restaurants note. U.S. companies appear only as competitors. Supplements and GLP-1 nutrition needs are in the US health wellness research; front-of-pack halo research is in the health wellness consumer and claims research. This note merges two research passes; peer-reviewed findings come first, and community sources (Reddit, articles) are kept as qualitative evidence of how people think.

## Customer psychology

### Fact
- **GLP-1 drugs shrink the snack basket.** 12% of U.S. adults are currently taking a GLP-1 drug and 18% have ever taken one (n=1,350, Nov 2025). [KFF](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/poll-1-in-8-adults-say-they-are-currently-taking-a-glp-1-drug-for-weight-loss-diabetes-or-another-condition-even-as-half-say-the-drugs-are-difficult-to-afford/) In a panel of about 150,000 households, adopters cut grocery spending 5.3% within six months (over 8% for higher-income households); savory snacks fell about 10%, with similar drops in sweets, baked goods and cookies, while yogurt, fresh fruit, nutrition bars and meat snacks rose modestly. [Hristakeva, Liaukonytė and Feler, "The No-Hunger Games," Journal of Marketing Research, 2025, DOI 10.1177/00222437251412834](http://dx.doi.org/10.1177/00222437251412834), summarized by [ScienceDaily, Jan 2026](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/01/260112001029.htm)
- **Store brands keep gaining.** Private label reached a record $282.8B in 2025, growing 3.3% versus 1.2% for national brands, with 21.3% dollar share and a record 23.5% unit share; refrigerated (+6.1%) and beverages (+4.8%) led food. [PLMA/Circana, Jan 2026](https://www.plma.com/article/us-private-label-industry-reached-2828-billion-sales-2025)
- **Trust in food safety is at a record low.** 65% of Americans are confident grocery food is safe, down from 73% a year earlier and 81% in 2019 (Gallup); for the first time most have little or no confidence in the federal government on food safety. Imported food is not broken out. [Food Dive, Oct 2026](https://www.fooddive.com/news/food-safety-consumer-confidence-gallup/832856/)
- **Korean food is the standout import.** Korean ramyeon exports passed $1.5B in 2025 (+21.9%); the U.S. is the top buyer of Korean agro-food ($1.8B). [Korea JoongAng Daily, Jan 2026](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/food-exports-hit-record-high-in-2025/12391090)
- **Trial starts in social video.** Analysts credit spice-challenge videos for Buldak's growth. [Aju Press, Nov 2025](https://www.ajupress.com/view/20251117162859124) TikTok says food sales on TikTok Shop more than doubled year on year; winners are visual, easy to demonstrate, or have unusual flavors or a benefit a creator can explain. [Food Dive, Jun 2026](https://www.fooddive.com/news/big-food-companies-tik-tok-shop/823959/)
- **Mainstream shoppers use Asian grocers, and that aisle is crowded.** Nearly one third of H Mart's customers are not Asian American (its president, 2024); it has about $2B in sales. [Commercial Observer, Jul 2026](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/07/h-mart-expansion-sun-belt/) Calbee said U.S. sales of its Japanese-origin brands fell in the year to March 2026 because of "a more competitive market for Asian and ethnic foods." [Calbee, p. 9](https://www.calbee.co.jp/en/ir/pdf/2026/Calbee_FY2026_results_presentation.pdf)
- **Simple ratings work best.** In FDA's test with 9,200 U.S. adults, a High/Med/Low nutrient box helped people pick the healthier product most accurately; "High In" warnings did worst. [FDA, May 2024](https://www.fda.gov/media/185007/download?attachment)
- **Labels change behavior, unevenly.** A meta-analysis found food labels shifted consumer diets (for example, lower calorie and fat intake) and pushed industry reformulation, with effects varying by label type and setting. [Shangguan et al., "A Meta-Analysis of Food Labeling Effects on Consumer Diet Behaviors and Industry Practices," American Journal of Preventive Medicine, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30573335/) Color-coded labels and warnings were linked to more healthful purchases. [Song et al., PLOS Medicine, 2021, via FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2021/10/08/Red-for-stop-green-for-go-Study-measures-effectiveness-of-colour-coded-front-of-pack-labels-on-consumer-behaviour/)
- **Wallet context:** U.S. households spent an average of $6,224 on food at home in 2024 (+2.8%, not statistically significant). [BLS Consumer Expenditures 2024](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cesan.htm)
- Also cited in the earlier pass but not yet checked here: reviews of health and nutrition claims, "natural" claims that backfire when expectations are broken, fruit-drink imagery that distorts sugar perception, and sustainability-label willingness to pay [VERIFY before quoting].

### Customer language (Reddit and articles, qualitative only)
- Snack, protein, tea and grocery communities keep debating taste versus macros, sweeteners and digestive effects, ingredient simplicity, value per serving, and whether a viral product actually gets bought again. [r/ProteinBars search](https://www.reddit.com/r/ProteinBars/search/?q=taste%20texture&restrict_sr=1), [r/tea search](https://www.reddit.com/r/tea/search/?q=wellness%20tea&restrict_sr=1) Use as interview and product-test prompts, not market data.
- Background reading: Michael Moss, *Salt Sugar Fat* (how big food engineers repeat purchases); Michael Pollan, *The Omnivore's Dilemma* (provenance and identity in American food).

### Inference
- **The journey:** a foreign food is first tried as a discovery or "challenge" purchase (TikTok, YouTube, an Asian or specialty grocer, Amazon), then becomes a pantry repeat at Walmart, Costco or Kroger. Buldak and Biscoff followed this path.
- **Credibility:** with food-safety trust falling, it comes from visible U.S. compliance (English label, allergen statement, named U.S. importer), a trusted retailer and a specific origin story.
- **"Premium" means an affordable treat with a concrete reason** (origin, ingredient, flavor intensity), priced near the national brand, because store brands are winning the value shopper.
- **Taste and occasion come first.** Health, ethics and function claims raise interest but rarely rescue a product people don't enjoy; lead with the eating or drinking occasion and the expected taste, then the ingredients.
- **Likely first customers (segment by occasion, not demographics):**

| Segment | Main barrier | Trial trigger | First channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Better-for-me convenience (breakfast, desk meal) | Taste, price, "processed" doubts | Familiar format, sample, clear nutrition | Grocery, office, gym, own-site refills |
| Ritual drink adopter (coffee, tea, hydration) | Off-flavors, claim distrust | Cafe or creator demo, cold sample | Cafes, natural retail, convenience |
| Global-flavor explorer | Authenticity, spice and prep uncertainty | Small pack, prep video, diaspora endorsement | Asian and specialty grocers, social |
| Ethical treat buyer | Premium price, greenwashing | Traceable story plus great taste | Specialty, Whole Foods, gifting |
| Macro-focused buyer | Texture, sweeteners, stomach effects | Transparent macros, blind taste proof | Gym, club, Amazon |
| Alcohol-alternative drinker | Medicinal taste, price | Occasion sampling, adult flavors | Bars and restaurants, specialty |

- "Swicy" and global-flavor trend data were not checked [VERIFY].

**So what for an entrant:** Lead with one shareable flavor or format and seed it with creators and Asian or specialty grocers before paying for mass shelf space. Put U.S. compliance and origin on the pack. Plan a portion-controlled or higher-protein version for GLP-1 households.

## Rules and regulatory risk (2025-2026)

### Fact
**Getting food into the country (FDA)**
- FDA does not pre-approve imports, but they must meet U.S. rules, come from registered facilities, have prior notice, and can be inspected and detained at the port. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-imports-exports/importing-food-products-united-states)
- **Registration:** foreign manufacturers register and renew every two years, Oct 1-Dec 31. **2026 is a renewal year; registrations not renewed by Dec 31, 2026 expire.** A DUNS number is required; there is no fee. [FDA, Sep 2026](https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-reminds-food-facilities-biennial-renewal-requirements) Low-acid canned and acidified food processors must also register and file processes. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/industry/importing-fda-regulated-products/importing-human-foods)
- **Prior notice:** at least 2 hours before arrival by road, 4 by rail or air, 8 by water; otherwise food is refused and held. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-industry-what-you-need-know-about-prior-notice-imported-food-shipments)
- **FSVP:** the FSVP importer is the U.S. owner or consignee at entry or, if none, the foreign owner's U.S. agent. It must analyze hazards, approve and verify each supplier (audits, testing or record review), keep records, and give its DUNS on every entry line. [FDA, May 2026](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-foreign-supplier-verification-programs-fsvp-importers-food-humans-and-animals)

**Labels and claims**
- Labels must be in English, and most packaged foods need Nutrition Facts. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/industry/importing-fda-regulated-products/importing-human-foods) Nine major allergens, including sesame since Jan 1, 2023, must be declared in parentheses or a "Contains" statement. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies)
- **"Healthy":** redefined in Dec 2024 (food-group minimums, limits on added sugars, sodium and saturated fat); compliance date Feb 25, 2028; the effective date was later delayed [VERIFY exact date]. [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/27/2024-29957/food-labeling-nutrient-content-claims-definition-of-term-healthy) No "healthy" symbol yet. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/use-healthy-claim-food-labeling)
- **Front-of-package:** a proposed (Jan 2025) box rating saturated fat, sodium and added sugars Low, Med or High. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/front-package-nutrition-labeling) FDA's July 2026 list projects a final rule in December 2026 [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/guidance-documents-regulatory-information-topic-food-and-dietary-supplements/foods-program-regulations-under-development), with proposed compliance three years later ($10M+ sales) or four years (smaller firms). [Food Engineering](https://www.foodengineeringmag.com/articles/103400-fda-could-finalize-front-of-pack-nutrition-label-rule-in-spring-2026)

**Dyes and state laws**
- FDA revoked Red No. 3 for food on Jan 15, 2025. [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/03/west-virginia-passes-law-prohibiting-the-use-of-seven-food-dye-ingredients) The Apr 2025 phase-out of other petroleum-based dyes is **voluntary**: the target for six dyes moved to end-2027, FDA had not revoked them as of April 2026, and it began revoking Citrus Red No. 2 and Orange B. General Mills and Mars have committed; Mondelez and Coca-Cola had not. [Consumer Reports, Apr 2026](https://www.consumerreports.org/health/food-additives/one-year-later-are-synthetic-dyes-still-in-our-food-a5846944223/) Since Feb 2026, "no artificial colors" is allowed on products free of petroleum dyes even if they use natural colors. [Barley Snyder](https://www.barley.com/fda-signals-shift-from-petroleum-based-synthetic-food-dyes-new-labeling-flexibility-reformulation-timelines/) A trade outlet reports a further slowdown in mid-2026 [VERIFY]. [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/food-beverage/fda-slows-synthetic-dye-phase-out-as-160-food-and-ag-groups-press-for-usmca-renewal)
- **California** AB 418 bans brominated vegetable oil, potassium bromate, propylparaben and Red No. 3 from Jan 1, 2027; AB 2316 removes six dyes from schools by end-2027. [ASTHO](https://www.astho.org/communications/blog/2025/states-moving-to-prohibit-additives-and-dyes-in-food/)
- **West Virginia** HB 2354 removed seven dyes from school meals on Aug 1, 2025, followed by a statewide sales ban on those dyes plus BHA and propylparaben: Jan 1, 2028 per [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/03/west-virginia-passes-law-prohibiting-the-use-of-seven-food-dye-ingredients), Jan 1, 2027 per [ASTHO](https://www.astho.org/communications/blog/2025/states-moving-to-prohibit-additives-and-dyes-in-food/) [VERIFY].
- **Texas SB 25:** labels created from Jan 1, 2027 for foods with any of 44 ingredients must warn the ingredient is "not recommended for human consumption" in Australia, Canada, the EU or the U.K.; fines up to $50,000 per day per product. A court blocked enforcement against members of the suing trade groups on Feb 11, 2026; Texas appealed. [Haynes Boone, Mar 2026](https://www.haynesboone.com/news/alerts/an-update-on-warnings-on-texas-food-labels-administrative) Texas's rule exempts GRAS or FDA/USDA-cleared ingredients, which may narrow it. [CHLPI, Sep 2026](https://chlpi.org/news-and-events/news-and-commentary/food-law-and-policy/what-aba-v-paxton-means-for-state-food-warning-label-laws/)

**USDA and alcohol**
- Products with over 3% raw or 2% cooked meat or poultry, and processed egg products, fall under USDA: the foreign plant needs USDA approval and every shipment is reinspected. Meat broths and flavorings are hard cases. [OFW Law, 2026](https://ofwlaw.com/fda-vs-usda-who-regulates-what/)
- Alcohol needs TTB import permits and label approval, then each state's three-tier system [VERIFY: no primary source checked; see the US country doc, section 4].

### Inference
- 2026-2027 is a reformulation window: launching without targeted dyes and additives means one redesign instead of one per state.
- Factory location is now a pricing decision: EU biscuits pay 10%, Japanese goods are capped at 12.5%, China-made noodles may pay far more.

**So what for an entrant:** Pick a capable U.S. importer of record for FSVP and renew registration by Dec 31, 2026. Formulate without targeted dyes and additives from day one. Price with your HTS line and origin known, and have a broker confirm Annex II status.

## Competitors

### Fact
- **Big snacks consolidated:** Mars closed its $35.9B Kellanova deal (Pringles, Cheez-It, RXBAR) on Dec 11, 2025, creating about $36B in snack revenue. [Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/29495-mars-completes-acquisition-of-kellanova)
- **Incumbents buy or partner with challengers:** PepsiCo agreed to buy Poppi for $1.95B in March 2025 after its U.S. sales rose 167% to $359.9M (NielsenIQ). [BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2025/pepsico-to-acquire-poppi-in-1-95b-deal) Mondelez partnered with Lotus Bakeries on Biscoff products. [Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/mondelez-biscoff-lotus-cadbury-milka-chocolate-parnership-into-partnership/718818/)
- **Incumbents copy the playbook:** PepsiCo, Mars and Hershey are expanding on TikTok Shop. [Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/big-food-companies-tik-tok-shop/823959/)
- **Discounters expand:** Aldi planned 225 openings in 2025 and nearly 800 new stores by end-2028. [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/retail/lidl-us-ceo-exits-amid-aldi-battle-for-buyers-name-recognition-132847)
- **Asian grocers scale:** H Mart has over 100 locations in 18 states. [Grocery Dive](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/h-mart-large-california-flagship-store-food-hall/810184/)
- **Slotting fees:** a peer-reviewed study found retailers charge higher slotting allowances when they know more than the manufacturer about a new product's chances, lower-cost (more powerful) retailers receive more, and manufacturers with strong market share pay relatively less. ["The Price of Launching a New Product," Marketing Science, 2003, DOI 10.1287/mksc.22.2.246.16035](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mksc.22.2.246.16035) [VERIFY authors, believed Rao and Mahi] No reliable current fee data found [VERIFY]. UNFI and KeHE are the main natural and specialty distributors [VERIFY: not checked].

### Inference
- Three competitor types: an incumbent's line extension (spicy, protein, dye-free) that can copy a flavor within a season; a store-brand copy at Costco, Trader Joe's or Aldi; and other imports in the Asian or international aisle.
- **Channel path:** Asian or specialty grocer plus Amazon or TikTok Shop, then Whole Foods or regional chains via a distributor, then Walmart, Costco or Kroger. Slotting economics reward brands that arrive with proof of sales speed.
- **Channel economics:** natural and specialty (Whole Foods, Sprouts, Erewhon) give discovery but limited scale and distributor complexity; mass grocery brings volume with trade spend and strict service levels; club (Costco, Sam's, BJ's) takes few SKUs in big packs and punishes weak repeat; convenience is cold-box, single-serve competition; foodservice and cafes demonstrate the product in use; direct-to-consumer gives data but rarely works for heavy, cheap items; value chains and private label (Aldi, Trader Joe's) want exclusives. A product also competes with the occasion's alternatives: a protein bar with yogurt, a functional drink with coffee or water.
- **White space:** portion-controlled indulgence and higher-protein formats for GLP-1 households, dye-free global flavors, and origin-specific products U.S. brands cannot claim.

**So what for an entrant:** Don't fight incumbents on price or store brands on value. Win one flavor or origin niche, build sales data in specialty channels, then use that proof to win national shelf space.

## Foreign entry cases since 2016

Method: non-U.S. packaged food and drink brands that entered or scaled in the U.S. since about 2016. "Success" means active and growing at the October 2026 check. Yakult, Huel and Oatly's coffee-shop entry are in the US health wellness research.

### Successes
- **Samyang Foods (Buldak) / South Korea / U.S. growth from mid-2010s**
  - Entry mode: export into mass retail; Walmart added Buldak lines in 2024 and 2026 [Wikipedia, VERIFY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buldak_Ramen).
  - Positioning and moves: extreme-heat noodles as a challenge; the "Fire Noodle Challenge" began on YouTube in 2014. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buldak_Ramen)
  - Outcome: thriving. U.S. sales were $280M in 2024 (+127%), and a new Korean plant targets the U.S. and Europe. [Korea Herald, Jun 2025](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10506916) Q1 2026 U.S. sales rose 37%; about 80% of group sales are overseas. [Korea Herald, May 2026](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10737361) It raised U.S. prices in late 2025 to offset tariffs. [Aju Press](https://www.ajupress.com/view/20251117162859124)
  - Why (source): spice-challenge videos (analysts via Aju Press).
- **Nongshim / South Korea / U.S. plants 2005 and 2022**
  - Entry mode: local manufacturing in Rancho Cucamonga, California; Shin Ramyun as the hero.
  - Outcome: thriving. U.S. revenue reached $490M in 2022 (+24%, versus 9% for the group); a third U.S. plant was planned [VERIFY status].
  - Why: cause not reported. Source: [KED Global, Mar 2023](https://www.kedglobal.com/newsView/ked202303240023)
- **Lotus Bakeries (Biscoff) / Belgium**
  - Entry mode: U.S. production in Mebane, North Carolina [Lotus IR](https://www.lotusbakeries.com/sites/default/files/documents-en/IR_Presentation_2025_Annual_Results.pdf), with a new production hall planned; Mondelez partnership; a viral TikTok yogurt recipe.
  - Outcome: thriving; Lotus calls Biscoff the fastest-growing U.S. brand in cookies and spreads, in over 10% of households. [Food Dive, Aug 2026](https://www.fooddive.com/news/biscoff-manufacturing-investment-global-growth/827675/)
  - Why: cause not reported for the U.S.
- **Kerrygold (Ornua) / Ireland / long present, scaling case**
  - Positioning: premium Irish grass-fed butter and cheese.
  - Outcome: passed $1B in U.S. retail sales in 2025; the second-best-selling branded butter in the U.S.
  - Why (source): format innovation, range expansion and grass-fed credentials. [FoodNavigator, Apr 2026](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/04/17/ornua-profits-surge-as-kerrygold-hits-1bn-sales/)
- **Tony's Chocolonely / Netherlands / U.S. from about 2015-16, national by 2017**
  - Entry mode: natural and specialty retail first (national by 2017, then targeting mainstream retail), later Walmart. [ConfectioneryNews, Aug 2017](https://www.confectionerynews.com/Article/2017/08/31/Tony-s-Chocolonely-targets-US-mainstream-retail-after-going-national/), [Snack and Bakery](https://snackandbakery.com/articles/111038-tonys-chocolonely-reports-rapid-growth-expanded-walmart-retail-presence-and-new-shareholder)
  - Positioning: chocolate with a mission against exploitation in cocoa; limited-edition bars for news value.
  - Outcome: the U.S. became its largest market in the year to Sep 2025, revenue up 50%; two products were recalled that April over possible stones and metal.
  - Why: cause not reported. [Food Business MEA](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/tonys-chocolonely-grows-sales-and-profits-in-2025-despite-cocoa-price-surge-product-recalls/)
- **Remedy / Australia / 2021** - launched shelf-stable, zero-sugar kombucha in the U.S. in 2021 and went into Target stores nationwide the same year; the format answered refrigeration and sugar objections. [BevNET, 2021](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2021/australian-brand-remedy-fermented-beverages-announces-its-launch-in-target-stores-across-the-u-s/), [BevNET, 2022](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2022/australias-remedy-drinks-aims-to-disrupt-u-s-kombucha-set) Later U.S. results not reported [VERIFY].

### Patterns (found 6 successes, 4 mixed, 3 struggles or exits, 3 recent with no outcome)
- **What winners share:** one hero product with a strong sensory identity (Buldak heat, Biscoff caramel, Kerrygold butter), a clear origin story, social-video moments that create trial, and U.S. production once volume is proven (Nongshim, Lotus).
- **What separates them from failures:** winners built a repeat habit across many retailers; strugglers relied on one big customer (Oatly) or a format U.S. shoppers did not adopt at scale (Lidl's discount stores, Graze's mail-order boxes).
- **Seeding before scale:** cafes (Minor Figures), specialty retail (Tony's) and Asian grocers (Buldak) proved the occasion before mass doors. Formats that fix a shelf-life, freight or convenience problem travel well (Remedy, waterdrop).
- **What breaks:** claims and labels translated without U.S. review; home-market sweetness, portion or spice assumed to transfer; national distribution before production and working capital are ready; distributor, promotion, free-fill and spoilage deductions underestimated. A listing is not success: launch announcements rarely disclose delistings or net margin.
- **Tensions to keep in mind:** shoppers want novelty inside familiar occasions; "clean" and "natural" build trust while staying vague; scarcity builds buzz but frustrates repeat buyers and retailers.
- **Survivorship bias:** quiet delistings are rarely reported, so failures are under-counted. A sponsored Business Insider piece on Buldak's retail expansion was not used as evidence.

**So what for an entrant:** Build one hero product into a repeat purchase across several retailers before adding lines. Avoid depending on one account. Move production to the U.S. or a low-tariff origin only once sales justify it.

## AI outlook (analysis)

### Scenario 1: AI assistants and retail media decide what gets found
- **Evidence:** 68% of food retailers surveyed by FMI use AI (up from 47%), and nearly a fifth expect conversational or agentic AI to change shopping. [Grocery Dive, Jul 2026](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/grocers-artificial-intelligence-technology-spending-fmi/824777/) Kroger is launching an AI shopping assistant with Google Cloud. [Grocery Dive, Apr 2026](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/grocers-ai-technology-2026-kroger-albertsons-awg/815720/) Albertsons joined OpenAI's ChatGPT ad pilot, and its retail media unit plans to help brands reach shoppers there. [Grocery Dive, Feb 2026](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/albertsons-ai-openai-chatgpt-advertising-pilot-grocery-supermarkets/812193/) Broader data is in the US technology research.
- **Signals to watch:** paid placements in assistants, assistants favoring store brands, and which product fields they filter on.

### Scenario 2: Faster copying shortens a viral product's window
- **Evidence:** TikTok Shop trends move fast from content to products, and incumbents launch TikTok exclusives (Skittles popped candy). [Food Dive, Jun 2026](https://www.fooddive.com/news/big-food-companies-tik-tok-shop/823959/) Imitators have sold fake Buldak-style products [Wikipedia, VERIFY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buldak_Ramen), and store brands hold a record unit share. [PLMA](https://www.plma.com/article/us-private-label-industry-reached-2828-billion-sales-2025)
- **Analysis:** AI trend-spotting and formulation tools would let incumbents and retailers copy a viral flavor faster [VERIFY: no specific AI flavor-prediction tools checked].
- **Signals to watch:** time from a viral flavor to a store-brand copy; counterfeit and trademark cases.

### Scenario 3: AI forecasting decides who keeps shelf space
- **Evidence:** Hy-Vee uses Relex for forecasting and replenishment, Grocery Outlet is adding Afresh, and Walmart uses agentic AI for a unified inventory view. [Grocery Dive, Jul 2026](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/pardon-the-disruption-grocers-ai-back-end-operations/823191/)
- **Analysis:** center-store items will increasingly be reordered by algorithm, so slow new items get cut faster. Ingredient-scanning apps (such as Yuka, covered by the Washington Post in 2026 [VERIFY]) add pressure for clean, legible ingredient lists, but simplistic scores can also flatten taste into a number.
- **Signals to watch:** shorter new-item review periods; retailers or apps scoring products on ingredients.

**So what for an entrant:** Publish complete product data (allergens, origin, nutrition, dye-free status) that assistants can read. Budget for retail media at launch. Concentrate early distribution so sales per store look strong to forecasting systems.
