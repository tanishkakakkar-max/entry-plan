# Food & beverage in the United States: market-entry research

## 1. Executive summary

The U.S. offers huge grocery, convenience, foodservice, club, specialty, and e-commerce channels, but packaged food and beverage is a velocity business: a listing is not success unless the product sells through, repeats, survives margin deductions, and remains compliant.

Five conclusions:

1. **Taste and format remain the admission price.** Health, sustainability, ethics, and functional claims can improve interest, but they rarely rescue an unpleasant product.
2. **The winning promise is often a familiar job with a distinctive mechanism.** Oatly made coffee performance the wedge; Huel made complete nutrition convenient; Tony’s combined an unusually visible product with an ethical story; Buldak turned extreme spice into social play before mainstream distribution.
3. **Claims create both demand and distrust.** Systematic reviews show that nutrition, health, natural, warning, and sustainability labels affect perceptions and choices, but effects vary and can create misleading halos. [P1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30573335/) [P2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29772293/)
4. **Channel sequencing matters.** Café seeding, specialty stores, DTC subscriptions, Asian grocery, natural retail, and limited regional chains can prove a use occasion before mass retail.
5. **Importer capability is non-negotiable.** FSVP, facility registration, prior notice, preventive controls, labeling, allergens, claims, and product-specific requirements must be built into the launch.

**Provisional recommendation:** choose one repeat occasion - breakfast, desk meal, hydration, afternoon energy, alcohol alternative, snack, cooking shortcut, or cultural treat - and launch 2-5 U.S.-compliant SKUs through a channel that naturally demonstrates that occasion. Gate broader retail on weekly velocity, repeat, gross-to-net margin, and production reliability.

**So what for an entrant:** sell a habit, not an ingredient list; prove repeat purchase in one channel before chasing national doors.

## 2. Research question, scope, and evidence rules

**Question:** how can a foreign packaged food or non-alcoholic beverage company enter the U.S. with a proposition that wins trial, repeat, and retail economics while meeting import and labeling obligations?

Scope includes shelf-stable, refrigerated, frozen, snacks, confectionery, tea/coffee, functional and non-alcoholic drinks, protein/meal products, and culturally specific foods. Alcohol, infant formula, meat/poultry, produce, and dietary supplements can require additional regimes and are not fully covered.

Peer-reviewed research supports behavioral mechanisms; FDA/USDA/FTC/Census/BLS sources support rules and context; primary company and reputable trade sources verify entry events; Reddit and books provide language and interpretation. Reddit is directional and non-representative.

**Peer-review audit:** 14 of 16 counted analytical articles/case studies are peer-reviewed (87.5%). Government sources, company announcements, event-verification reporting, books, Reddit, and preprints are separately classified and excluded from that denominator.

**So what for an entrant:** separate three questions - does the promise attract, does the product repeat, and can the supply/retail system make money?

## 3. Market context

Census reported that U.S. food and beverage stores generated an estimated $982.2 billion in 2024 revenue and employed about 3.4 million people. [G1](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/2024-aies-data-release.html) This is retail-establishment revenue, not the total addressable market for any one product. BLS reported average household spending of $6,224 on food at home in 2024. [G2](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cesan.nr0.htm) USDA’s Food Expenditure Series distinguishes food at home from food away from home and supports long-run and state comparisons. [G3](https://ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-expenditure-series/documentation)

The important channel economics are:

- **natural/specialty:** stronger storytelling and discovery, but limited scale and distributor complexity;
- **mass grocery:** large volume potential, slotting/trade spend, strict service and velocity expectations;
- **club:** few SKUs, large packs, high volume, operational risk if repeat disappoints;
- **convenience:** immediate-use occasions, cold-box competition, small formats;
- **foodservice:** product demonstration and trusted-context seeding;
- **DTC:** data and subscription potential, but difficult shipping economics for heavy/low-price products;
- **Amazon/social commerce:** fast reach, price transparency, review/counterfeit exposure.

**So what for an entrant:** a $1 billion category can still be a poor entry if the product cannot achieve retailer velocity or survive distributor and promotional deductions.

## 4. Customer psychology and purchase journey

### Taste, familiarity, and convenience

Food choice combines sensory reward, price, convenience, health, familiarity, identity, and context. A clean-label review finds that “natural,” organic, and free-from cues matter, but the term clean label is not singular or uniformly interpreted. [P3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28784520/) A U.S. protein-bar experiment similarly shows that label design and clean-label cues interact with sensory acceptance. [P4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12028730/)

**Inference:** trial communication should lead with the use occasion and expected taste, then explain ingredients and benefits. A product that feels like homework is unlikely to become a snack or drink habit.

### Health claims and halos

A meta-analysis found that food labeling can change consumer behavior and industry practices, but effects are heterogeneous. [P1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30573335/) A systematic review of nutrition and risk-reduction claims found no universal positive effect; response depends on consumer and product fit. [P2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29772293/) Natural claims can raise purchase intention through brand trust and product attitude, but disappointment is damaging when expectations are contradicted. [P5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35940335/)

Research on fruit-drink claims and imagery shows that natural/health cues can distort perceptions of juice or sugar content. [P6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39894077/) The commercial implication is not “avoid claims”; it is to make the complete package truthful and comprehensible.

### Front-of-pack and warning information

Systematic reviews find that front-of-package labels can help consumers make healthier choices in some settings, while warning labels can reduce selection of sugar-sweetened beverages. [P7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33517247/) [P8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33059917/) Laboratory evidence is more abundant than long-run real-world evidence, so an entrant should test comprehension rather than assume a symbol has the same meaning for every segment.

### Sustainability and ethics

Environmental sustainability labels generally produce positive responses, but actual behavior and willingness to pay vary by format, product, knowledge, and affordability. [P9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34456340/) [P10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34444837/) Ethical sourcing can differentiate, as Tony’s case suggests, but must be operationally credible and easy to retell.

### Novelty, identity, and cultural translation

Global flavors can offer discovery and identity, while unfamiliar preparation, spice, texture, or labeling creates risk. Buldak shows how social challenges and diaspora/Asian-grocery availability can precede mainstream retail. M&S Percy Pig shows how a character and British nostalgia can turn cultural specificity into an accessible treat. These are case observations, not causal academic results.

### Community evidence

Reddit food, snack, tea, protein, and grocery communities repeatedly debate taste-versus-macros, sweeteners, digestive effects, ingredient simplicity, value per serving, and whether a viral product is actually repurchased. [R1](https://www.reddit.com/r/ProteinBars/search/?q=taste%20texture&restrict_sr=1) [R2](https://www.reddit.com/r/tea/search/?q=wellness%20tea&restrict_sr=1) These themes should become interview and product-test questions, not market-share claims.

### Books as interpretive context

Michael Moss’s *Salt Sugar Fat* examines how large food companies engineer and market repeatable sensory reward. [B1](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/209536/salt-sugar-fat-by-michael-moss/) Michael Pollan’s *The Omnivore’s Dilemma* helps explain ingredient provenance, industrial systems, and identity in American food choices. [B2](https://michaelpollan.com/books/the-omnivores-dilemma/) Books are interpretive and excluded from the peer-review ratio.

**So what for an entrant:** the strongest proposition aligns taste, occasion, ingredients, and claim; if one contradicts the others, trust and repeat weaken.

## 6. Competitor and channel map

| Arena | Benchmarks | Customer expectation | Entrant opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mass grocery | Walmart, Kroger, Albertsons, Target | Value, availability, promotions | Distinctive product with reliable supply and simple shelf message |
| Club | Costco, Sam’s Club, BJ’s | Large pack and exceptional value | Proven hero SKU with strong repeat and production capacity |
| Natural/specialty | Whole Foods, Sprouts, Erewhon, regional chains | Discovery, ingredients, mission | Emerging foreign brand with credible proof and sampling |
| Value/private label | Aldi, Trader Joe’s, retailer brands | Quality-to-price and novelty | Exclusive product or manufacturing partnership |
| E-commerce | Amazon, Thrive Market, DTC | Reviews, convenience, subscriptions | Lightweight/high-value repeat products and bundles |
| Foodservice | Independent cafés, chains, restaurants, gyms | Trusted preparation and trial | Ingredient/format that performs visibly in context |
| Global CPG | Nestlé, PepsiCo, Coca-Cola, Mondelez, Unilever, Danone | Distribution, familiarity, R&D | Speed, community, culturally specific product truth |

The competitive set includes substitute occasions: a protein bar competes with yogurt, a functional drink with coffee and water, and a premium confection with ordinary chocolate plus a charitable donation.

**So what for an entrant:** benchmark against the customer’s occasion alternative, including homemade and “buy nothing,” not only products with the same ingredient.

## 7. Market access, food safety, labeling, and claims

- **Food imports:** imported foods must meet U.S. requirements and may be inspected or detained. [G4](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-imports-exports/importing-food-products-united-states)
- **FSVP:** the U.S. owner/consignee or designated U.S. representative may be the FSVP importer and must perform risk-based supplier verification subject to the rule and exemptions. [G5](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/final-rule-foreign-supplier-verification-programs-fsvp-key-requirements)
- **Facility registration/prior notice:** determine foreign facility registration, U.S. agent, prior notice, and preventive-control obligations. **[VERIFY by product and process.]**
- **Labeling:** Nutrition Facts, ingredients, major allergens, net quantity, identity, responsible firm, and claims require U.S.-compliant presentation. **[VERIFY final artwork.]**
- **Special regimes:** acidified/low-acid canned food, juice HACCP, seafood HACCP, meat/poultry/egg, alcohol, infant food, supplements, colors/additives, and novel ingredients can trigger additional requirements. **[VERIFY.]**
- **Advertising/reviews:** health, environmental, testimonial, and comparative claims require substantiation; FTC review rules apply. [G6](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
- **Tariffs:** classification depends on ingredients, preparation, packaging, alcohol/sugar content, and origin. **[VERIFY every SKU with a customs broker.]**

**So what for an entrant:** appoint the importer and food-safety owner before booking production; compliance cannot be delegated vaguely to a distributor.

### Failure and constraint overlays

- **Oatly stockouts:** early demand exceeded supply, showing that scarcity can create attention but also limit trial and strain partners. [C1](https://www.businessofbusiness.com/articles/history-oatly-oat-milk-ipo-10-billion-swedish-alterna-starbucks-sold-out/)
- **Huel formula/logistics localization:** customer forums document questions about flavor and U.S. shipping during launch. These are community signals, not failure rates. [R3](https://discourse.huel.com/t/united-states-shipping-delayed/265?page=3)
- **Functional-claim transfer:** ingredients and claims acceptable or familiar abroad may be classified or perceived differently in the U.S. **[VERIFY before translation.]**
- **Retail listing without velocity:** public launch announcements rarely disclose delisting, chargebacks, spoilage, or net margin, so continued availability should not be confused with success.

**So what for an entrant:** prove the product in the use context, then secure capacity and trade-spend economics before adding doors.

## 9. Pattern synthesis

### What travels well

- a familiar occasion with a distinctive product experience;
- a story that can be repeated in one sentence and verified;
- formats that improve shelf life, freight, or convenience;
- community or foodservice proof before mass retail;
- a hero SKU with strong sensory recognition.

### What breaks

- translating claims and labels without U.S. review;
- assuming home-market sweetness, portion, spice, or texture will transfer unchanged;
- accepting national distribution before production and working capital are ready;
- underestimating distributor, broker, promotional, free-fill, returns, and spoilage deductions;
- health positioning that creates a halo inconsistent with the full nutrition/ingredient profile.

### Contradictions

- Consumers want novelty but rely on familiar categories and occasions.
- “Clean” and “natural” can build trust while remaining ambiguous.
- Scarcity can build buzz but frustrate repeat buyers and retail partners.
- DTC produces data, while retail often produces trial more economically for low-price/heavy items.

**So what for an entrant:** design for the second purchase and the retailer’s reorder, not only the launch photo.

## 10. AI and technology outlook

### Documented present

Retailers and brands use AI for demand forecasting, assortment, promotion, social listening, review analysis, formulation discovery, and service. Consumer-facing tools increasingly interpret labels and ingredients; this can pressure brands to make product data clearer. A 2026 Washington Post report described app-driven ingredient scrutiny influencing reformulation, but it is journalism rather than causal evidence. [A2](https://www.washingtonpost.com/food/2026/06/09/yuka-app-food-cosmetics-ingredients/)

### 2026-2028 scenarios

- **Base:** retailers improve local assortment and forecast new-product velocity; brands automate claims screening and content variants.
- **Upside:** better household-level personalization connects taste, allergy, budget, and occasion without overwhelming the shopper.
- **Downside:** simplistic ingredient scoring and generated health claims amplify misinformation; fake reviews degrade trial decisions.

### 2029-2031 scenarios

Shopping agents may compare unit price, nutrition, allergens, origin, sustainability evidence, and delivery across retailers. Brands with structured, accurate product data become easier to recommend. This is inference.

### No-regret actions

1. Maintain one governed source of truth for ingredients, allergens, nutrition, claims, certifications, origin, and lot data.
2. Preserve sensory and repeat-purchase data, not only click data.
3. Require human review for health, disease, child, pregnancy, and allergen communications.
4. Monitor generated reviews and comply with FTC rules.
5. Build traceability and recall-ready records.

**So what for an entrant:** use AI to improve forecast, compliance, and relevance; do not let it invent benefits or flatten taste into a score.

## 11. Recommendations and 90-day test plan

1. Pick one occasion, hero SKU, channel, and geography.
2. Complete FDA/FSVP/label/claims review before final packaging. **[VERIFY]**
3. Run blinded sensory testing against three substitutes, followed by branded concept testing.
4. Pilot through 5-20 foodservice/specialty accounts or one controlled regional retailer.
5. Measure weekly unit velocity, repeat, sample-to-purchase, gross-to-net margin, spoilage, and on-time/in-full delivery.
6. Interview non-repeaters at 14 and 45 days.
7. Stress-test production at 2x expected demand and model a slow-velocity downside.

**So what for an entrant:** scale only after both consumer repeat and channel economics work; either one alone is insufficient.

## 12. Uncertainties and items to verify

- Exact product category, FDA/USDA jurisdiction, FSVP importer, facility, and process obligations. **[VERIFY]**
- Ingredient status, additive/color permissions, and claim substantiation. **[VERIFY]**
- Final Nutrition Facts, allergen, net quantity, identity, and responsible-firm artwork. **[VERIFY]**
- HTS classification, current origin-specific tariffs, and agricultural restrictions. **[VERIFY]**
- Cold-chain, shelf-life, and distribution deductions by target channel. **[VERIFY]**
- U.S.-only revenue, net margin, repeat, and delisting history for case brands.

**So what for an entrant:** the decision-changing unknowns are regulatory category, sensory repeat, and gross-to-net economics.

## 13. Interviews and tests next

- 20 occasion-based customer interviews with pantry/fridge walkthroughs.
- Two rounds of 50-person blind and branded sensory tests.
- Five buyer and three distributor/broker interviews.
- Three food-safety/importer consultations using the actual process flow.
- Shelf simulation comparing package comprehension in five seconds.
- Four-week foodservice or specialty-retail pilot with weekly velocity tracking.
- Post-purchase diary on taste, digestion, preparation, and replacement behavior.

**So what for an entrant:** observe use in the actual occasion; concept liking without consumption and repeat data is weak evidence.
