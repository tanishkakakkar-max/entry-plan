# Home and living in the United States: psychology, rules, competitors, foreign entry history, AI outlook (checked October 2026)

Scope: this note is about **foreign (non-U.S.) home brands entering the U.S.**: furniture, mattresses, decor, tableware and ceramics, cookware, lighting and home textiles. U.S. brands appear only as competitors. It adds to the ceramics and home goods notes research and section 5 of the US country doc without repeating them. It merges two research passes; peer-reviewed findings come first, and community sources (Reddit, review sites) are kept as qualitative evidence of how people think. Bulky furniture and small parcel goods (a vase, a pan) behave very differently, so check which one a finding is about.

## Customer psychology

### Fact
- **Wallet context:** U.S. households put 3.3% of average spending into household furnishings and equipment in 2024. [BLS, Consumer Expenditures 2024](https://www.bls.gov/opub/reports/consumer-expenditures/2024/home.htm)
- **Moves drive furniture, and Americans are barely moving.** About 28 of every 1,000 U.S. homes (2.77%) changed hands in the first nine months of 2025, the lowest turnover in at least 30 years, 31% below 2019 and 38% below 2021. [Redfin, Oct 2025](https://www.redfin.com/news/home-turnover-report-2025/) IKEA U.S. sales fell about 4.3% to roughly $5.3B in fiscal 2025. [Retail Dive, Feb 2026](https://www.retaildive.com/news/ikea-open-10-us-stores/812435/)
- **Seeing it in your room helps.** AR "service augmentation" (placing a product in the shopper's own space and letting them manipulate it) improves decision comfort in online shopping. [Hilken et al., Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science, 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-017-0541-x) AR also raised perceived informativeness and enjoyment, lifting brand responses, but raised perceived intrusiveness. [Smink et al., Electronic Commerce Research and Applications, 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1567422319300316)
- **Green claims help only with other proof.** Health consciousness and environmental awareness both feed intention to buy green furniture. ["Consumer's intention to purchase green furniture: Do health consciousness and environmental awareness matter?" Science of the Total Environment, 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0048969719352672) (non-U.S. sample [VERIFY])
- **Returns follow regret.** Research on online returns links expectation gaps and post-purchase cognitive dissonance to return behavior. [Journal of Business Research 174, 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296323008214) In home goods, damage and "it doesn't fit the room" add operational causes.
- **Origin works through visible quality.** Country of design and of assembly can both shape perceived quality, alongside store image and brand familiarity. [Australasian Marketing Journal, 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S144135820170167X) [VERIFY authors and sample]

### Customer language (Reddit and reviews, qualitative only)
- r/furniture and home-design threads keep asking the same three things about unfamiliar online brands: is it durable, is the couch comfortable if I can't sit on it, and how do returns work? [Reddit search](https://www.reddit.com/r/furniture/search/?q=online%20sofa%20quality&restrict_sr=1)
- Review sites for direct-to-consumer furniture show praise for style and service next to complaints about delays, damage, cushion wear and return fees (for example, [Castlery on Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.castlery.com)). Directional only; review samples are self-selected.
- Background reading: Donald Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things* (usability and error prevention); Marie Kondo, *The Life-Changing Magic of Tidying Up* (home products as emotional order).

### Inference
- **The purchase is risk management.** Shoppers can't judge comfort, scale, material or color online, so dimensions, swatches, room visualization, reviews and the return policy each remove a different fear. A beautiful render with wrong dimensions converts now and returns later.
- **Logistics is part of the product.** Delivery windows, damage, room-of-choice service, assembly and spare parts shape perceived quality as much as design.
- **Low housing turnover shrinks the "new home" trigger**, so replacement, small-space and upgrade jobs matter more than full-room furnishing.
- **Likely first customers:**

| Segment | Main barrier | Trial trigger | First channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urban space optimizer (renters, small apartments) | Dimensions, stairs, assembly | Doorway planner, AR, modular demo, fast samples | Own site plus a metro pop-up |
| Design-aware upgrader | Quality and comfort doubt | Material proof, showroom, designer endorsement | Own site, design trade, showroom |
| Family and pet durability buyer | Stains, wear, safety | Abrasion and cleaning proof, spare covers | Search, own site |
| Health and low-emissions buyer | Greenwashing, price premium | Named certification, emissions data | Specialty retail, designers |
| Design professional | Samples, lead times, replacement risk | Trade portal, CAD files, swatches | Trade program, showroom |

**So what for an entrant:** Pick one product and one physical context (a city apartment, a family room) and design the delivery and ownership promise around it. Publish exact dimensions and offer swatches before you spend on ads. Don't count on movers; sell the upgrade.

## Rules and regulatory risk (2025-2026)

### Fact: tariffs and de minimis
- **Section 232 (national security) tariffs hit wooden furniture.** A September 29, 2025 proclamation set 25% on upholstered wooden furniture, kitchen cabinets and vanities. Planned increases (to 30% for upholstered furniture and 50% for cabinets and vanities on Jan 1, 2026) were postponed on Dec 31, 2025 to **January 1, 2027**, citing "productive negotiations" with several countries. [Thompson Hine, Jan 2026](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/01/president-trump-delays-section-232-tariff-increase-on-wood-furniture-cabinets-and-vanities/), [Mohawk Global, Jan 2026](https://mohawkglobal.com/word-from-the-white-house/furniture-and-cabinet-imports-section-232-tariff-increase-delayed-to-2027/) The same action covered softwood timber and lumber; country caps for the U.K., EU and Japan and how Section 232 goods interact with the July 2026 Section 301 tariffs [VERIFY with a customs broker].
- **MFN (normal) duty examples, 2026 HTS:**

| HTS line | Product | MFN duty | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6912.00.48 | Ceramic (non-porcelain) tableware and kitchenware, other | 9.8% | [US Tariff Rates](https://ustariffrates.com/hts/6912.00.48) |
| 6302.31.90 | Cotton bed linen with trim, not napped | 6.7% | +7.5% if China-origin ([HTS Hub](https://www.htshub.com/us-hs/detail/63023190)) |

  Commercial mirrors of the USITC schedule; hts.usitc.gov could not be opened from this session [VERIFY each line there, plus porcelain 6911, glassware 7013 and wooden furniture 9403].
- **Section 301 (July 24, 2026):** home goods are not generally exempt; see the Section 301 table in the US country doc for country rates and caps.
- **De minimis is gone.** The $800 exemption has been suspended for all countries since Aug 29, 2025, made indefinite by CBP rule in 2026, and ends by law on Jul 1, 2027 (details in the US apparel accessories research). [CBP](https://www.cbp.gov/newsroom/national-media-release/cbp-modernizes-low-value-shipment-processing)

### Fact: product safety and materials
- **Upholstered furniture flammability:** 16 CFR Part 1640 adopts California's TB117-2013 smolder standard nationally and requires a certification label. [CPSC, upholstered furniture FAQ](https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Upholstered-Furniture)
- **Tip-overs:** under the STURDY Act, CPSC adopted ASTM F2057-23 as the mandatory stability standard for clothing storage units (dressers, chests, wardrobes) in 2023. [TÜV SÜD, 2023](https://www.tuvsud.com/en/e-ssentials-newsletter/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/e-ssentials-4-2023/usa-cpsc-adopts-astm-f2057-23-as-the-mandatory-standard-for-sturdy-act) [VERIFY effective date]
- **Formaldehyde:** composite wood (plywood, MDF, particleboard) and finished goods made with it must meet EPA's TSCA Title VI emission limits and labeling, and importers certify compliance. [EPA, composite wood FAQ](https://epa.gov/formaldehyde/frequent-questions-consumers-about-formaldehyde-standards-composite-wood-products-act)
- **Wood origin:** many wood products need a Lacey Act plant product declaration (species and country of harvest), and APHIS keeps adding product types in phases. [Livingston](https://www.livingstonintl.com/aphis-lacey-act-requirements-20-products-begin-august/)
- **Lead in tableware:** tableware above FDA lead levels cannot be sold in the U.S.; in California, tableware above Proposition 65 levels needs a written warning. "Lead-free" means no lead; "lead-safe" means leaching below Prop 65 limits. [California Department of Public Health](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CCDPHP/DEODC/CLPPB/Pages/Q-A-Lead-in-Tableware.aspx)
- **PFAS bans are spreading across home goods:**
  - Cookware: Minnesota (2025), Colorado and Maine (Jan 2026), Connecticut (Jul 2026), New Jersey (2028); Washington requires reporting.
  - Upholstered furniture and textile furnishings: Minnesota (2025), Washington, Maine and Vermont (2026), Connecticut (Jul 2026), New Hampshire (2027).
  - Rugs and carpets: Minnesota (2025), Vermont (2026), Connecticut (2026), New Hampshire (2027), New Jersey (2028).
  - Cleaning products and fabric treatments are also covered in several states.
  - Source: [Morgan Lewis, Jan 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/01/state-regulation-of-pfas-in-consumer-products-continues-to-gain-momentum-in-2026)
- **Children's furniture** adds testing, certificates and tracking labels; CPSC eFiling of certificates at entry started Jul 8, 2026 (see the US apparel accessories research). [CPSC, furniture requirements guide](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/04.12.2016%20Guide%20to%20US%20Furniture%20Requirements.pdf)
- **Claims and reviews:** environmental, durability and origin claims need substantiation, and the FTC fake-review rule applies. [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)

### Inference
- For upholstered wood furniture, the tariff stack can now outweigh the design advantage; mattresses, decor, tableware and textiles sit outside Section 232 but face MFN and Section 301 duties.
- State PFAS laws make "stain-resistant" and "non-stick" claims a legal question, not just a marketing one.

**So what for an entrant:** Classify every SKU before pricing, and model 25% Section 232 duty on upholstered wood furniture with a possible step-up in January 2027. Put flammability, tip-over, formaldehyde, lead and PFAS tests into supplier contracts, and ship in bulk to a U.S. warehouse.

## Competitors

### Fact
- **IKEA** is investing to grow: 10 U.S. openings in 2026 (including its first city-center store in Los Angeles), more small formats, nine plan-and-order points, pickup points and a kitchen-planning shop-in-shop pilot with Best Buy, under a $2.2B omnichannel investment; it is closing its Memphis store. [Retail Dive, Feb 2026](https://www.retaildive.com/news/ikea-open-10-us-stores/812435/)
- **Wayfair** replaced its Decorify app with **Muse**, a generative AI tool that turns a style description into room images with shoppable products. [Furniture Today, Feb 2025](https://www.furnituretoday.com/e-commerce/wayfair-retools-its-consumer-ideation-experience-replacing-decorify-with-muse)

### Competitor map (inference)
| Arena | Examples | What shoppers expect | Opening for an entrant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mass and general merchandise | Walmart, Target, Costco, Amazon | Low price, fast delivery, easy returns | Better design or durability with equally clear service |
| Flat-pack global | IKEA | Accessible design, store trial, modular range | More distinctive design, better materials or white-glove delivery |
| Online breadth | Wayfair, Amazon | Huge assortment, reviews, promotions | A curated range with trustworthy specifications |
| U.S. specialty | Ashley, West Elm, Pottery Barn, Crate & Barrel, Room & Board | Showrooms, customization | A foreign design capability plus simpler choice |
| Premium | RH, Design Within Reach, the design trade | Aspiration, materials, provenance | Attainable collectible design |
| Direct-to-consumer modern | Article, Burrow, Floyd, Castlery, Sundays | Curated online buying, social design | A hero product and better ownership support |
| Off-price | HomeGoods, outlets, vintage and resale | Treasure hunt, value | Limited drops, craft, a repair or resale story |

- Small home goods face a different set: OXO, Joseph Joseph, Muji, Target private labels, Amazon sellers and specialty kitchen stores.
- **White space:** modular furniture for small urban homes, design-led pieces between IKEA and RH, verified low-emission materials, and repairable products with spare parts.

**So what for an entrant:** Benchmark more than looks: compare lead time, delivery tier, return fee, warranty, spare parts, swatches and financing. Win one arena; don't take on IKEA and Wayfair on breadth.

## Foreign entry cases since 2016

Method: non-U.S. brands whose U.S. entry, push or exit falls in 2016-2026. "Success" means active and growing in October 2026, not proven profitable. The home casebook is smaller than apparel or beauty because big furniture entries are rare.

### Successes
- **Castlery / Singapore / 2019**
  - Entry mode: online only for about six years, then a 3,000 sq ft flagship in Manhattan's Chelsea (opened May 2026) on a 10-year lease, with 17 room sets for small city homes and free styling consultations.
  - Outcome: thriving. The U.S. is its largest market, at 65% of sales; the co-founder expects the store to break even in 1.5 to 2 years.
  - Why (source): patient online growth first ("This isn't about doing it fast. It's about doing it right").
  - Sources: [Vulcan Post, Mar 2026](https://vulcanpost.com/910270/castlery-first-us-store-new-york/), [Inside Retail US](https://insideretail.us/castlery-launches-first-us-flagship-store-in-manhattans-chelsea/)
- **Hem / Sweden / 2017**
  - Entry mode: New York pop-up (fall 2017), Los Angeles pop-up (June 2018), a permanent downtown Los Angeles showroom (January 2019) and a SoHo studio (2020), plus a trade program, Hem Pro.
  - Outcome: growing at the time; revenue about $15M in 2019, more than double 2018, with New York its biggest market. The U.S. share of sales was not reported [VERIFY].
  - Why (source): team and culture, and Hem Pro "key" to its success; the target customer is the urban creative.
  - Sources: [Business of Home, Feb 2020](https://businessofhome.com/articles/this-swedish-furniture-brand-keeps-doubling-its-revenue), [Architectural Digest](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/swedish-favorite-hem-makes-its-stateside-debut)
- **Sundays / Canada / 2022**
  - Entry mode: U.S. direct-to-consumer with East and West Coast warehousing, then pop-ups in Los Angeles and New York, a first U.S. showroom in Seattle (March 2023) and Santa Monica (July 2026).
  - Outcome: the U.S. was about 30% of the business a year after launch.
  - Why: cause not reported beyond following its sales traffic into cities.
  - Sources: [Furniture Today, Mar 2023](https://www.furnituretoday.com/e-commerce/dtc-brand-sundays-spreads-its-reach-with-2-more-showrooms), [Forbes, Jun 2022](https://www.forbes.com/sites/margauxlushing/2022/06/14/sustainably-minded-dtc-furniture-startup-sundays-launches-in-the-us/), [Home News Now, Jul 2026](https://homenewsnow.com/blog/2026/07/14/sundays-opens-santa-monica-showroom/)

### Mixed
- **HAY / Denmark / 2018** - entered with a U.S. partner: Herman Miller took a stake in 2018, and HAY opened its first North American store in Portland in November 2018. [Interior Design, Nov 2018](https://interiordesign.net/designwire/hay-sets-up-shop-in-north-america/), [Business of Home](https://businessofhome.com/news/herman-miller-acquires-big-stake-in-this-scandi-brand) Still operating; U.S. results not reported.
- **Koala / Australia / U.S. launch about 2023** - sells sofas and mattresses in the U.S., Australia, Japan and the U.K.; listed on the ASX in 2026. [AAP, 2026](https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/furniture-e-seller-koala-successfully-floats-on-the-asx) The earlier pass reported a California-only launch with one hero product, going nationwide within eight months, per a company filing [VERIFY]. U.S. profit not reported.

### Struggles and exits
- **Muji USA (Ryohin Keikaku) / Japan / Chapter 11 in 2020**
  - Entry mode: own lifestyle stores (home goods, stationery, apparel), U.S. since 2007.
  - Outcome: filed for Chapter 11 in July 2020, closed stores (all Bay Area locations) and shifted to an online focus.
  - Why (source): pandemic store shutdowns. [Fortune, Jul 2020](https://fortune.com/2020/07/10/muji-us-bankruptcy-coronavirus-chapter-11), [CFO, Jul 2020](https://www.cfo.com/credit-capital/2020/07/muji-files-chapter-11-plans-online-focus/) [VERIFY current U.S. store count]
- **Made.com / U.K. / collapsed 2022 (cross-border warning, not a U.S. entry)** - long lead times, inventory commitments and freight costs that rose from £8.2M to £45.3M in a year broke the model. [The Guardian, Dec 2022](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2022/dec/04/what-broke-made-com-furniture-collapse) The lesson: long production cycles plus customer deposits plus freight exposure are fragile when delivery promises slip.

### Patterns (found 3 successes, 2 mixed, 1 struggle in the U.S., 1 non-U.S. warning)
- **What winners share:** one design language or hero product, online demand proven before leases (Castlery took six years), pop-ups before permanent space (Hem, Sundays), local warehousing, and a trade program or partner for credibility (Hem Pro, Herman Miller and HAY).
- **What separates winners from failures:** fixed costs that run ahead of demand. Muji's store base could not survive a revenue shock; Made.com's supply chain could not survive a freight shock.
- **Repeated failure patterns:** national shipping before final-mile partners are proven, long production times with optimistic dates, large assortments before the hero product works, and hidden return costs.
- **Tensions to keep in mind:** shoppers want fast delivery and customization; free returns lower risk but are ruinous for bulky goods; a showroom solves comfort but only for one city; flat-pack cuts freight but moves assembly pain to the customer.
- **Survivorship bias:** failures are under-reported. Leads not checked: Article (Canada), Zara Home, Le Creuset, Emma Sleep, Kave Home, Muuto, Ferm Living.

**So what for an entrant:** Stage everything: one hero product, one or two delivery metros, then a temporary showroom, then a lease. Keep cash and service capacity for the bad-order path (damage, a sofa that won't fit the door, a missing part), not just the expected deliveries.

## AI outlook (analysis)

### Scenario 1: AI room design becomes the shop window
- **Evidence:** Wayfair's Muse generates rooms from a description and links to products (above). AR in your own room improves decision comfort (Hilken et al.).
- **Signals to watch:** whether AI design tools pull products from multiple brands, room-scan features in major apps, and whether retailers accept third-party 3D models.

### Scenario 2: Accurate dimensions become the ranking signal
- **Analysis:** if assistants check whether a sofa fits the room and the doorway, brands with exact 3D geometry, package sizes and assembly data get recommended; vague product pages drop out.
- **Signals to watch:** retailer requirements for 3D files, AI referral traffic to furniture sites (see the US technology research, Scenario 1).

### Scenario 3: Photoreal renders widen the expectation gap
- **Analysis:** cheap AI renders make every product look perfect, which may raise returns from expectation gaps (Journal of Business Research, 2024) and draw scrutiny under the FTC review and advertising rules.
- **Signals to watch:** disclosure norms for AI-generated room images, return-reason data, enforcement actions.

**So what for an entrant:** Publish precise 3D geometry, package dimensions, weights and clearances. Separate renders from real photos, track damage and returns by carrier and SKU, and build a spare-parts catalog that customers and service agents can use.
