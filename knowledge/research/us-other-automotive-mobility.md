# Automotive and mobility in the United States: market-entry research

## 1. Executive summary

This field includes vehicles, e-bikes, micromobility, chargers, accessories and connected services. Safety certification, service coverage, infrastructure and policy exposure make it structurally different from ordinary ecommerce.

1. **The buyer purchases a mobility system, not a vehicle alone.** Range, charging, maintenance, insurance, resale and parts availability shape adoption. U.S. EV research repeatedly identifies cost, infrastructure and range as interacting barriers. [P1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38326682/) [P2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364032122006885)
2. **Trust is local and long-lived.** A foreign brand’s design or value can trigger interest, but buyers need confidence that technicians, parts, software and warranties will remain available.
3. **Use context is highly regional.** Density, climate, commuting distance, electricity, incentives and road design change the viable first market.
4. **Regulation can alter the product after launch.** NHTSA certification, battery/fire expectations and connected-vehicle restrictions require product-specific review. [G1](https://www.nhtsa.gov/importing-vehicle/importation-and-certification-faqs) [G2](https://www.bis.gov/connected-vehicles)
5. **Recent foreign entrants show both paths.** Genesis built patiently with established group infrastructure; INEOS used a narrow enthusiast wedge; VinFast and VanMoof show how service or quality failures can outrun demand generation.

**Provisional recommendation:** enter one region and use case with a service-and-parts promise financed before launch. Do not scale sales faster than repair capacity.

**So what for an entrant:** serviceability is part of product-market fit, not a downstream operations problem.

## 2. Scope and evidence rules

The report covers passenger vehicles, e-bikes, selected micromobility, charging and connected accessories. It does not provide legal, safety or investment advice. Peer-reviewed analysis is counted separately from government sources, polls, company reports, journalism, books and Reddit.

**So what for an entrant:** establish whether the product is a motor vehicle, regulated equipment, consumer product, radio device or software service before applying the channel strategy.

## 3. Market and demand structure

The U.S. has long travel distances, fragmented state rules and strong incumbent dealer/service networks. EV and e-bike value depends on where the customer can charge, store and repair the product. TCO messaging may fail when financing, insurance or resale uncertainty dominates.

Charging-station studies show infrastructure access varies across metropolitan areas. [P3](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214629622001670) Range anxiety combines objective route constraints with perceived control and familiarity. [P4](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959652620328249)

**So what for an entrant:** choose launch metros using route, housing, climate, service and incentive data - not population alone.

## 4. Customer psychology and target customers

| Segment | Job | Main fear | Proof required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tech-forward vehicle buyer | Better driving/ownership experience | New-brand reliability and resale | Real-world range, warranty, service map |
| Practical commuter | Lower trip cost and friction | Weather, theft, charging and repairs | Route demonstration and local service |
| Enthusiast | Distinctive capability/identity | Compromise or dilution | Independent testing and owner community |
| Fleet/professional buyer | Predictable utilization and TCO | Downtime and residual value | SLA, parts, telematics and financing |
| Safety-first family | Reliable transport | Crash, battery and software risk | Certification, transparent incidents, support |

Systematic EV-adoption evidence finds cost, charging, range, social influence and environmental attitude interact. [P2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364032122006885) Texas research demonstrates that barriers vary even inside one country. [P1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38326682/) U.S. e-bike studies similarly find health, convenience and environmental motivation alongside safety, theft and infrastructure barriers. [P5](https://doi.org/10.1080/19463138.2019.1672696) [P6](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/9/9/1662)

Reddit searches in [r/electricvehicles](https://www.reddit.com/r/electricvehicles/search/?q=service%20experience&restrict_sr=1), [r/ebikes](https://www.reddit.com/r/ebikes/search/?q=repair&restrict_sr=1) and [r/whatcarshouldIbuy](https://www.reddit.com/r/whatcarshouldIbuy/search/?q=new%20brand&restrict_sr=1) surface questions about real range, charging, dealer markups, parts, insurance and resale. Treat them as issue discovery, not representative measurement.

**So what for an entrant:** buyers need proof that an unfamiliar brand will still work for them in year five.

## 7. Market access, regulation and operations

- Imported vehicles and regulated equipment must meet NHTSA certification and import requirements. [G1](https://www.nhtsa.gov/importing-vehicle/importation-and-certification-faqs) [G3](https://www.nhtsa.gov/importing-vehicle/importation-and-certification-faqs-0)
- BIS connected-vehicle rules can restrict certain hardware/software links by country and model year. [G2](https://www.bis.gov/connected-vehicles)
- E-bikes and lithium systems can face CPSC, UL-standard, transport, fire-code and local-use requirements. **[VERIFY product and jurisdiction.]**
- Dealer, direct-sales, registration, insurance and incentive rules differ by state. **[VERIFY launch states.]**
- FCC, privacy, cybersecurity, repair and software-update obligations may apply. **[VERIFY functionality.]**
- HTS classification, country of origin, normal duty, Section 301/232 and any quota exposure require broker and counsel review. **[VERIFY every SKU.]**

**So what for an entrant:** regulatory architecture and service footprint must be completed together before reservations or deposits.

## 9. Success and failure patterns

Success patterns: one unmistakable capability; local homologation; staged metro launch; funded parts and technicians; independent real-world testing; transparent warranty; and ownership/community programs.

Failure risks: announcing national availability before service; proprietary components with no fallback; treating a factory plan as customer proof; underestimating state sales rules; price cuts that impair resale; and ignoring policy exposure in connected systems.

James Womack, Daniel Jones and Daniel Roos’s *The Machine That Changed the World* remains useful for thinking about quality systems and production learning. It is a book, not counted evidence.

**So what for an entrant:** growth should be throttled by service capacity and field quality, not factory ambition.

## 10. AI and future outlook

### 2026-2028

- **Base:** AI improves route/charge planning, driver support, maintenance prediction and service triage.
- **Upside:** local models reduce downtime and adapt interfaces to real use.
- **Downside:** unsafe automation claims, opaque monitoring, cyber risk and regulatory restrictions fragment products by market.

### 2029-2036

Mobility value may shift toward software-defined fleets, predictive maintenance and integrated energy. Durable entrants will retain repair access, safe human override and auditable updates. This is a scenario.

No-regret actions: software bills of materials, incident logs, long-term update policy, independent security tests and manual fallback.

**So what for an entrant:** AI should make ownership more predictable, not make the vehicle impossible to repair or understand.

## 11. Recommendation and 90-day test

1. Pick one metro/use case and complete regulatory classification.
2. Map chargers, routes, storage, climate, technicians and parts lead times.
3. Run 20 buyer interviews and 15 supervised multi-week pilots.
4. Publish realistic range/runtime and maintenance assumptions.
5. Contract local service before sales; define repair and replacement SLAs.
6. Model landed margin, warranty, financing and residual-value exposure.
7. Expand only when field failure and repair-cycle metrics are stable.

**So what for an entrant:** a constrained local launch is a quality system, not a lack of ambition.

- Questions: What almost stopped the purchase? Where would you repair it? What downtime is acceptable? How did insurance or charging change the economics? What would make resale impossible?
