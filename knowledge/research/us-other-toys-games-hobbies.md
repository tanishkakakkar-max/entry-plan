# Toys, games and hobbies in the United States: market-entry research

## 1. Executive summary

Toys, tabletop games, collectibles, hobby kits and screen-free audio share a discovery economy but have different users, safety duties and repeat cycles. The Toy Association reports a $45.6 billion 2025 total U.S. toy market estimate and $30.3 billion in tracked retail sales; scope and methodology matter when sizing a niche. [G1](https://www.toyassociation.org/ta/toys/research-and-data/data/us-sales-data.aspx)

1. **Many purchases have two customers.** Children seek fun, identity and social currency; adults control safety, price, clutter and values. Parent-child co-shopping research shows active negotiation and mediation rather than a simple “pester power” model. [P1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9925357/)
2. **Adult fandom is a separate segment, not an extension of children’s play.** Nostalgia, completion, display and community can support premium collectibles, but speculative scarcity can damage trust. [P2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35992386/) [P3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36046369/)
3. **Age fit and repeat engagement beat generic educational claims.** Caregivers select for safety, developmental fit and child interest; overstated learning outcomes create skepticism. [P4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33472096/) [P5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30509931/)
4. **Foreign concepts can scale when culturally legible.** Tonies and Yoto translated screen-free audio into a repeat content system; Miniso and Pop Mart used physical discovery and collectible cadence.
5. **The key failure risks are channel and lifecycle mismatch.** Flying Tiger’s U.S. closure shows that a successful foreign retail concept may not support U.S. leases, density and shopping behavior.

**Provisional recommendation:** enter through one community and one play loop, pair independent safety evidence with demonstrable fun, and prove repeat without manipulative scarcity.

**So what for an entrant:** decide whether the primary engine is play, learning, collecting or making; each needs different evidence and channels.

## 2. Scope and evidence rules

This report covers physical and connected toys, tabletop games, collectibles, screen-free audio and hobby products. Video games are included only where physical/digital play overlaps. Peer-reviewed analytical sources are counted separately from regulation, market data, company reports, books and Reddit.

**So what for an entrant:** never transfer evidence from adult collectors to children, or from educational outcomes to entertainment demand, without a direct test.

## 3. Market and demand structure

The market is event-driven: birthdays and holidays create gifting peaks; fandom releases and blind boxes create drop cycles; board games and hobbies depend on social participation; audio systems depend on content replenishment. Returns, missing pieces, counterfeits and age misclassification can erase margin.

**So what for an entrant:** model a calendar and replenishment loop, not just average annual demand.

## 4. Customer psychology and target customers

| Segment | Job | Friction | Purchase proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent of young child | Safe, absorbing, age-fit play | Clutter, small parts, weak engagement | Demonstration, age rationale, easy storage |
| Education-seeking caregiver | Support a skill | Overstated claims | Specific activity and bounded outcome evidence |
| Gift buyer | Delight without getting it wrong | Duplication and unfamiliarity | Recognizable age/use cue and easy return |
| Adult collector | Identity, nostalgia, completion | Artificial scarcity and resale risk | Authenticity, design quality, community |
| Social hobbyist | Shared ritual and mastery | Learning curve and group coordination | Tutorial, local events, replayability |

Caregiver toy choice balances child preference with safety and developmental appropriateness. [P4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33472096/) Guidance in the digital era emphasizes active, engaging and socially interactive play rather than assuming “educational” labels are beneficial. [P5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30509931/) Research on blind boxes links uncertain rewards with impulse and risk mechanisms, especially important when children are exposed. [P2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35992386/) [P3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36046369/) Research on adult blind-box buying (sample country **[VERIFY]**) supports treating adult collectors as a distinct customer. [P6](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0969698924004235)

Reddit searches in [r/boardgames](https://www.reddit.com/r/boardgames/search/?q=buying%20advice&restrict_sr=1), [r/ActionFigures](https://www.reddit.com/r/ActionFigures/search/?q=quality&restrict_sr=1), [r/Labubu](https://www.reddit.com/r/Labubu/search/?q=authentic&restrict_sr=1) and [r/toys](https://www.reddit.com/r/toys/search/?q=gift&restrict_sr=1) reveal language around replayability, component quality, authenticity, scalping, missing parts and gifts. These are qualitative discovery sources only.

**So what for an entrant:** marketing must pass both tests - “the user wants it” and “the gatekeeper feels good approving it.”

## 7. Market access, regulation and operations

- CPSC rules can require age grading, small-parts warnings, tracking labels, third-party testing and a Children’s Product Certificate. [G2](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Toy-Safety-Business-Guidance--Small-Entity-Compliance-Guide) [G3](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Testing-Certification/Childrens-Product-Certificate)
- Connected products directed to children can trigger COPPA and state privacy rules. [G4](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/childrens-privacy)
- Educational, health and environmental claims require substantiation; licensed IP requires territorial rights. **[VERIFY each claim and license.]**
- Batteries, magnets, project chemicals and food-contact components can create additional requirements. **[VERIFY by design.]**
- HTS classification, origin and trade-action duties require broker review. **[VERIFY every SKU.]**
- Build U.S. processes for replacement parts, missing components, counterfeit reporting and recalls.

**So what for an entrant:** age grade and intended use are product architecture decisions, not label copy added at the end.

## 9. Success and failure patterns

Success patterns: an immediately legible play loop; clear age fit; parent-safe autonomy; content or community replenishment; retail demonstration; cultural localization; and reliable component replacement.

Failure risks: entering with a broad novelty assortment; manipulating children through uncertain rewards; overstating educational benefits; licensing without renewal economics; building hardware before a content pipeline; and opening stores before proven local density.

David Elkind’s *The Power of Play* and Susan Linn’s *Consuming Kids* provide useful interpretive lenses on child-led play and commercial pressure. They are not counted evidence.

**So what for an entrant:** optimize for repeat engagement and parent recommendation, not only unboxing excitement.

## 10. AI and future outlook

### 2026-2028

- **Base:** AI helps recommendation, translation, rules explanation and customer support.
- **Upside:** adaptive stories and hobby tutoring increase replayability while keeping parents in control.
- **Downside:** conversational toys collect sensitive data, produce unsafe content or replace social play without transparent limits.

### 2029-2036

Physical play may become more adaptive, with persistent characters and user-generated content. Advantage will depend on safe child design, parental consent, strong IP and offline value. This is a scenario, not a prediction.

No-regret actions: minimize data, disable open-ended generation by default for young users, provide parent dashboards and deletion, red-team content, and keep a complete offline play mode.

**So what for an entrant:** AI should deepen play without turning the child into the product.

## 11. Recommendation and 90-day test

1. Define one primary user, payer and play loop.
2. Confirm age grade, testing, privacy and import requirements.
3. Run 20 observed play sessions and 30 four-week home trials.
4. Measure independent setup, repeat sessions, caregiver intervention, breakage and requests for more content.
5. Test one specialty/community channel and one scalable channel.
6. Calculate margin after royalties, returns, missing pieces and support.
7. Scale drops or content only after repeat is demonstrated without artificial urgency.

**So what for an entrant:** the strongest signal is voluntary repeat play after novelty wears off.

## 13. Interviews and validation

- 15 parent-child pairs, 10 adult collectors and five hobby-store operators matched to the wedge.
- Include returners and buyers who refused the product.
- Questions: Who requested it? Who vetoed it? What happened after week one? Which part broke or confused? Would you buy content, expansion or a second item at full price?

**So what for an entrant:** observe play; self-reported enthusiasm systematically misses setup and abandonment friction.
