# Restaurants in the United States: market-entry research

## 1. Executive summary

The U.S. restaurant market can make a foreign concept globally visible, but it combines local real estate, labor, food safety, delivery platforms, tipping norms, franchise law, and intense price competition. A concept that succeeds at home may fail because the operating model - not the cuisine - is poorly translated.

Five conclusions:

1. **Familiarity plus difference is the strongest entry formula.** Tim Ho Wan offered recognizable dim sum plus Michelin-linked value; Marugame paired unfamiliar production theater with a familiar cafeteria line; Jollibee uses both Filipino identity and familiar fast-food formats.
2. **Value is total experience, not menu price.** Portion, speed, hospitality, convenience, cleanliness, fees, and reliability shape value. Reviews often discuss service more intensely than food. [P1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950329320303293)
3. **Authenticity is powerful and unstable.** Research on restaurant reviews shows customers construct authenticity through food, people, place, and self-experience, while work on immigrant cuisines warns that “authentic” and “exotic” framing can reproduce status bias. [P2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278431921002450)
4. **Overexpansion kills otherwise interesting concepts.** Ikinari Steak expanded to 11 New York units within roughly two years, then closed or converted nine; Leon planned 15 U.S. stores by 2020 but exited after four. Both cases argue for one-city unit economics before regional rollout.
5. **Technology should remove friction without removing hospitality.** Kiosks, apps, loyalty, robotics, and AI can improve throughput, but current reporting and research indicate that customers still use human warmth and service recovery as quality signals.

**Provisional recommendation:** enter with one flagship or high-control pilot in a neighborhood where the target customer already has a reason to care; simplify the menu for U.S. throughput and supply; preserve one or two signature experiences; and do not franchise until company-operated unit economics, training, and food safety are repeatable.

**So what for an entrant:** prove one restaurant and one operating playbook before selling a national story.

## 2. Research question, scope, and evidence rules

**Question:** how can a foreign restaurant, café, bakery, dessert, or beverage-service concept enter the U.S. with a customer proposition and operating model that produce repeat visits and scalable unit economics?

The report covers company-owned, joint-venture, licensed, and franchised entry; quick service, fast casual, casual dining, café, and experience-led concepts. It does not provide state-specific liquor, immigration, employment, tax, or food-establishment legal advice.

Peer-reviewed research supports consumer and operating mechanisms; government sources support rules; filings/company and reputable restaurant reporting verify events; Reddit provides language; books provide interpretation. Reddit is non-representative. Expansion by a foreign brand already present before 2016 is labeled “scale,” not “first entry.”

**Peer-review audit:** 14 of 16 counted analytical articles/case studies are peer-reviewed (87.5%). Government, filings, company releases, event-verification journalism, books, and Reddit are separately classified and excluded from that denominator.

**So what for an entrant:** distinguish cuisine demand from concept economics; a popular food can still sit inside a weak labor, real-estate, or throughput model.

## 3. Market context

U.S. households spent an average of $3,945 on food away from home in 2024, including restaurant, delivery, and takeout meals. [G1](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cesan.nr0.htm) USDA tracks food-away-from-home spending across restaurants, fast food, schools, and other outlets. [G2](https://ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-expenditure-series/documentation) National totals are context, not a site forecast.

Restaurant demand is hyperlocal. A concept’s opportunity depends on:

- trade area and daypart;
- office, residential, tourism, and student traffic;
- cuisine familiarity and diaspora density;
- average check, portion, and tipping/service model;
- rent, labor, utilities, occupancy, and delivery commissions;
- liquor eligibility and local licensing;
- dine-in, pickup, catering, drive-through, and delivery mix;
- unit throughput and kitchen constraints.

**So what for an entrant:** the useful market unit is a site-daypart-customer model, not U.S. restaurant revenue.

## 4. Customer psychology and purchase journey

### Food, service, atmosphere, and value

Analysis of 175,879 Yelp reviews found that diners used more sentiment words for service than food, illustrating how human interaction shapes overall ratings. [P1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950329320303293) Restaurant quality is inferred from food, waiting time, service, cleanliness, atmosphere, and value; these signals interact rather than substitute perfectly.

### Reviews and social proof

Research finds that consumer ratings, review volume, and comments on food, environment, and service influence restaurant online popularity. [P3](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278431910000198) Review credibility, trust, and image richness affect purchase intention. [P4](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1447677021000383) Competition can also increase incentives for review manipulation. [P5](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278431923001792)

The FTC review rule prohibits specified fake reviews, sentiment-conditioned incentives, review suppression, and undisclosed insider practices. [G3](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)

### Authenticity and cultural translation

Peer-reviewed research shows that diners judge authenticity through multiple dimensions rather than recipe fidelity alone. [P2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278431921002450) For foreign entrants, “authentic” can mean ingredients, preparation, staff knowledge, atmosphere, price, or emotional connection. It can also create unfair expectations that non-European cuisines should be cheap; peer-reviewed work using U.S. Yelp data documents racialized framing of immigrant cuisines. [P6](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.284/)

**Inference:** local adaptation should be explicit and principled. Hiding adaptation risks betrayal; refusing all adaptation can produce poor throughput, sourcing, or accessibility.

### Health information and menu labeling

Systematic reviews and meta-analyses find modest and context-dependent effects of calorie/menu labeling on calories selected or purchased. [P7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28973989/) [P8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27358442/) [P9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29045080/) Menu labeling can also influence supply-side reformulation and reputation, but implementation has operational barriers. [P10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32295647/)

### Convenience, delivery, and transparency

Customers weigh speed and ease against fees, food quality after travel, packaging, and trust in the storefront. Virtual brands can create suspicion when many listings share one kitchen; Reddit-led reporting documents mistrust of opaque storefronts. [A1](https://www.businessinsider.com/virtual-brands-doordash-san-francisco-pizzeria-1338-polk-food-delivery-2024-1) This is not evidence that every virtual brand is disliked, but it supports transparent identity.

### Community evidence

Restaurant and city subreddits repeatedly discuss “authenticity,” hidden fees, tipping, portion size, wait time, reservation difficulty, service decline, and whether a new chain is worth the hype. [R1](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskNYC/search/?q=new%20restaurant%20worth%20it&restrict_sr=1) [R2](https://www.reddit.com/r/fastfood/search/?q=price%20quality&restrict_sr=1) These themes are interview prompts, not prevalence estimates.

### Books as interpretive context

Danny Meyer’s *Setting the Table* emphasizes hospitality and service recovery as strategic systems. [B1](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/setting-the-table-danny-meyer) David Chang’s *Eat a Peach* provides founder-level context on restaurant culture, scale, and the costs of ambition. [B2](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/598849/eat-a-peach-by-david-chang-with-gabe-ulla/) Books are interpretive and excluded from the peer-review ratio.

**So what for an entrant:** design the entire visit - search, queue, order, wait, eat, pay, recover from error, and decide to return - rather than treating food as the only product.

## 5. First-customer hypotheses

| Segment / job | Motivation | Barrier | Trial trigger | Site/channel | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Diaspora anchor customer | Taste, identity, community | Inauthenticity or diluted menu | Founder/chef credibility and signature dishes | Diaspora-adjacent neighborhood, community events | High on mechanism |
| Cuisine-curious urban diner | Novel experience and social discovery | Unfamiliar menu and value uncertainty | Simple ordering, visual signature, review proof | Dense mixed-use neighborhood | High |
| Value-led lunch customer | Fast, filling, reliable meal | Queue, price, inconsistency | Clear combo/value and visible throughput | Office/student corridor | High |
| Experience seeker | Theater, design, collectability | Gimmick without food quality | One memorable mechanism backed by good food | Destination retail, entertainment district | Medium-high |

**So what for an entrant:** choose one anchor segment and one secondary discovery segment; trying to serve every daypart and audience makes the pilot unreadable.

## 6. Competitor and format map

| Arena | Benchmarks | Customer expectation | Entrant opening |
|---|---|---|---|

| Fast casual | Chipotle, Cava, Sweetgreen, Panda Express | Customization, perceived freshness, digital convenience | Foreign preparation or flavor with simple throughput |

| Asian/global specialists | Jollibee, Marugame, Din Tai Fung, Wagamama, Kura, Nando’s | Authenticity plus chain consistency | Underserved cuisine/format with scalable kitchen design |
| Delivery/virtual | DoorDash/Uber Eats ecosystems, ghost kitchens | Convenience and ratings | Travel-designed menu and transparent identity |

**So what for an entrant:** compare the concept to the customer’s true alternative at that time and place, including grocery, leftovers, and staying home.

## 7. Market access, food safety, labor, and franchise structure

- **Food safety:** the FDA Food Code is a model adopted and adapted by state/local jurisdictions; local health departments license and inspect establishments. [G4](https://www.fda.gov/food/fda-food-code/food-code-2022)
- **Local licenses:** required permits vary by activity and location; building, zoning, fire, health, signage, liquor, music, and outdoor dining can all matter. [G5](https://www.sba.gov/document/report--licenses-permits-approvals)
- **Menu labeling:** chains with 20 or more locations doing business under the same name and offering substantially the same menu items generally fall under federal calorie-posting requirements. [G6](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/menu-labeling-requirements)
- **Franchising:** the FTC Franchise Rule requires a disclosure document with 23 specified items for covered offerings; state registration/disclosure rules may add requirements. [G7](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/franchise-rule)
- **Employment:** wage, overtime, scheduling, tip, paid-leave, safety, immigration verification, and predictive-scheduling obligations vary by federal/state/local law. **[VERIFY by city and operating model.]**
- **Imported ingredients/equipment:** food imports, equipment certification, customs classification, and agricultural restrictions need product-level review. **[VERIFY.]**
- **Tax/entity:** foreign ownership, U.S. entity, transfer pricing, withholding, sales tax, and franchise/joint-venture structure require professional advice. **[VERIFY.]**

**So what for an entrant:** select the city and operating structure before signing a site; legal, labor, liquor, and construction realities can change the model.

### Deep failure lessons

**Ikinari Steak:** the concept brought stand-up, quick-service steak to Manhattan and expanded rapidly. Within roughly two years, nine of eleven locations were slated for closure or conversion. [C5](https://www.nrn.com/casual-dining/ikinari-steak-to-close-or-convert-9-of-11-nyc-units) The documented facts support overexpansion and format/value mismatch as key hypotheses; a full causal claim requires internal unit data.

**Leon:** the health-oriented British fast-food chain opened in D.C. in 2018, planned 15 U.S. sites, reached four, and exited in 2021 after pandemic pressure; Restaurant Dive reported the Fairfax unit achieved only 25% of expected revenue. [C10](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/british-qsr-leon-shutters-us-locations/596859/) Pandemic shock mattered, but underperforming unit economics made the system less resilient.

**Wagamama:** its long U.S. history includes openings, closures, and later changes in operating control. [C14](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/emerging-brands/wagamama-takes-control-its-us-operations) The lesson is that a famous global brand can still require governance and operating-model reset.

**So what for an entrant:** cap the pilot, define stop/go thresholds before opening, and do not let opening-week queues substitute for six-month repeat and labor economics.

## 9. Pattern synthesis

### What travels well

- a signature item customers can name and photograph;
- a familiar format with one distinctive production or service element;
- diaspora credibility plus broader accessibility;
- visible value and fast menu comprehension;
- company-operated proof before franchise sales;
- city clusters that simplify supply, management, and marketing.

### What breaks

- importing the home-market menu intact;
- oversized menus and unique ingredients that complicate U.S. supply;
- expanding from opening buzz rather than stable cohorts;
- underestimating U.S. labor, rent, tipping, delivery, and construction;
- treating technology as a substitute for hospitality;
- opaque virtual-brand identity or manipulated reviews.

### Contradictions

- Diners seek authenticity but reward convenience and adaptation.
- A queue creates social proof but also abandonment and poor first experience.
- Digital ordering improves throughput but can reduce welcome and upselling.
- Franchising lowers corporate capital per unit but increases control and disclosure burdens.

**So what for an entrant:** standardize the invisible operating system while preserving the visible signature experience.

## 10. AI and technology outlook

### Documented present

Restaurants use kiosks, apps, digital loyalty, demand forecasting, scheduling, computer vision, robotics, voice ordering, and review analytics. Online reviews materially influence choice, but manipulation risk is documented. [P5](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278431923001792) The FTC has acted against AI-enabled false-review practices. [G3](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)

### 2026-2028 scenarios

- **Base:** AI improves labor forecasting, prep planning, menu testing, translation, and service recovery.
- **Upside:** better demand and waste prediction materially improve unit margin; multilingual training improves consistency.
- **Downside:** automated ordering mistakes, inaccessible interfaces, dynamic-price backlash, and synthetic reviews damage trust.

### 2029-2031 scenarios

Dining agents may optimize party needs, allergies, wait, travel time, total bill, and review credibility. Restaurants with accurate menus, allergen data, availability, reservation, and provenance information become easier to recommend. This is inference.

### No-regret actions

1. Maintain structured menu, ingredient, allergen, nutrition, price, availability, and location data.
2. Keep human override and visible help at automated ordering points.
3. Track wait, remake, refund, review, and repeat by order channel.
4. Prohibit generated customer reviews and monitor franchisee practices.
5. Use AI forecasts as decisions with accountability, not unquestioned schedules.

**So what for an entrant:** automate variability and administration; protect hospitality, food safety, and accountability.

## 11. Recommendations and 90-day pilot plan

1. Choose one city, trade area, target segment, and anchor daypart.
2. Reduce the menu to signature items plus operationally compatible choices.
3. Run a 2-4 week residency, food-hall test, or delivery/pickup kitchen only if it faithfully tests the intended experience.
4. Time every station; test food after realistic delivery and holding.
5. Build a bottom-up unit model with rent, labor, benefits, card/delivery fees, waste, repairs, local taxes, and management.
6. Set stop/go gates for sales by daypart, repeat, labor minutes, ticket time, review themes, food cost, and four-wall contribution.
7. Delay franchise offering until training, supply, quality assurance, and company-operated economics are repeatable.

**So what for an entrant:** the pilot should test operations under pressure, not only customer enthusiasm at a curated event.

## 13. Interviews and tests next

- 20 target-diner meal-occasion interviews.
- Five diaspora/community leader tastings where relevant.
- Ten blind and branded menu tests.
- Five restaurant-operator interviews on labor and real estate.
- Three food-safety/licensing consultations in the proposed city.
- A live pilot measuring queue, ticket time, abandonment, remakes, waste, and repeat.
- A delivery test at 15, 30, and 45 minutes.
- Two franchising/legal structure sessions only after the company-operated model is viable.

**So what for an entrant:** combine customer evidence with stopwatch-and-P&L evidence; neither is sufficient alone.
