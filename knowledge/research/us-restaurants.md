# Restaurants in the United States: diner psychology, rules and costs, competitors, foreign entry history, AI outlook (checked October 2026)

Scope: this note is about **foreign restaurant brands entering the U.S.** (quick-service, fast casual, cafe, bakery-cafe, bubble tea and casual dining). U.S. chains appear only as competitors. Packaged food is in the separate food and beverage note. Not repeated here: CAVA and Sweetgreen (`research/cava-brand-teardown.md`), Pepper Lunch and cross-border lessons (`research/foreign-fast-casual-abroad-lessons.md`), delivery economics (`research/food-delivery-platforms.md`) and tariff rates (US country doc, section 5). This note merges two research passes; peer-reviewed findings come first, and community sources (Reddit, articles) are kept as qualitative evidence of how people think.

## Customer psychology

### Fact
- **Value is the first filter.** In the National Restaurant Association's 2025 survey, a daily special, discount or value promotion matters when choosing a restaurant for 82% of delivery customers, 75% of quick-service, snack or coffee customers and 62% of sit-down customers. Limited-service customers split 53% price-first, 47% experience-first. [NRA State of the Industry 2025](https://go.restaurant.org/rs/078-ZLA-461/images/SOI-2025-Report.pdf?version=0)
- Prices keep rising: USDA ERS forecasts food-away-from-home prices up 3.7% in 2026, above the 20-year average of 3.5%. [Grocery Dive citing USDA ERS, Feb 2026](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/food-at-home-prices-increase-2026-usda/813359/)
- **The market is splitting by income.** McDonald's CEO said lower-income quick-service traffic fell by nearly double digits while higher-income traffic rose by a similar amount; Extra Value Meals (about 15% off) are about 30% of its U.S. transactions. "The number one driver of consumers' value perception is actually what's going on the menu board." [Restaurant Dive, Nov 2025](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/mcdonalds-extra-value-meals-insulated-sales-from-diner-price-sensitivity/804743/) 54% of households under $75K say better prices would increase visits (Circana). [L.E.K., 2026](https://www.lek.com/sites/default/files/2026-05/end-price-led-growth-2026-restaurant-playbook.pdf)
- Haidilao's average spend per guest in North America fell from $43.5 to $39.1 (Q3 2024 to Q3 2025). [Super Hi Q3 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001995306/000110465925116156/tm2532161d1_ex99-1.htm)
- **Tipping fatigue is real but setting-specific.** 72% of U.S. adults say tipping is expected in more places than five years ago. 92% always or often tip at sit-down restaurants, 25% for coffee and 12% at fast casual. 40% oppose suggested tip amounts (24% favor), and 72% oppose automatic service charges. [Pew, Nov 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/2023/11/09/tipping-culture-in-america-public-sees-a-changed-landscape/)
- **Off-premises dominates.** Nearly 75% of restaurant traffic is off-premises, and nearly 6 in 10 Gen Z and millennial diners order takeout or drive-thru weekly. [NRA, May 2025](https://restaurant.org/education-and-resources/resource-library/value-is-a-big-piece-of-off-premises-dinings-popularity-play/) 67% of Gen Z adults call takeout essential. [NRA SOI 2025](https://go.restaurant.org/rs/078-ZLA-461/images/SOI-2025-Report.pdf?version=0)
- **Loyalty is a choice factor**, especially off-premises: important to 61% of delivery, 54% of quick-service and 41% of full-service customers. [NRA SOI 2025](https://go.restaurant.org/rs/078-ZLA-461/images/SOI-2025-Report.pdf?version=0) Starbucks had 33.9M 90-day active U.S. Rewards members spending 58% of tender dollars; mobile orders were 31% of transactions. [Starbucks Q3 FY25 dashboard](https://s203.q4cdn.com/326826266/files/doc_financials/2025/q3/Q3-FY25-Digital-IR-Dashboard.pdf)
- **Discovery:** in a Toast survey of about 1,450 U.S. diners (Oct 2025), word of mouth (38%) and walking or driving past (30%) lead, then Facebook (27%), map apps (18%), search (16%), Instagram (15%) and TikTok (14%). For Gen Z, TikTok is the top channel at 38%. [Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/data/how-guests-discover-new-restaurants)
- **Asian concepts are where growth is.** Korean, Vietnamese and Filipino foods were among the most popular items in 2025. [NRA SOI 2025](https://go.restaurant.org/rs/078-ZLA-461/images/SOI-2025-Report.pdf?version=0) Two of the five fastest-growing Top 500 chains by units were Asian (KPOT Korean BBQ & Hot Pot +73%, Kyuramen +62.5%); newer ramen, hot pot and bowl brands outperformed P.F. Chang's and Benihana in 2024. [NRN citing Technomic, Apr 2025](https://www.nrn.com/top-500-restaurants/3-growing-foodservice-categories-to-watch)

**Peer-reviewed** (DOIs checked against publisher or university repository records):

Anderson and Magruder (2012), "Learning from the Crowd: Regression Discontinuity Estimates of the Effects of an Online Review Database," *The Economic Journal*. [Paper](https://are.berkeley.edu/~mlanderson/pdf/Anderson%20and%20Magruder.pdf), [Berkeley](https://news.berkeley.edu/2012/09/04/yelp-reviews-boost-restaurant-business/) [VERIFY DOI 10.1111/j.1468-0297.2012.02512.x]
- Perceived authenticity was a critical determinant of brand equity (awareness, association, quality, loyalty), which drove brand choice for ethnic restaurants. Lu, Gursoy and Lu (2015), "Authenticity perceptions, brand equity and brand choice intention: The case of ethnic restaurants," *International Journal of Hospitality Management* 50. [doi:10.1016/j.ijhm.2015.07.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhm.2015.07.008)
- Diners judge authenticity before visiting from the setting, staff and other customers; their own ethnicity changes how cues are read; authenticity, cosmopolitanism and familiarity drive patronage. Wang and Mattila (2015), "The Impact of Servicescape Cues on Consumer Prepurchase Authenticity Assessment and Patronage Intentions to Ethnic Restaurants," *Journal of Hospitality and Tourism Research* 39(3). [doi:10.1177/1096348013491600](https://doi.org/10.1177/1096348013491600)
- In five experiments, restaurants matching diners' stereotypes got higher purchase and recommendation intent via perceived authenticity, more so for diners with low preference for ethnic food and greater cultural distance. Zhang et al. (2026), "Serving up stereotypes?", *International Journal of Hospitality Management* 133. [doi:10.1016/j.ijhm.2025.104415](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhm.2025.104415)
- In interviews, Millennial and Gen Z Instagram users described photographing food as a routine part of dining (memory, identity, belonging), with pressure to document as a downside. Dyce, Marcella-Hood and Pirie (2024), "Pic first, eat later," *Hospitality and Society* 14(3). [doi:10.1386/hosp_00086_1](https://doi.org/10.1386/hosp_00086_1)
- Ratings, review volume and comments on food, environment and service drive a restaurant's online popularity. Zhang, Ye, Law and Li (2010), "The impact of e-word-of-mouth on the online popularity of restaurants," *International Journal of Hospitality Management* 29(4). [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278431910000198)
- U.S. Yelp reviews frame immigrant cuisines with more "othering" and lower-status language, which feeds an expectation that non-European food should be cheap. Luo, Gligorić and Jurafsky (2024), "Othering and Low Status Framing of Immigrant Cuisines in US Restaurant Reviews and Large Language Models," *ICWSM*. [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/31367)
- Calorie labeling on menus has modest, context-dependent effects on what diners order. [Long et al., American Journal of Public Health, 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25790388/), [meta-analysis of U.S. adults, 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5691705/)

### Customer language (Reddit and articles, qualitative only)
- City and restaurant subreddits keep returning to "authenticity," hidden fees, tipping, portion size, waits, hard-to-get reservations, slipping service, and whether a new chain is worth the hype. [r/AskNYC search](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskNYC/search/?q=new%20restaurant%20worth%20it&restrict_sr=1), [r/fastfood search](https://www.reddit.com/r/fastfood/search/?q=price%20quality&restrict_sr=1) Interview prompts, not prevalence data.
- Delivery shoppers distrust opaque "virtual brands" when many listings turn out to share one kitchen. [Business Insider, Jan 2024](https://www.businessinsider.com/virtual-brands-doordash-san-francisco-pizzeria-1338-polk-food-delivery-2024-1)
- Background reading: Danny Meyer, *Setting the Table* (hospitality and service recovery as systems); David Chang, *Eat a Peach* (ambition and the costs of scale).

### Inference
- **Credibility** comes from visible authenticity (open kitchen, recognizable dishes, diaspora diners), home-market scale and early ratings, which matter most for unknown brands.
- **"Premium" means a visible reason to pay more** (handmade, freshly brewed, a hero dish), plus a value entry point.
- **Launch lines are not demand.** Opening queues come from diaspora communities and social media; repeat visits from other locals decide the business.
- The tipping data favors no-tip counter service.
- **Familiar plus different travels best:** a format diners already know (cafeteria line, fried chicken, bakery) with one distinctive element (handmade udon, dim sum, fruit tea). Be explicit about adaptation; hiding it risks a sense of betrayal, while refusing all adaptation hurts speed and sourcing.
- **Likely first customers:**

| Segment | Main barrier | Trial trigger | Site |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diaspora anchor | A diluted menu | Chef credibility, signature dishes | Near the community, events |
| Cuisine-curious urban diner | Unfamiliar menu, value doubts | Simple ordering, a photogenic signature, reviews | Dense mixed-use neighborhood |
| Value-led lunch customer | Queues, price, inconsistency | Clear combos, visible speed | Office or student corridor |
| Experience seeker | A gimmick without good food | One memorable mechanism | Destination retail |
| Delivery-first customer | Fees, soggy food, opaque brand | Travel-tested menu, clear kitchen identity | Dense delivery zone |

**So what for an entrant:** Lead with one recognizable hero product and make authenticity visible in the store. Price with a clear value tier from day one. Plan for off-premises and app ordering, and treat the first few hundred reviews as a launch milestone.

## Rules, costs and regulatory risk (2025-2026)

### Fact
- **Franchising:** the FTC Franchise Rule (16 CFR Part 436) requires the franchise disclosure document at least 14 calendar days before a prospect signs or pays. Fourteen states require registration or a filing: California, Hawaii, Illinois, Indiana, Maryland, Michigan, Minnesota, New York, North Dakota, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Virginia, Washington and Wisconsin. [Wiley Rein summary](https://www.wiley.law/assets/htmldocuments/Regulation%20of%20Franchise%20Sales.pdf) (document dated 2013) [VERIFY current list with state regulators]
- **Food safety and local permits:** states and cities adopt and adapt the FDA Food Code, and local health departments license and inspect; building, zoning, fire, signage, music and outdoor dining permits vary by city. [FDA Food Code](https://www.fda.gov/food/fda-food-code/food-code-2022), [SBA](https://www.sba.gov/document/report--licenses-permits-approvals)
- **Menu labeling:** chains with 20 or more locations selling substantially the same menu under one name must post calories on menus and boards with the 2,000-calorie statement, and give written nutrition information on request (since May 7, 2018). [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-labeling-nutrition/menu-labeling-requirements)
- **Scheduling laws:** NYC's Fair Workweek law requires fast-food schedules 14 days ahead, premiums for changes and clopenings, offering hours to current staff first, and just cause for firing or cutting hours over 15%. [NYC DCWP](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/businesses/fairworkweek-deductions-laws-employers.page) Paris Baguette paid $3M in September 2023 to settle violations at all its NYC locations, covering over 1,500 workers. [NYC DCWP, Sep 2023](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/news/045-23/department-consumer-worker-protection-settles-paris-baguette-widespread-violations)
- **Wages:** California's fast-food minimum has been $20 since April 2024. The Fast Food Council, which can raise it yearly for inflation, went dormant in 2025 without deciding an increase. [Restaurant Business, 2025](https://restaurantbusinessonline.com/workforce/californias-fast-food-council-has-fallen-dormant) [VERIFY 2026 status] State minimums from Jan 1, 2026 include Washington $17.13, Connecticut $16.94, California $16.90, New Jersey $15.92, Colorado $15.16 and Arizona $15.15. [EPI](https://www.epi.org/chart/2026-jan-mw-figure-a-19-states-will-increase-their-minimum-wages-on-january-1-2026-minimum-wage-increase-type-of-increase-number-of-affected-workers-and-wage-impacts-by-state/) New York City is $17.00. [NELP, Dec 2025](https://nelp.org/insights-research/raises-from-coast-to-coast-in-2026)
- **Joint employer:** on Feb 27, 2026 the NLRB reinstated its 2020 standard, requiring "substantial direct and immediate control" over essential terms such as wages, hours and hiring. It covers only NLRB matters, not wage-and-hour law, where the Labor Department's position has wavered. [QSR, Mar 2026](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/joint-employer-rule-where-are-we-now/)
- **Liquor (California example):** from Jan 1, 2026, an original beer-and-wine license application costs $1,135, while a new full-liquor (Type 47) license, $19,840, is "available only to priority drawing winners"; others buy one by transfer. [California ABC](https://www.abc.ca.gov/licensing/license-fees/application-fee-schedules) Transfer market prices [VERIFY].
- **Visas:** the E-2 lets a business at least 50% owned by treaty nationals bring officers, supervisors and critical-skill staff (five-year validity); it is increasingly popular with Japanese companies. [Fisher Phillips, Mar 2025](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/e-2-visa-japanese-companies) The L-1 needs a parent, subsidiary or affiliate link and one year with the foreign company in the past three; new-office approvals last one year, and L-1A stays can reach seven years. [Clark Hill](https://clarkhill.com/services/practice/immigration-law/l-1-visas-intracompany-transferee) [VERIFY against USCIS; its page could not be opened]
- **Costs and tariffs:** Kura Sushi USA cited "tariff pressures" on margins in fiscal 2025, with labor at 31.1% of sales in Q4 and about $2.5M net capex per new unit. [Kura 8-K, Nov 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1772177/000119312525269685/krus-ex99_1.htm) Paris Baguette is building a Texas plant partly to lower cost of goods. [NRN, Jan 2026](https://www.nrn.com/fast-casual/here-s-how-paris-baguette-plans-to-almost-quadruple-in-size-by-2030) For imported ingredient and equipment rates, use the Section 301 table in the US country doc; health permits are covered in the licensing note.

### Inference
- Franchising is the fast path, but it needs an FDD, state registrations and U.S. counsel before the first sale.
- Labor risk is not only the wage level but city scheduling and fast-food rules; Paris Baguette still paid $3M in NYC.
- Concepts relying on home-country sauces, teas or equipment should test U.S. sourcing early (as HeyTea and Paris Baguette did) to hedge tariffs.

**So what for an entrant:** Budget for franchise counsel and an FDD before promising franchisees anything. Model labor at California and NYC rates, with scheduling compliance built into your software. Decide early whether alcohol is core; in quota states it can cost five figures.

- **Foreign brands now compete with each other:** HeyTea, Luckin and Chagee are entering the same cities (see cases). Luckin's U.S. drip coffee costs about $3-5, close to Starbucks. [Korea Times, Nov 2025](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/business/companies/20251106/genesis-bbq-expands-its-us-fried-chicken-market-to-33-states)

### Inference
- The real competitor depends on format: **Starbucks and Dutch Bros** for drinks; **Chick-fil-A, Raising Cane's and hot chicken** for chicken; **Chipotle, Cava and Panda Express** for bowls; **Darden brands and Cheesecake Factory** for casual dining occasions; and **other Asian imports** in the same malls. Delivery-only virtual brands and grocery prepared foods, meal kits and frozen meals compete for the same night in.
- Chick-fil-A and Raising Cane's show that a narrow menu done well wins the highest unit volumes.
- White space: Asian formats at mainstream scale (udon, Filipino, Korean bakery, tea houses) in suburbs and the Sun Belt, and "affordable premium" between McDonald's value and $15+ bowls.

**So what for an entrant:** Benchmark unit economics against the leader in your format. Pick a format where the U.S. leader is weak in your cuisine. Expect another Asian import in the same mall, and differentiate on product, not origin.

## Foreign entry cases since 2016

Method: foreign brands that entered, scaled or restructured in the U.S. from 2016 to October 2026; some entered earlier. "Success" means growing at the October 2026 check, not proven profitable.

### Successes
- **Jollibee / Philippines / Chicago entry 2016 (U.S. earlier [VERIFY year])**
  - Entry mode: company-owned plus franchising; suburban drive-thrus, urban strip centers.
  - Outcome: thriving. 107 North American stores (78 U.S.), over $500M systemwide sales, about $15K daily sales per store; target 500 by 2030; international arm's U.S. listing likely late 2027.
  - Why: cause not reported.
  - Source: [Bisnow, Feb 2026](https://www.bisnow.com/news/chicago/retail/jollibee-posts-record-sales-with-global-spinoff-on-horizon-eyes-chicago-expansion-133227)
- **Paris Baguette / South Korea (SPC) / U.S. entry year [VERIFY]**
  - Entry mode: franchising (530 units in development); positioning: neighborhood bakery-cafe with "choose your own" bakery boxes.
  - Outcome: thriving. 260 U.S. stores, 1,000 targeted within four years; 27% unit and 31% sales growth in 2024; 19 straight quarters of positive same-store sales.
  - Why (source): management credits "leadership and vision."
  - Source: [NRN, Jan 2026](https://www.nrn.com/fast-casual/here-s-how-paris-baguette-plans-to-almost-quadruple-in-size-by-2030)
- **HeyTea / China / December 2023 (New York)**
  - Entry mode: stores in landmark areas; U.S. warehouses, 13 product categories sourced locally.
  - Key moves: buy-one-get-one launch; Times Square flagship (3,500 cups on opening day); a New York teabar built for lingering, not takeaway.
  - Outcome: growing. 2 U.S. stores in early 2024, over 30 by August 2025, over 40 by July 2026.
  - Why: cause not reported.
  - Sources: [TechNode](https://technode.com/2023/12/11/chinese-bubble-tea-chain-heytea-enters-america/), [EqualOcean, Aug 2025](https://equalocean.com/news/2025080621624-heytea-overseas-store-count-surges-over-6-fold-past-year), [China Daily, Jul 2026](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202607/13/WS6a543cffa310986e2b464e2b.html)
- **Kura Sushi USA / Japan / entry year [VERIFY]**
  - Entry mode: company-owned "technology-enabled" sushi chain; U.S. IPO in 2019 funded faster growth. [Kura](https://kurasushi.com/newsroom/15-year-anniversary)
  - Outcome: growing. Fiscal 2025 sales $282.8M (from $237.9M), 79 restaurants after a record 15 openings; comparable sales down 1.3%.
  - Why: cause not reported.
  - Source: [Kura 8-K, Nov 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1772177/000119312525269685/krus-ex99_1.htm)

### Struggles and exits
- **Guzman y Gomez / Australia / January 2020 (Naperville, IL)**
  - Entry mode: build-your-own fast casual; eight Chicago-area restaurants at exit.
  - Outcome: exited May 22, 2026; US$30-40M one-off hit.
  - Why (source): "this was not translating to an improvement in sales momentum"; the U.S. would take "significantly more time and capital than we had expected."
  - Sources: [NRN, May 2026](https://www.nrn.com/fast-casual/guzman-y-gomez-has-exited-the-u-s-market), [Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/guzman-y-gomez-exits-us-market-to-refocus-on-australia-singapore-and-japan)
- **Le Pain Quotidien / Belgium / U.S. Chapter 11 in May 2020**
  - Outcome: all 98 U.S. units closed; sold to Aurify Brands for about $3M, with plans to reopen at least 35.
  - Why (source): multi-year sales decline from more competition, "a lack of investment in digital platforms" and the shift to grab-and-go; the pandemic was the "last straw."

### Patterns (found 4 successes, 6 mixed, 5 struggles or exits, 3 recent with no outcome)
- **What winners share:** a narrow hero product (Chickenjoy, bakery boxes, fruit tea, sushi), a home-community base, a scalable growth engine (franchising or a listing) and local supply (Texas plant, U.S. warehouses).
- **What separates them from failures:** the failures were sub-scale in one metro (GYG) or sit-down bakery-cafes that missed digital and grab-and-go (Le Pain Quotidien, Maison Kayser). Tim Hortons shows home-market fame does not transfer when the format does not fit.
- **Targets slip:** Marugame (150 targeted, about two dozen reached), Leon (15 planned, four opened) and Tim Hortons show announced targets are weak evidence.
- **Scaling on opening buzz kills concepts:** Ikinari Steak and Leon expanded before one-city unit economics were proven. Set stop and go thresholds before opening, and don't let opening-week queues stand in for six-month repeat and labor data.
- **Tensions to keep in mind:** diners seek authenticity but reward convenience and adaptation; a queue is social proof but also abandonment; kiosks speed service but can cut warmth and upselling; franchising saves capital but adds control and disclosure burdens.
- **Survivorship bias:** quiet closures by small foreign tea and dessert franchises are rarely reported, so failures are under-counted. Leads not confirmed in this check: Din Tai Fung, Tous les Jours, Gong Cha, Nando's, Ichiran, Bonchon. Wagamama's first U.S. unit was Boston in 2007. [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/operations/london-born-wagamama-takes-another-swing-us-growth)

**So what for an entrant:** Enter with density in one metro and enough capital for several years, not a handful of test stores. Start with the diaspora but plan the menu and format for mainstream repeat visits. Localize supply before scaling.

## AI outlook (analysis)

### Scenario 1: Voice AI takes orders, but busy sites keep humans
- **Evidence:** McDonald's ended its IBM voice test at about 100 restaurants in 2024 but said voice ordering "will be part of our restaurants' future." [NRN, Jun 2024](https://www.nrn.com/quick-service/mcdonald-s-is-ending-its-ai-drive-thru-test-with-ibm) Taco Bell, after rolling voice AI out to hundreds of drive-thrus and a prank order of 18,000 water cups, is reconsidering it at busier sites. [Food On Demand, Sep 2025](https://foodondemand.com/09242025/it-seems-every-brand-wants-to-use-voice-ai-yet-taco-bell-is-pulling-back/)
- **Signals to watch:** published accuracy and speed data, and phone-order AI at smaller chains.

### Scenario 2: AI maps and assistants decide where people eat
- **Evidence:** Google's Ask Maps (Mar 12, 2026, U.S. and India) answers requests like "a cozy aesthetic and a table for 4," draws on reviews from over 500 million contributors and books reservations. [Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-immersive-navigation/) Map apps and search already drive 18% and 16% of discovery. [Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/data/how-guests-discover-new-restaurants)
- **Signals to watch:** reservations and directions coming from AI answers, and whether new brands with few reviews get recommended at all.

### Scenario 3: AI scheduling and pricing run into labor rules and value backlash
- **Evidence:** Wendy's faced backlash in February 2024 after saying digital menu boards could enable dynamic pricing, then said it "will not implement surge pricing." [FOX 29, Feb 2024](https://www.fox29.com/news/wendys-backlash-surge-pricing-reports-denies-claims) NYC's Fair Workweek rules penalize last-minute schedule changes. [NYC DCWP](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/businesses/fairworkweek-deductions-laws-employers.page)
- **Analysis:** AI forecasting is most useful for compliant schedules 14 days out and for off-peak deals, not peak price hikes.
- **Signals to watch:** rules on algorithmic pricing and scheduling, and chains reporting labor savings from forecasting.

**So what for an entrant:** Keep Google listings, menus, photos and hours complete, since AI maps read them. Use AI for phone orders and scheduling before the drive-thru. Avoid any pricing that looks like surge pricing.
