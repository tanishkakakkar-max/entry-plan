# Emerging and specialty products in the United States: First Port routing note

## 1. Purpose

“Other” is not a coherent U.S. market. It is a routing label for ideas whose customer, risk and channel do not fit an existing category. Use this note to decide which full First Port research track should own the product.

**So what for an entrant:** classify the customer job and regulatory surface before estimating market size.

## 2. Routing table

| If the offer mainly… | Route to | First question |
|---|---|---|
| Changes what a person or pet ingests | Food & Beverage, Health & Wellness or Pet Care | Is it food, supplement, drug, animal food or cosmetic? |
| Touches babies or children | Baby, Kids & Family or Toys, Games & Hobbies | What is the intended age and use? |
| Supports exercise or outdoor activity | Sports, Fitness & Outdoor | Is the promise performance, safety, adherence or identity? |
| Moves people or interacts with vehicles | Automotive & Mobility | What certification, service and infrastructure are required? |
| Teaches, assesses or advises | Education & Professional Services | Who pays, who uses and what outcome is promised? |
| Collects data or contains connected hardware | Technology plus the relevant vertical | What data, radio, safety and end-of-life duties apply? |
| Furnishes, decorates or organizes space | Home & Living | Is the buying driver style, function, durability or installation? |
| Is worn or applied to the body | Apparel & Accessories, Beauty & Personal Care or Health & Wellness | Is the claim aesthetic, protective, therapeutic or medical? |
| Is a new retail/service format | The customer’s underlying category plus Restaurants or Professional Services | Does the format improve unit economics or only novelty? |

**So what for an entrant:** assign one primary vertical and add secondary reviews only for real overlaps; multi-category positioning should not obscure compliance ownership.

## 3. Four-gate screen

### Gate 1 - customer and job

Name the user, payer, approver and beneficiary. Reconstruct the last real purchase or workaround. If the product is “for everyone,” research is not ready.

### Gate 2 - claim and risk

Write the strongest planned claim and worst plausible harm. Determine whether FDA, FTC, CPSC, FCC, NHTSA, USDA, COPPA, state professional rules or another regime may apply. **[VERIFY with product-specific counsel.]**

### Gate 3 - delivery and service

Map import classification, landed cost, inventory, returns, repair, replacement and adverse-event handling. Confirm HTS code, origin and tariff stacking with a customs broker. **[VERIFY every physical SKU.]**

### Gate 4 - evidence and economics

Define the smallest observable success outcome and the full cost to produce it. Include refunds, warranty, human support, channel margin and customer acquisition.

**So what for an entrant:** stop at the first failed gate; more promotion will not repair unclear classification or negative service economics.

## 4. Common first-customer patterns

| Pattern | Useful when | Required proof |
|---|---|---|
| Problem community | Pain is specific and recurring | Before/after behavior, safety and repeat |
| Enthusiast niche | Users can recognize a technical advantage | Independent comparisons and credible participation |
| Trusted professional channel | Risk or complexity is high | Training, evidence, liability and support |
| Values-led microsegment | Provenance or sustainability is central | Traceability plus equal-or-better performance |
| Employer/institution pilot | Buyer and user differ | Implementation, utilization and outcome data |

**So what for an entrant:** the first segment should make the benefit easiest to observe, not merely be the largest audience.

## 5. Evidence standard

For every new “Other” subcategory:

1. Use peer-reviewed work for behavioral or outcome claims where available.
2. Keep government rules and official statistics in a separate source class.
3. Use Reddit and reviews to discover objections and language, never to estimate prevalence.
4. Use books and expert opinions as interpretive lenses, not proof.
5. Verify foreign-company U.S. entry dates, modes and outcomes from direct or reputable sources.
6. Aim for five to ten cases per year only where the public record supports them; never pad.
7. Mark facts that depend on SKU, state, claim or private economics as **[VERIFY]**.
8. Maintain at least an 80% peer-reviewed share among counted analytical articles/case studies; publish the numerator and denominator.

**So what for an entrant:** source diversity is useful only when each source type has a clearly bounded role.

## 6. AI and future scenarios

### 2026-2028

- **Base:** AI lowers content, support and localization costs, making differentiation harder.
- **Upside:** specialized models reduce customer uncertainty and improve fit, maintenance or adherence.
- **Downside:** automated claims, unsafe advice, privacy failures and low-quality personalization invite enforcement and distrust.

### 2029-2036

Generic digital features become cheap. Defensibility shifts toward proprietary consented data, validated outcomes, physical operations, professional trust, community and service reliability. This is a scenario, not a forecast.

**So what for an entrant:** use AI where it improves a measured customer outcome; do not treat “AI-powered” as a category.

## 7. Initial 30-day validation sprint

1. Assign the primary category and regulatory owner.
2. Interview 15 recent buyers/non-buyers around the same job.
3. Audit ten direct substitutes and three workarounds.
4. Obtain preliminary product classification and landed-cost review.
5. Test one concrete promise with a prototype or concierge service.
6. Define stop conditions for safety, demand and unit economics.

**So what for an entrant:** the result should be a decision - route, narrow, redesign or stop - not a larger pile of trend statistics.
