# Health and wellness brands: what the research says about claims, labels and buyers (checked October 2026)

Nine of the eleven sources below are peer-reviewed journal articles (82%). The two that are not are marked [industry/regulatory] and used only for market size and rules.

## Cross-market: how health claims work on shoppers
- Ikonen, Sotgiu, Aydinli & Verlegh (2020), "Consumer effects of front-of-package nutrition labeling: An interdisciplinary meta-analysis," Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science 48(3), 360-383. 114 studies. Labels make healthier products easier to spot but are weaker at changing choices; they create halo effects that improve perceptions of indulgent products as well as healthy ones; interpretive nutrient labels lift health perceptions of both but raise purchase intent only for products already seen as healthy. So what: a health label on a snack or chocolate improves how it is judged, but it sells only if the product already reads as a "virtue" product. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-019-00663-9

## Japan
- Ujiie, Shimada, Narui & Watanabe (2018), "A Survey of Japanese Consumers' Recognition and Consciousness of the Foods with Function Claims System," Japanese Journal of Drug Informatics 20(2), 104-110. 238 consumers, Tokyo area. Only 18.1% knew the FFC term well; 59.2% had heard of it without knowing what it meant; 52.9% would buy an FFC product only at the same price as a regular one, 32.4% would pay a little more. So what: the FFC label is not a price lever on its own; the product has to carry the premium. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjdi/20/2/20_104/_article/
- [industry/regulatory] nippon.com on the FFC system: notification to the Consumer Affairs Agency, not individual approval; 6,795 products from 1,693 companies notified as of March 22, 2024; market forecast ¥686.5B in 2023 (Fuji Keizai); a government task force reviewed the system after the 2024 Kobayashi red yeast rice (beni koji) health problems. So what: expect tighter scrutiny of functional claims; check the current rules before filing. https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h01960/

## Singapore
- Shin, Puri & Finkelstein (2023), "A randomized trial to evaluate the impact of Singapore's forthcoming Nutri-grade front-of-pack beverage label on food and beverage purchases," International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity 20(1). 138 participants, real purchases in a functional online store. The Nutri-Grade label cut sugar purchased from beverages by 1.51 g per serving but did not improve overall diet quality or saturated fat. So what: a graded label moves the specific nutrient it grades; a lower-sugar drink or snack gets a measurable edge, a "healthy" halo does not. https://doaj.org/article/b6c57b89287a4e308447c15397535650
- Finkelstein, Doble, Ang, Wong & van Dam (2021), "A randomized controlled trial testing the effects of a positive front-of-pack label with or without a physical activity equivalent label on food purchases," Appetite 158, 104997. The Healthier Choice Symbol raised the share of HCS-labelled products bought by about 5 percentage points without changing calories per serving. So what: qualifying for the Healthier Choice Symbol is worth a real but modest sales lift; it is a shelf signal, not a brand. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.appet.2020.104997

## India
- Jadhav et al. (2023), "Factors governing consumers buying behavior concerning nutraceutical product," Food Science & Nutrition 11(9), 4988-5003. Review plus a 400-person Mumbai survey (Jan 2022, ages 15-75, 60% women). Drivers: perceived health benefit, knowledge and education, trust (during COVID people relied on others' experience over clinical trials), recommendations from health professionals and gym instructors, brand and packaging; younger consumers (15-35) and women bought more. Barriers: price, limited evidence and skepticism, poor taste, low awareness and side-effect fears among older consumers, availability outside metros. So what: metro, young, female, educated is the entry segment; taste and trusted recommenders matter more than claims. https://doi.org/10.1002/fsn3.3518
- Garg, Narwal & Kumar (2024), "Exploring the determinants of purchase intention of organic consumers for organic food items: an exploratory study in India," British Food Journal 126(3), 1238-1258. 574 Indian organic-food consumers. Purchase intention driven by health consciousness, ecological trustworthiness and functional value. So what: for an Indian health-positioned food, "healthy and safe" is the message, with trust in the claim as the gate. https://doi.org/10.1108/BFJ-09-2023-0788
- Chopra, Jagose & Pandey (2025), "Health is wealth-eating for tomorrow: Factors influencing purchase intention of plant-based diets in India," Cogent Business & Management 12(1). Environmental attitude first, health benefits second, epistemic value third; social value worked through celebrity recommendation; subjective norms (family, peers) not significant. So what: health-food adoption in urban India is a personal, influencer-led choice; brands should lead with health and environment rather than animal welfare. https://doi.org/10.1080/23311975.2024.2443567
- [industry/regulatory] FSSAI Nutra Regulations 2022 (health supplements, nutraceuticals, FSDU, FSMP, pre- and probiotics): no disease treatment or prevention claims without Food Authority approval; structure, function and general well-being claims allowed with evidence; labels must state "HEALTH SUPPLEMENT" and "NOT FOR MEDICINAL USE" in bold capitals, target group, dosage, RDA percentages and warnings; imports need an FSSAI licence tied to a DGFT Importer-Exporter Code. https://food.chemlinked.com/foodpedia/india-health-supplement-regulation

## United States
- McKeon & Hallman (2024), "Front-of-Package Protein Labels on Cereal Create Health Halos," Foods 13(8), 1139. 1,022 US adults. A protein label made a cereal seem healthier and more nutritious even with no health claim: 54.0% said it would build muscle vs 4.7% for the original; 57.3% were more likely to buy it; 59.2% expected it to cost more; most did not notice it had more sugar, sodium and calories per cup. So what: a single nutrient callout (protein, fiber) is the most powerful front-of-pack lever in the US, and it invites regulatory and media scrutiny if the rest of the panel does not back it up. https://doi.org/10.3390/foods13081139
- Chattaraman, Lee, Robinson, Book & Al-Amin (2023), "The effects of social distance and front-of-package claims on healthy food selection," Nutrients 15(15), 3427. 171 US parents from low-to-mid income households. Buying for themselves, people preferred nutrient claims (or no claim) over health claims; buying for their children, health claims won; consumers who rate healthy eating as important respond much more to nutrient claims. So what: nutrient claims for the adult self-buyer, health-benefit claims for the parent gift or family buyer. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15153427

## How to use this in a plan
- Lead with one specific nutrient or ingredient fact, not "healthy" (Ikonen; McKeon & Hallman).
- In Japan, do not expect the FFC label to justify a premium (Ujiie); in Singapore, qualify for Healthier Choice or a good Nutri-Grade where the product allows (Shin; Finkelstein).
- In India, the buyer is young, urban and recommendation-driven; taste and trust beat claims (Jadhav; Chopra); label and claim rules are strict (FSSAI).
- Everywhere, a health halo on an indulgent product improves perception but not purchase unless the product already reads as a virtue (Ikonen).
