# What happened when fast-casual and QSR brands crossed borders (checked October 2026)

Use these as the "learn from their mistakes" set for any restaurant brand. Each line: what happened, the lesson, the source.

## Japan
- Shake Shack entered Tokyo in November 2015 with Sazaby League (the group behind Starbucks Japan), on an exclusive development agreement signed Feb 2015. Opened early because the partner secured a good site fast and the supply chain came together. Planned only about ten stores by 2020; the CEO said he preferred slow growth to protect taste and service. Lesson: a partner with a US-brand track record plus deliberate pace. https://toyokeizai.net/articles/-/93107
- Taco Bell and Carl's Jr. both entered Japan in the late 1980s and left within a few years without building recognition ("we couldn't figure out a way to increase brand recognition," Taco Bell's CEO). Burger King also failed in the 1980s and returned in 2007 with limited-time offers and low prices. Taco Bell returned to Shibuya in April 2015 with Japan-only items like Taco Rice. Lesson: awareness and localization, not just product. https://toyokeizai.net/articles/-/77351
- Carl's Jr. Japan was a subsidiary of Mitsuuroko Group (a water and energy company). It stalled at four stores; in 2024 Mitsuuroko transferred the operating rights to Aleph (operator of Bikkuri Donkey) to refocus on its own brands. Lesson: a non-restaurant parent is the wrong partner. https://www.ryutsuu.biz/strategy/q062540.html
- Guzman y Gomez has operated in Tokyo since April 2015 through a master-franchise partner and is still opening. https://www.marketing-interactive.com/guzman-y-gomez-exits-us-market-to-refocus-on-australia-singapore-and-japan
- Crisp Salad Works (domestic, app-first customizable salads): 55th store opened Apr 16, 2026 at Mitamachi Terrace, takeout only, weekdays only, app pre-order and self-checkout. The model to study for Tokyo office lunch. https://www.haveagood-holiday.com/en/articles/crisp-salad-works-mitamachi-terrace-opening

## Singapore
- Guzman y Gomez: first international restaurant opened in Singapore end of 2013; 24th Singapore restaurant opened May 2026; run by a master-franchise partner with "healthy unit economics" while the US business was shut. Lesson: partner-led build-your-own fast casual works here. https://www.marketing-interactive.com/guzman-y-gomez-exits-us-market-to-refocus-on-australia-singapore-and-japan
- Stuff'd (local kebab, burrito and bowl chain): 36 outlets by Nov 2021; added Impossible Beef across all outlets. Direct competitor for any pita or wrap concept. https://qsrmedia.com/indonesian/node/514519493
- SaladStop! (founded 2009, Temasek-backed): 69 outlets across eight markets at its S$12M raise; group brands include Heybo and Wooshi. https://vulcanpost.com/?p=769159
- Deliveroo exited Singapore Mar 4, 2026 (DoorDash retiring the brand there, in Japan, Qatar and Uzbekistan). Grab and foodpanda remain. https://www.ntu.edu.sg/business/news-events/news/story-detail/deliveroo%27s-singapore-exit-may-lead-to-higher-fees--fewer-discounts--analysts-say

## India
- Taco Bell India: master franchisee Burman Hospitality (Dabur family); about three dozen stores in May 2019, 50th in Dec 2019 (Bangalore), target 600 restaurants by 2029 and India as Taco Bell's largest market outside the US. Current count [verify]. https://www.restaurantindia.in/news/taco-bell-reaches-50th-restaurant-milestone-in-india.n19015
- Dunkin' India: Jubilant FoodWorks (3,500+ stores across six markets, Domino's and Popeyes in India) is closing Dunkin' India as its development agreement expires Dec 31, 2026, after about 15 years. Lesson: even the strongest Indian franchisor cannot save a US brand whose product does not fit. https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/dunkin-donuts-to-close-in-india-by-2026-end-heres-why-523949-2026-04-03
- Just Falafel (Dubai Lebanese QSR) launched in India in 2019 via Yellow Tie Hospitality with a plan for 100 outlets by 2024; outcome [verify], likely far short. https://www.hospitalitynewsmag.com/just-falafel-opens-in-india-with-plans-for-100-more/
- Delivery-native healthy bowl brands: Harvest Salad Co, 49 cloud kitchens across Mumbai, Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad, Gurugram (Jul 2026) https://indianretailer.com/franchisetv/archives/news/harvest-salad-co-enters-delhi-ncr-49th-outlet-strengthens-healthy-qsr-expansion ; Salad Days, 25 cloud kitchens, Rs 30 crore Series A (Jan 2025) https://www.restaurantindia.in/news/salad-days-raises-inr-30-crores-in-series-a-funding-co-led-by-v3-ventures-and-client
- FDI: up to 100% foreign investment in QSRs under the automatic route. https://www.mondaq.com/india/inward-foreign-investment/1535116/fdi-in-restaurants-a-gateway-to-enhanced-customer-experience

## United States (for foreign brands entering)
- Guzman y Gomez opened its first US store Jan 2020 (Naperville, IL) and closed all US restaurants May 22, 2026; US$30-40M one-off hit; co-CEO: the US would take "significantly more time and capital than we had expected." https://www.marketing-interactive.com/guzman-y-gomez-exits-us-market-to-refocus-on-australia-singapore-and-japan
- Pepper Lunch (Japan) is scaling in the US purely through multi-unit franchise development agreements (Carl Karcher Group 20 units; Majestic 10 units Florida; NiKU 10 units Hawaii). https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/pepper-lunch-and-carl-karcher-group-sign-20-unit-franchise-deal-california-las-vegas/729396/

## Pattern
- Winners abroad used an experienced local food-service operator (Sazaby, GYG's partners, Alshaya, Alsea, Burman). Losers had the wrong parent (Mitsuuroko), no awareness plan (1980s Taco Bell), or a product that did not fit (Dunkin' India), or went sub-scale into a huge market (GYG in the US).
