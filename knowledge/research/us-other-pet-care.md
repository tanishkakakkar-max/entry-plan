# Pet care in the United States: market-entry research

## 1. Executive summary

Pet care includes food, treats, supplements, grooming, apparel, beds, toys, tracking devices, insurance-adjacent services and other products for companion animals. It should not be treated as one regulatory class: pet food and supplements fall under animal-food and sometimes animal-drug rules, while accessories and connected devices create different safety, warranty and privacy obligations.

Five findings:

1. **The emotional buyer and biological user are different.** Attachment and “family member” identity can raise spending, but the pet’s palatability, comfort, tolerance and behavior determine repeat. Peer-reviewed work consistently finds health, nutrition, quality, ingredients and freshness among leading pet-food criteria. [P1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31156266/) [P2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41301945/)
2. **Trust must be more specific than “premium” or “natural.”** Ingredient clarity, veterinary evidence, feeding instructions, manufacturing controls and claim boundaries matter because owners encounter conflicting advice and can overestimate fashionable claims. [P3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41514729/) [P4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30832667/)
3. **The U.S. is large but crowded.** APPA reports $158 billion in 2025 U.S. pet-industry expenditure and 95 million pet-owning households. This includes veterinary care and other services, so it is not a product-level addressable market. [G1](https://americanpetproducts.org/2026-state-of-the-industry)
4. **Subscription economics depend on continuing proof.** Tractive’s U.S. expansion demonstrates the appeal of recurring safety and wellness value; its Whistle migration also shows how hardware, software and subscription continuity become one trust promise. [C2](https://tractive.com/blog/en/press/tractive-acquires-whistle)
5. **Foreign provenance works when it conveys a concrete benefit.** New Zealand sourcing can signal traceability or novel protein, but exotic origin alone cannot substitute for U.S.-compliant formulation, claims, supply continuity and palatability.

**Provisional recommendation:** enter with one species, one need state and one hero product; combine a proof bundle with a U.S. replenishment model. For food or supplements, secure regulatory and veterinary review before advertising. For connected products, plan replacement, privacy and end-of-life support before launch.

**So what for an entrant:** the defensible wedge is not “pet lovers”; it is a clearly evidenced job such as digestive tolerance, escape prevention, winter protection or easier cleaning.

## 2. Research question, scope, and evidence rules

**Question:** how should a foreign pet-food, supplement, accessory or pet-tech company enter the United States and build trust, repeat purchase and compliant claims?

Peer-reviewed studies support durable behavioral findings; FDA and industry bodies support rules and current context; dated company and reputable trade sources verify entry events; books, Reddit and reviews provide interpretive or exploratory evidence only.

**Peer-review audit:** 9 of 10 counted analytical articles/case studies are peer-reviewed (90.0%). Government sources, trade statistics, company records, entry-verification reporting, books and Reddit are separately classified and excluded from that denominator.

**So what for an entrant:** decide first whether the product is ingested, applied, worn, connected or purely decorative; that choice changes the compliance, proof and post-purchase model.

## 3. Market context and category structure

The broad market has four practical arenas:

- **Nutrition and wellness:** food, treats, supplements and dental products; high repeat but high claim and safety exposure.
- **Everyday equipment:** bowls, leashes, beds, litter, grooming and travel products; proof comes from fit, durability, cleaning and pet acceptance.
- **Identity and gifting:** apparel, matching products and décor; emotional attachment and relational identity matter. [P5](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296321008456)
- **Connected care:** GPS, activity, feeding and health monitoring; usefulness, accuracy, privacy and subscription continuity drive adoption.

The reachable first customer is often not the average pet owner. It is an owner experiencing a visible problem, actively seeking advice, and willing to document whether a solution works.

**So what for an entrant:** size the opportunity by species, need state, channel and repeat cadence - not by total pet-industry expenditure.

## 4. Customer psychology and target customers

### Health guardian

This buyer wants to prevent discomfort and reduce future veterinary risk. Product knowledge can be low even when involvement is high. U.S. research finds natural ingredients, value, convenience and health claims all influence pet-food choice. [P6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34828032/) Veterinary guidance is a meaningful trust path, although not every veterinarian will endorse a retail product. [P7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33272957/)

### Attachment and identity buyer

Pet attachment, anthropomorphism and self-expansion are associated with fashion-product purchase intention. [P5](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296321008456) More recent work links relational identity with pet and matching-fashion intention. [P8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42792344/) These findings support identity-led accessories, but they do not prove repeat demand in the U.S.

### Skeptical ingredient reader

Owners may distrust processed food, preservatives or opaque labels. Raw-diet research shows that ingredient-control motives can coexist with weak risk knowledge. [P4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30832667/) The entrant should explain formulation choices without attacking established nutrition science or implying unsupported disease treatment.

### Sustainable experimenter

Novel-protein acceptance depends on both perceived pet health and sustainability, while food neophobia can block trial. [P9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38225138/) U.S. owners have also shown willingness to pay for animal-welfare-friendly ingredients. [P10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38487449/)

### Connected-care adopter

This customer values escape prevention, activity visibility or early warning but worries about battery life, false alerts, location privacy and subscription value. Human-wearable evidence is only an analogy: usefulness, ease, security and privacy shape trust, but pet use needs direct validation. **Inference.**

Reddit searches in [r/dogs](https://www.reddit.com/r/dogs/search/?q=pet%20food%20ingredients&restrict_sr=1), [r/catfood](https://www.reddit.com/r/catfood/search/?q=brand%20trust&restrict_sr=1) and [r/puppy101](https://www.reddit.com/r/puppy101/search/?q=supplement&restrict_sr=1) repeatedly expose questions about ingredients, stomach tolerance, veterinarian advice, subscription cancellation and whether premium products are worth it. These are qualitative prompts, not frequency estimates.

**So what for an entrant:** sell observable progress - eating, stool quality, escape incidents, coat, fit, cleaning time or engagement - while avoiding medical certainty that the evidence cannot support.

## 7. Market access, regulation, and operations

- FDA states that imported animal food and pet food can require prior notice, facility registration, FSVP compliance and other import controls. [G2](https://www.fda.gov/industry/importing-fda-regulated-products/importing-animal-and-veterinary-products) [G3](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/import-exports/importing-animal-food)
- Disease-treatment or prevention language can make a product an animal drug rather than ordinary food or supplement. FDA provides claim guidance. [G4](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-foods-feeds/animal-food-labeling-and-pet-food-claims)
- State feed-registration and labeling rules can apply in addition to federal requirements. **[VERIFY by state and formulation.]**
- Animal-derived ingredients may require USDA APHIS permits or certificates. **[VERIFY by ingredient and country of origin.]**
- Devices need product-safety, radio/FCC, battery-shipping, privacy and cybersecurity review as applicable. **[VERIFY by functionality.]**
- Product-specific HTS classification, origin rules and current trade-action stacking must be confirmed with a broker. **[VERIFY every SKU.]**

**So what for an entrant:** complete ingredient, claim, device and import classification before ordering U.S. packaging or inventory.

## 9. Success/failure pattern synthesis

### Repeated success patterns

- one clear pet problem and one measurable promise;
- local inventory for repeat products;
- veterinarian, trainer or credible expert interpretation;
- samples, transition instructions and responsive adverse-event handling;
- subscription tied to ongoing utility rather than discount alone;
- retail expansion after DTC reveals objections and cohorts.

### Repeated failure risks

- human-health language copied into pet claims;
- “natural,” “human grade” or “clean” positioning without evidence;
- poor palatability or fit hidden behind attractive branding;
- subscription friction and expensive customer acquisition;
- proprietary connected hardware without service, parts or shutdown planning;
- importing animal ingredients before permit and FSVP responsibilities are clear.

Alexandra Horowitz’s *Inside of a Dog* and Patricia McConnell’s *The Other End of the Leash* are useful reminders to interpret products from the animal’s behavior rather than projecting only human preferences. They are interpretive books, not counted evidence. [B1](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Inside-of-a-Dog/Alexandra-Horowitz/9781416583431) [B2](https://www.patriciamcconnell.com/store/product/the-other-end-of-the-leash)

**So what for an entrant:** a pet-care brand wins twice - first with the owner’s story, then with the animal’s lived response.

## 10. AI and future outlook

### 2026-2028

- **Base:** AI improves support triage, feeding guidance, image/video interpretation, demand forecasting and product recommendations, with human escalation.
- **Upside:** longitudinal pet data helps owners detect meaningful changes earlier and improves personalized product selection.
- **Downside:** false health alerts, unsupported diagnoses, biased breed models and opaque data sharing create harm and enforcement risk.

### 2029-2036

Connected ecosystems may combine food, activity, location, insurance and veterinary records. The moat shifts from generic recommendations to consented longitudinal data, validated outcomes, professional trust and reliable physical execution. This is a scenario, not a forecast.

### No-regret actions

1. Separate wellness guidance from diagnosis and treatment.
2. Validate model performance by species, breed/body type and use context.
3. Publish data retention, sharing and deletion rules.
4. Keep a human review path for safety and adverse events.
5. Build portable records so customers are not trapped if hardware or service ends.

**So what for an entrant:** AI should reduce uncertainty without pretending to replace veterinary judgment.

## 11. Recommendations and 90-day test plan

1. Choose one species, one need and one hero SKU.
2. Complete U.S. ingredient/claim/device classification and a broker review.
3. Run palatability, tolerance, fit, durability or battery tests appropriate to the product.
4. Recruit 30 target owners for a four-to-eight-week documented trial.
5. Test expert-led education against lifestyle-led creative using the same offer.
6. Measure first use, adverse events, support contacts, repeat, cancellation and net contribution.
7. Add one specialty-retail or veterinary pilot only after the proof bundle is stable.

**So what for an entrant:** do not scale paid acquisition until repeat and safety behavior are visible by cohort.

## 13. Interviews and validation questions

- 15 recent purchase reconstructions by owners with the exact need state.
- Five veterinarians or veterinary nutrition professionals for claim and evidence interpretation.
- Five independent pet retailers or groomers for sell-through and objection data.
- A 30-household trial with diaries, photos and reorder choice at full price.
- Questions: What triggered the search? Who did you trust? What did you fear? What result would justify repeat? What would make you stop immediately? Which claim sounds exaggerated?

**So what for an entrant:** ask about the last real problem episode, not abstract willingness to buy.
