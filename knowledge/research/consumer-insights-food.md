# Consumer insights for food and restaurant brands (peer-reviewed and survey sources, checked October 2026)

## Japan
- Oe & Weeks (2022), "Revisiting the global expansion strategy of fast-food brand," International Journal of Services and Operations Management 42(4). 24 interviews on McDonald's Japan: consumers valued it mainly as a global brand and "American icon" and gave less weight to localization. So what: keep the core brand unmistakable; localize edges, not identity. https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/35058/
- Asahi Group Foods survey (Mar 2023, n=800 workers aged 20-59, via nippon.com): 56.3% have days without an adequate lunch break; 52.4% buy lunch at a convenience store at least weekly (38.4% of women and 48.2% of men three or more times a week); "value for money" top priority at 25.8%; 49.3% have a staple-only lunch at least once a week. So what: speed, pickup and visible value are the product. https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h01696/japanese-workers-put-time-and-money-ahead-of-relaxing-lunch-breaks.html
- From the CAVA Japan field project: Mediterranean already signals health and longevity; enter as a Mediterranean lifestyle brand, not an American import; rice base, less sweet, curated sets alongside build-your-own; Instagram, LINE and Tabelog drive discovery; Japanese IP collaborations earn Gen Z attention.

## Singapore
- Naidoo, van Dam, Ng et al. (2017), "Determinants of eating at local and western fast-food venues in an urban Asian population," International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity 14:69. National Nutrition Survey 2010 (n=1,647) plus 18 focus groups. 77.3% eat at least one main meal out; 61.1% at hawker centres (median 5 times a week); only 1.9% usually at Western fast food; lunch the most common meal out (70.6%); weekly Western fast food for 70.8% of 18-21 year olds vs 3.0% of over-60s. Hawker chosen for variety, low cost (about US$3-4 a meal in 2010) and access despite being judged unhealthy; Western fast food judged unhealthy but convenient. Authors suggest limited halal hawker options push Malay diners toward chains. So what: hawker is the benchmark; convenience and price beat health claims; halal widens the market. https://ijbnpa.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12966-017-0515-x

## India
- Pew Research Center (2021), 29,999 face-to-face interviews, Nov 2019-Mar 2020: 39% of Indian adults self-identify as vegetarian; 81% restrict meat in some way; vegetarianism by religion: Jains 92%, Hindus 44%, Christians 10%, Muslims 8%; 72% of Hindus say eating beef disqualifies someone from being Hindu. https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/07/08/eight-in-ten-indians-limit-meat-in-their-diets-and-four-in-ten-consider-themselves-vegetarian/
- Natrajan & Jacob (2018), "'Provincialising' Vegetarianism," Economic & Political Weekly 53(9): survey-based vegetarian share 23-37% depending on survey (NSSO 36.9%, NFHS 24.7%, IHDS 23.5%); authors argue the true figure is no more than 30% and more realistically about 20%, with huge regional variation (under 2% in some north-eastern states, over 75% in Haryana, Rajasthan, Punjab). So what: vegetarian-first menu is mandatory; beef is off the table; regional menus differ. https://wpunj.edu/cohss/departments/anthropology/faculty/natrajanpubs/NatrajanJacobProvincializingVegetarianisimEPW.pdf
- Najeemudeen & Panchanatham (2016), Ushus Journal of Business Management 15(4), 105 customers of a QSR in Kerala: taste the top reason; friends the biggest influence (43%); 57% dine with family; 73% visit occasionally; typical spend Rs 501-1,000 per visit (40%). Small, one-brand sample. So what: QSR in India is social and occasional, not solo daily lunch. https://journals.christuniversity.in/index.php/ushus/article/download/1640/1369
- TikTok is banned in India; Instagram Reels, YouTube and WhatsApp carry the discovery and service load.

## United States and general
- Tsofliou, Vlachos, Hughes & Appleton (2022), "Barriers and Facilitators Associated with the Adoption of and Adherence to a Mediterranean Style Diet in Adults," Nutrients 14(20):4314. Barriers: high food cost (olive oil especially), unfamiliar ingredients, lack of knowledge or confidence preparing pulses, time, cold climate, limited availability. Facilitators: taste and pleasure, familiarity, warm climate, good access to suitable options in catering outlets. Authors note the difficulty of transferring the diet to non-Mediterranean countries. So what: a Mediterranean fast casual removes the barriers (cost, skill, availability); sell flavor first and explain ingredients plainly in new regions. https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/14/20/4314
