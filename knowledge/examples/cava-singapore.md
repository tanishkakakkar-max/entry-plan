# CAVA → Singapore

**Brand:** CAVA - fast-casual Mediterranean. Build-your-own bowls, pitas and salads, signature dips like Crazy Feta, digital ordering and pickup. Founded by Greek-American friends; 476 company-owned US restaurants and NYSE-listed since 2023 [W1]. No international stores.
**Customer today:** US office workers and suburban families who want a fresh, customizable meal fast.
**Target market:** Singapore

---

## 1. The call
**Test first, and treat Singapore as the rehearsal for Asia, not the prize.** The market is small, English-speaking, rich and brutally expensive, which makes it the cheapest place to learn how CAVA travels and the hardest place to make money doing it. The format works here: build-your-own fast casual has scaled through master-franchise partners, and local bowl chains are everywhere. CAVA should open two stores with a Singapore operator who already runs a Western fast-casual brand, certify halal from day one, and use the results to decide whether Asia is worth a real team.

## 2. Recommended entry point
- **City:** Singapore, starting in the CBD (Raffles Place to Tanjong Pagar) for weekday lunch, with a second site in a high-footfall mall such as Orchard or Bugis for evenings and weekends.
- **Channel:** Master franchise or license with one local operator; two company-standard stores; app pickup plus Grab and foodpanda from launch.
- **Customer:** office workers aged 25-45 who already pay for a healthy lunch bowl, plus Malay-Muslim diners who are underserved by non-halal hawker food.
- **Price:** working hypothesis of S$14-18 per bowl, to be confirmed against a price check of local bowl chains and the S$5-7 hawker lunch [verify].

## 3. Entry strategy
**First 90 days (partner, licences, halal)**
- Pick a partner from groups that already operate a US or Australian fast-casual brand in Singapore. Guzman y Gomez runs its 24 Singapore restaurants through a master-franchise partner that delivers "healthy unit economics," and keeps opening while GYG retreats from the US [W2]. That is the model.
- Register the entity with ACRA, then apply for the SFA Food Shop Licence (S$195 a year) through GoBusiness; the tenancy agreement is needed only at the final stage [W3].
- Start MUIS halal certification before the menu is final: at least two Muslim staff per premises, a trained Halal Team, halal-only declared ingredients, and no pork contact on equipment; restaurant application fees run S$775-1,140 before GST and normal processing is under 14 working days [W4]. CAVA's lamb, chicken and falafel menu makes this achievable; the pork-free kitchen is a feature here.

**Months 3-9 (open the CBD store)**
- Open the CBD store with pre-order pickup and a lunch set that lands inside the working price band. List on GrabFood and foodpanda on day one; after Deliveroo's March 2026 exit they are the only two platforms that matter [W5], with Grab at roughly 69% of the market and foodpanda at 24% [W6].
- Seed Singapore food creators on Instagram and TikTok; English content works as-is, but casting and festive timing (Chinese New Year, Hari Raya, Deepavali) should be local.

**Months 9-18 (second store, regional decision)**
- Open the mall store if the CBD store clears the partner's targets on sales per square foot and delivery mix.
- Decide, with data, which Southeast Asian market comes next. Singapore success does not predict Indonesia or Vietnam.

## 4. Consumer psychology
- **Eating out is the default, and hawker is the benchmark.** In a national study, 77% of adults ate at least one main meal out of home and 61% did so at hawker centres, with lunch the most common meal out; Western fast food was weekly for 71% of 18-21 year olds but only 3% of over-60s [W7]. CAVA is competing with S$5 hawker lunches for the same office worker, so the pitch is protein, freshness and customization, never "cheap".
- **Health concern loses to convenience and price.** The same study found health and hygiene worries were outweighed by convenience and affordability [W7]. Speed and pickup matter more than nutrition copy.
- **Halal widens the market.** The authors suggest limited halal hawker options push Malay diners toward chains [W7]. A certified CAVA has a reason to exist for them.
- **Delivery is a household habit, and people are watching the duopoly.** 64% of households use food delivery and 92% of active users default to GrabFood or foodpanda [W8]. Be on both, but push the app.
- **Shoppers compare globally.** They know what a CAVA bowl costs in the US. Explain the Singapore price in ingredients and rent, or they will do the math for you.

## 5. Brand adaptation
- **Keep:** Crazy Feta, the dips, build-your-own, the hospitality, the digital ordering.
- **Change:** certify halal and say so clearly; add a rice base and a spicier house sauce; smaller lunch portion at a lower entry price with the full bowl above it; festive limited editions across the three big calendars.
- **Add:** group-order bundles for office teams, which is how CBD lunch actually gets bought.
- **Drop:** any pork item, US-only price anchoring in marketing, and American-origin framing. Here CAVA is Mediterranean first.

## 6. Competitive reality
- **Guzman y Gomez:** 24 restaurants, master-franchised, build-your-own, still growing [W2]. The closest format analog and the partner playbook to copy.
- **Stuff'd:** a local kebab, burrito and bowl chain with 36 outlets by 2021 [W9]. It sits exactly where CAVA's pita would land, at lower prices.
- **SaladStop! and the local bowl chains:** SaladStop! is Temasek-backed and operated 69 outlets across eight markets as of its last raise [W10]. The CBD healthy-lunch slot is crowded and loyal.
- **Hawker centres:** the price and habit anchor for most lunches [W7].
- **Mediterranean fast casual at scale:** none confirmed. Independent Greek and Lebanese restaurants exist; no chain owns the category.

## 7. Risks
| Risk | Type | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rent eats the margin | Cost | High | High | Orchard prime rents averaged S$31.90 psf a month in 2Q2026 and recent closures have been mostly F&B [W11]. Negotiate through the partner; prefer upper floors and food halls; short first leases |
| Delivery commissions compress margin | Cost | High | Medium | Grab has said merchant commissions run 25-30% of order value [W12] [verify current]; chains gain more incremental takeout from platforms than independents [W13], so keep delivery prices and pickup incentives disciplined |
| Halal non-compliance or late certification | Regulatory | Medium | High | Start MUIS before menu lock; two Muslim staff and a trained Halal Team per store [W4] |
| Priced as "expensive salad" | Cultural | High | High | Lunch set inside the band; show protein weight and freshness, not health slogans |
| Partner misalignment on brand standards | Operational | Medium | High | Brand standards, training and audit rights written into the master-franchise agreement, as the biggest US chains do abroad [W14] |
| Treating Singapore success as Southeast Asia proof | Strategic | Medium | Medium | Decide the next market from data, not from Singapore sentiment |

## 8. Rewards
- The cheapest real-world lesson in how CAVA's menu, supply chain and brand travel, in English and under strict food rules.
- A halal-certified playbook that opens Malaysia and Indonesia conversations later.
- A partner relationship and regional supplier map that de-risks every later Asian market.

## 9. Validate before you spend
1. Interview three Singapore operators who run a foreign fast-casual brand; ask for real sales per square foot and delivery mix in the CBD.
2. Price-check 10 lunch items at Guzman y Gomez, Stuff'd, SaladStop! and two hawker stalls near Raffles Place.
3. Get a written MUIS pre-assessment on the menu and kitchen layout [verify].
4. Run a two-week Instagram and TikTok creator seed plus a waitlist; set the sign-up threshold first.
5. Model one CBD store at Orchard-level rent, 25-30% delivery commission and the working price band; if it only works at US prices, stop.

**Confidence:** Medium. Strong on route and consumer evidence; weak on unit economics until a Singapore operator shares real numbers.
