# Hero Cosmetics → Singapore

**Brand:** Hero Cosmetics - hydrocolloid acne patches (Mighty Patch) plus a small acne-care range. Friendly, science-backed, fun. Sold in the US through Amazon, Target, Walmart, Ulta and DTC. Owned by Church & Dwight since 2022 [W1]; already in Canada [W2], Australia [W3] and Greece, where a local group placed it exclusively in one beauty chain [W4].
**Customer today:** US teens and twenty-somethings who treat a breakout as a normal, fixable thing.
**Target market:** Singapore

---

## 1. The call
**Go, but this is a deepen plan, not an entry.** Mighty Patch is already on Singapore shelves: Watsons lists the Invisible+ 39-pack at S$24.20, discounted to S$16.94, with Korea as the country of origin [W5]. So the real question is why a brand that is number one on Amazon in the US sits on a Watsons page at 30% off with no story around it. Singapore should become Hero's Southeast Asia showroom: fix the price architecture, turn on creators and TikTok Shop, add a second retail door, and use the results to decide whether Malaysia and Indonesia come next.

## 2. Recommended entry point
- **City:** Singapore, island-wide; the market is one city.
- **Channel:** the existing Watsons listing as the anchor, plus Shopee and TikTok Shop for discovery and a second physical door at a beauty specialist or department-store beauty floor within 12 months. Brand content and creators do the work the shelf cannot.
- **Customer:** women and men aged 16-30 who already buy Korean skincare, plus Malay and Indian shoppers who are underserved by Korea-centric beauty marketing.
- **Price:** hold a list price in the S$20-25 band for the 36-39 packs and stop living at 30% off; add a smaller entry pack near S$10 for first purchase [verify against local shelf].

## 3. Entry strategy
**First 90 days (own what is already there)**
- Audit the Watsons listing: who the importer is, how often it is promoted, and what the review count looks like. Then confirm the HSA cosmetic notification is in the right name and renewed; every cosmetic sold in Singapore must be notified to HSA before sale, renewed yearly, and labelled in English with a full ingredient list and the responsible company [W6].
- Check the claims. Singapore cosmetics may not claim to treat or prevent a medical condition, and products intended for treatment, such as acne creams, are not cosmetics [W6]. "Acne patch" language needs a classification check against HSA's borderline-product guide before any campaign runs [verify].

**Months 3-9 (make the brand visible)**
- Launch on TikTok Shop and Shopee with the peel-off content format and 25 local creators across Chinese, Malay and Indian communities; time pushes to the 9.9 and 11.11 sale days the market already shops around.
- Run an in-store education moment at Watsons: shelf talkers that explain hydrocolloid in one sentence, because the shopper is choosing between Hero, Garnier patches and Korean patches on the same shelf [W5] [W7].

**Months 9-18 (second door, regional read)**
- Add a second retailer once Watsons sell-through and review velocity are up. Then write the Malaysia and Indonesia case from Singapore data, remembering that Singapore does not predict either.

## 4. Consumer psychology
- **They already know the category; they need a reason to pick you.** Singaporeans are well-travelled and brand-literate; being American is not news. The reason to exist is the US review base, the Korean manufacturing quality and a patch that disappears under makeup in a humid climate.
- **Convenience and price beat health talk.** In a national study of Singapore eating habits, health concerns were outweighed by convenience and affordability [W8]; the same instinct runs through beauty. Sell "fix it overnight, invisible by morning," not skin science.
- **Value-conscious despite income.** Platform sale days shape buying; a brand that is absent on 9.9 or 11.11 disappears for a month.
- **Multicultural, not monocultural.** Casting and creators must reflect Chinese, Malay and Indian faces and skin tones; Korea-only cues leave half the market out.
- **Humidity is the product story.** Thin, invisible patches that hold through sweat are a functional claim here, not a lifestyle one.

## 5. Brand adaptation
- **Keep:** the full Mighty Patch range, the friendly tone, the peel-off video format, Korean manufacturing as a quality cue.
- **Change:** list-price discipline instead of permanent discounting; English labels with the HSA-compliant claim set [W6]; creators across three communities; festive campaigns for Chinese New Year, Hari Raya and Deepavali.
- **Add:** a small entry pack and a Surface (body) patch push for the gym and swim crowd.
- **Drop:** any claim that reads as treatment; US retailer references; US-only pricing cues.

## 6. Competitive reality
- **Garnier pimple patches:** on the same Watsons shelf at a mass price point [W7]. They own the first-time buyer unless Hero explains why hydrocolloid thickness and adhesion differ.
- **Korean hydrocolloid patches:** the category's quality reference across Asia, widely sold online and in Watsons. Hero's Korean manufacturing is parity, not advantage.
- **Marketplace private labels on Shopee:** very cheap multipacks. Hero cannot win the price fight; it wins on trust and visibility.

## 7. Risks
| Risk | Type | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claims cross from cosmetic into treatment | Regulatory | Medium | High | HSA classification check and claim rewrite before campaigns; notification renewed yearly in the right name [W6] [verify] |
| Permanent discounting erodes the premium | Cost | High | Medium | Fixed list price, entry pack for trial, promotions only on platform sale days |
| Lost in a crowded shelf | Competitive | High | Medium | Shelf education, creator volume, TikTok Shop presence |
| Distributor underinvests | Operational | Medium | High | Marketing commitments and sell-through targets in the distribution agreement |
| Mistaking Singapore for Southeast Asia | Strategic | Medium | Medium | Separate business cases for Malaysia and Indonesia, each with local halal and pricing checks |
| Parallel imports undercut the listing | Cost | Medium | Low | Monitor Shopee for grey stock; keep the official price competitive |

## 8. Rewards
- A working playbook (pricing, claims, creators) that Hero's parent can hand to distributors across Southeast Asia.
- Church & Dwight already names Hero as an international growth driver [W9]; Singapore is cheap proof that the growth can be brand-led, not just shelf-led.
- A visible Asian presence strengthens the brand story at home, where the pimple-patch category is getting crowded [W1].

## 9. Validate before you spend
1. Pull Watsons sell-through and review data for the last 12 months; find out who the current importer is and what they spend on marketing.
2. Shelf-check Watsons and Shopee for every hydrocolloid patch sold in Singapore; record price per patch.
3. Confirm HSA notification status and get a written claim review from a Singapore regulatory consultant [verify].
4. Run a 30-day TikTok Shop and creator test with a S$10 entry pack; set a conversion threshold first.
5. Interview 10 shoppers aged 18-28 across the three communities about how they chose their last pimple patch.

**Confidence:** Medium-high. The product is already selling here, so the risk is wasted marketing, not a failed entry; the claims question is the one real unknown.
