# CAVA → India

**Brand:** CAVA - fast-casual Mediterranean. Build-your-own bowls, pitas and salads, signature dips like Crazy Feta, digital ordering and pickup. Founded by Greek-American friends; 476 company-owned US restaurants and NYSE-listed since 2023 [W1]. No international stores.
**Customer today:** US office workers and suburban families who want a fresh, customizable meal fast.
**Target market:** India

---

## 1. The call
**Not yet.** India has the appetite: metro, English-speaking, Instagram-native diners who already order healthy bowls on Zomato and Swiggy, and a culture built around chickpeas, flatbread, yogurt and vegetables. What it does not have is room for a US chain with zero international experience to learn on the job. Local delivery-first bowl brands sell a close cousin of CAVA's menu at a fraction of the price, the price-value test is harsher than anywhere else in this set, and a strong Indian franchisor could not make Dunkin' work in 15 years. CAVA should come to India only after Singapore proves its Asia playbook, and then only through a master franchise built around a vegetarian-forward menu in two cities. The work to do now is the research below.

## 2. Recommended entry point (when the time comes)
- **Cities:** Bengaluru first, Gurugram second. Both have the tech-office lunch crowd, delivery density and willingness to pay that CAVA needs.
- **Channel:** master franchise with an Indian operator who already runs a foreign QSR brand; a delivery-led launch from two or three kitchens, with one flagship dine-in store per city for brand proof.
- **Customer:** upper-middle-class professionals aged 24-40 who already order from premium healthy-bowl brands, plus families for weekend dine-in.
- **Price:** working hypothesis of Rs 349-499 per bowl before taxes, to be tested against local bowl brands on Zomato [verify]. Set in rupees from local food cost, never by converting dollars.

## 3. Entry strategy
**Now (before any commitment)**
- Finish the Singapore test and write down what the menu, pricing and partner model taught you.
- Menu engineering for India: build a vegetarian-first board (falafel, roasted vegetables, paneer or a local protein, hummus, Crazy Feta, dips) and check every item against Indian beef and pork sensitivities; remove anything that fails.
- Study the operators: Taco Bell's Indian master franchisee opened its 50th restaurant in 2019 with a stated target of 600 by 2029 [W2]. Find out what that partner would want from CAVA.

**Months 0-9 (if the data says go)**
- Sign the master franchise. Up to 100% foreign investment is allowed in restaurants under the automatic route [W3], but a partner is still the only sensible way to lease, hire and license across cities.
- Apply for FSSAI licensing per premises; a chain operating across more than one state needs a central licence regardless of turnover [W4] [verify current thresholds].
- Open two or three delivery kitchens in Bengaluru on Zomato and Swiggy, with CAVA's app as a loyalty layer, and one flagship store in an office corridor.

**Months 9-18 (prove repeat, then Gurugram)**
- Track repeat rate, average order value and vegetarian mix weekly. Expand to Gurugram only if Bengaluru holds a repeat rate the partner is proud of.

## 4. Consumer psychology
- **Vegetarian is the main course, not the side.** 39% of Indian adults call themselves vegetarian and 81% restrict meat in some way [W5]; careful analysis of national surveys puts strict vegetarianism closer to 20-30%, with huge regional variation [W6]. The menu must be excellent without meat, and beef is off the table.
- **The price-value test is brutal, even for the affluent.** Local D2C and QSR brands have reset expectations. A US price converted to rupees reads as arrogance.
- **Fast food is social and occasional.** A Kerala study found taste drove QSR choice, most customers dined with family, visits were occasional and a typical bill ran Rs 501-1,000 per visit [W7]. Build for groups and weekends in dine-in, and for solo weekday lunches on delivery.
- **Delivery is the storefront.** Zomato and Swiggy dominate ordering, with new entrants such as Rapido's Ownly and magicpin pushing in [W8]. For a new brand, the app listing is the first impression.
- **Localization is table stakes, not a differentiator.** Every foreign chain here adapts its menu. The question is whether the Indian version of CAVA is still worth paying more for.

## 5. Brand adaptation
- **Keep:** Crazy Feta, hummus and the dips, falafel, build-your-own, the warm hospitality.
- **Change:** vegetarian-first menu architecture with clear veg and non-veg marking; spicier sauces; smaller single-serve portions at the entry price; rupee pricing with a value lunch; Hindi plus English copy; Instagram Reels and YouTube only (TikTok is banned in India, so that channel does not exist here).
- **Add:** group and family bundles, festive menus for Diwali and the wedding season, WhatsApp for ordering support.
- **Drop:** beef, pork, US portion sizes, and any claim that leans on "American".

## 6. Competitive reality
- **Delivery-native healthy bowl brands:** Harvest Salad Co. runs 49 cloud kitchens across five cities including Gurugram [W9], and Salad Days operates 25 cloud kitchens in Delhi-NCR, Bengaluru and Mumbai after a Rs 30 crore Series A [W10]. They already own CAVA's lunch occasion, without rent.
- **Foreign QSRs run by Indian master franchisees:** Taco Bell under Burman Hospitality is the model for a US fast casual scaling here [W2]. They set the partner expectations CAVA would face.
- **Earlier Mediterranean entrants:** a Dubai falafel chain launched in India in 2019 with a plan for 100 outlets by 2024 [W11]; check how far it got before assuming the category is open [verify].
- **Local Lebanese and shawarma independents:** cheap, everywhere, and the taste reference for hummus and pita.

## 7. Risks
| Risk | Type | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brand works in the US, dies in India | Strategic | High | High | Jubilant FoodWorks is closing Dunkin' India after 15 years as its development agreement expires at the end of 2026 [W12]. Enter only with a tested menu and an operator who has scaled a foreign brand |
| Price-value rejection | Cultural | High | High | Rupee pricing from local food cost; value lunch; show protein and freshness per rupee |
| FSSAI and labeling gaps across states | Regulatory | Medium | High | Central licence plus per-premises licences; local compliance partner [W4] [verify] |
| Delivery platform dependence | Cost | High | Medium | Platforms add more incremental takeout for chains than independents [W13], but commissions and discounts set the margin; keep the app and pickup alive |
| Imported ingredients (olive oil, tahini, feta) priced out by duties | Cost | Medium | High | Local sourcing for most inputs; import only signature items; quote landed cost before menu lock [verify] |
| Cultural misstep on meat | Cultural | Medium | High | No beef; strict veg and non-veg separation in kitchens and on the board |

## 8. Rewards
- A vegetarian-forward CAVA menu, proven in India, is an asset in every market with large vegetarian and flexitarian populations.
- Bengaluru and Gurugram are dense enough that a working model there scales to Mumbai, Pune and Hyderabad through the same partner.
- Chickpeas, lentils and flatbread are already Indian staples. If CAVA earns trust here, the category ceiling is high.

## 9. Validate before you spend
1. Order and taste 10 bowls from Harvest Salad Co. and Salad Days in Bengaluru; record prices, portion, delivery time and packaging.
2. Interview two Indian master franchisees of foreign QSRs about unit economics, royalty expectations and what they would need from CAVA.
3. Run a 20-person taste panel in Bengaluru on a vegetarian-first CAVA menu; test three price points.
4. Confirm FSSAI licence tiers, import duties on olive oil, tahini and cheese, and GST treatment with a local adviser [verify].
5. Set the go criteria from the Singapore test before anyone books a flight to Bengaluru.

**Confidence:** Medium-high on the call, low on timing. The evidence on diet, price and competition is strong; what India looks like after CAVA has one working international market is the open question.
