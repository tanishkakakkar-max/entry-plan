# CAVA → Japan

## 1. The call  
**Test first, go.** Japan’s lunch‑hour office market is hungry for healthy, quick meals, and the fast‑casual Mediterranean concept fits the “customizable bowl” trend (see fast‑casual trends [W1]). However, Japanese diners research heavily, expect flawless quality, and trust third‑party endorsements more than brand ads. CAVA should enter with a low‑risk pilot in Tokyo through a vetted local restaurant partner, using a small‑format store and a shared commissary kitchen. This will let you prove demand, adapt recipes to local palate, and build the credibility needed for a longer‑term roll‑out.

## 2. Recommended entry point  

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **City** | Tokyo (first in a business‑district such as Marunouchi or Shibuya, where office workers concentrate). |
| **Channel** | Joint‑venture‑style partnership with an established fast‑casual operator that runs a “CAVA‑by‑Partner” store (≈30 sqm). The partner supplies the lease, local staffing, and food‑service licence; CAVA provides the menu, branding, and proprietary dips (Crazy Feta, hummus). |
| **Customer** | Urban office workers (25‑40) and Gen Z lunch‑buyers who value health, customization, and digital ordering. |
| **Pricing** | ¥1,200‑¥1,800 per bowl, positioned between konbini set meals and full‑service Mediterranean restaurants. |

## 3. Entry strategy  

### Phase 1 – 0‑3 months (validation)  
1. **Partner selection:** Identify 1‑2 Japanese fast‑casual operators with existing kitchen space and experience in “build‑your‑own” concepts. Use Tabelog reviews and LINE engagement metrics to shortlist.  
2. **Menu localisation:** Keep core items (Mediterranean bowls, pitas, Crazy Feta) but add two Japan‑only options: a matcha‑infused grain bowl and a miso‑glazed chicken pita. This mirrors the CAVA‑Japan recipe tweak strategy noted in the research.  
3. **Regulatory prep:** Register the imported dip formulas under the Food Sanitation Act; appoint a Japanese “importer of record.” Ensure allergen labels meet the new mandatory list (cashew added 1 Apr 2026) [W6].  
4. **Digital rollout:** Launch a Japanese‑language ordering page on cava.com with JPY pricing, PayPay and konbini payment options, and LINE‑based loyalty coupons.  

**Go/no‑go metric:** Minimum of 300 pre‑orders through the digital waitlist in the first 6 weeks, or ≥70 % repeat orders from the pilot store.

### Phase 2 – 3‑9 months (earn credibility)  
1. **Store launch:** Operate the pilot for 6 weeks, staffed by the partner’s team trained by CAVA chefs (virtual training + 2‑week on‑site stint).  
2. **Supply chain:** Import proprietary dips in bulk to a Tokyo 3PL; source fresh vegetables, grains, and proteins from local wholesale markets to meet Japanese freshness expectations.  
3. **Marketing:** Seed 15‑20 Japanese food creators on Instagram, LINE, and YouTube; have them film “build‑your‑own” videos focusing on ingredient quality and the story behind Crazy Feta. No paid ads initially.  
4. **Feedback loop:** Collect daily sales data, QR‑code surveys, and Tabelog reviews. Adjust seasoning levels (less sweet, more umami) based on feedback.  

### Phase 3 – 9‑24 months (scale)  
1. **Second location:** Open a second store in Osaka (business district) using the same partner model, leveraging lessons from Tokyo.  
2. **DTC & marketplace:** After 12 months, add a cross‑border DTC option (Shopify Japan) for “CAVA at home” kits (pre‑portioned veggies + dip), priced at a premium with no early discounting.  
3. **Loyalty program:** Integrate LINE official account for point‑based rewards tied to repeat lunch purchases; align with the high‑usage of LINE among office workers (≈96 M users [Research]).  

## 4. Consumer psychology  

* **Feel > values:** Japanese diners care more about taste, texture, and freshness than “clean‑eating” slogans. Emphasize the crispness of the vegetables, the creaminess of the dip, and the Mediterranean provenance on every menu board.  
* **Proof via third parties:** A positive Tabelog rating and a feature in a local lifestyle magazine (e.g., “Brunch”) will outweigh any brand‑owned ad.  
* **Risk‑averse ordering:** Provide clear Japanese‑language nutrition facts, ingredient lists, and a hassle‑free return/complaint process on the website and in‑store.  
* **Seasonality matters:** Rotate one seasonal bowl each quarter (e.g., sakura‑cherry rice bowl in spring) to match Japan's seasonal menus.  

## 5. Brand adaptation  

| Keep | Change | Drop |
|---|---|---|
| Core Mediterranean menu, Crazy Feta dip, “build‑your‑own” format, health‑first positioning. | Add matcha grain bowl, miso‑glazed protein, local vegetable suppliers; rename “Crazy Feta” to a Japanese‑friendly phonetic (クレイジーフェタ) with subtitle “Greek cheese spread”. | US‑centric copy (“fresh from the Greek coast”) that sounds generic; any “American‑style” promotional slang; heavy reliance on Instagram‑only visuals (add LINE). |
| Visual identity: bright colors, clean layout, modern typography. | Use softer pastel background tones that align with Japanese café aesthetics; include QR‑code menus. | Large‑format US TV ads. |

## 6. Competitive reality  

* **Domestic fast‑casual bowl and salad chains:** already serve quick, health‑focused meals; CAVA must differentiate via authentic Mediterranean flavors and proprietary dips.  
* **International Mediterranean entrants:** limited presence; CAVA’s scale and dip portfolio are a clear advantage.  
* **Convenience‑store set meals (7‑Eleven, Lawson):** price benchmark (≈¥800‑¥1,100); CAVA’s price point must be justified through premium ingredients and customization.  

## 7. Risks  

| Risk | Type | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Import duty & labeling compliance** | Regulatory | Medium | High | Use a Japanese importer of record; label dips per Food Sanitation Act and new allergen list (cashew) [W6]; verify tariff rates for dip HS codes for US-origin goods [W5] [verify]. |
| **Menu mis‑fit (too foreign taste)** | Cultural | Medium | High | Conduct taste‑testing panels during Phase 1; incorporate Japanese‑style seasoning (miso, matcha) as limited items. |
| **Partner misalignment (brand dilution)** | Operational | Low | High | Draft a joint‑branding agreement that mandates CAVA’s visual standards, staff training, and quality audits. |
| **Digital payment friction** | Operational | Medium | Medium | Offer PayPay, konbini, credit cards; integrate LINE Pay to match local habits. |
| **Negative Tabelog reviews early on** | Reputation | High | High | Implement rigorous QC, prep staff on plating standards, and respond within 24 h to any complaint via LINE. |
| **Currency volatility (yen swings)** | Financial | Medium | Medium | Price bowls in JPY based on landed‑cost forecasts; include a 5 % buffer for yen fluctuations. |

## 8. Rewards  

* **First‑mover credibility** in the Japanese fast‑casual Mediterranean niche; a successful Tokyo pop‑up will generate media buzz and make future partner negotiations easier.  
* **Scalable supply model** – once the dip formulas are approved, bulk import reduces per‑unit cost, enabling expansion to other Asian markets with similar EPA benefits.  
* **Brand equity lift** – Japanese quality endorsement (e.g., Tabelog 4‑star rating) can be leveraged in US marketing to position CAVA as a globally trusted health brand.  

## 9. Validate before you spend  

1. **Regulatory checklist:** Confirm dip HS code duty rate for US-origin goods and ensure allergen labeling meets the April 2026 update [W5][W6].  
2. **Partner due diligence:** Interview at least three potential restaurant operators; evaluate their Tabelog scores, staff turnover, and existing kitchen capacity.  
3. **Taste test:** Run a blind tasting with 20 Tokyo office workers comparing core CAVA bowls vs. a local competitor; record preference and perceived price acceptability.  
4. **Digital prototype:** Launch a Japanese‑language landing page with a “pre‑order waitlist” and measure sign‑up conversion; aim for ≥300 sign‑ups in 4 weeks.  
5. **Financial model:** Build a landed‑cost spreadsheet incorporating import duties, 3PL fees, and a 5 % yen‑volatility buffer; verify that the ¥1,200‑¥1,800 price yields ≥30 % gross margin.  

**Confidence:** Medium‑high. Consumer demand for healthy, customizable lunches is strong, but success hinges on flawless regulatory compliance, local taste adaptation, and credible partner execution.
