# CAVA → Japan

**Brand:** CAVA - fast-casual Mediterranean. Build-your-own bowls, pitas and salads, signature dips like Crazy Feta, digital ordering and pickup. Founded by Greek-American friends; 476 company-owned US restaurants and NYSE-listed since 2023 [W1]. No international stores.
**Customer today:** US office workers and suburban families who want a fresh, customizable meal fast.
**Target market:** Japan

---

## 1. The call
**Test first, through a partner.** Tokyo office lunch is a real opening for CAVA: time-poor workers, a convenience-store price anchor that leaves room above it, and a proven appetite for app-first, customizable bowls. But CAVA has never run a store outside the US, its own capital is committed to 75-77 US openings a year [W1], and Japan punishes half-attention. The honest route is a Japanese operating partner who leases, hires and licenses, with CAVA supplying the menu, the dips and the standards. Prove two Tokyo stores before anyone says the word "rollout".

## 2. Recommended entry point
- **City:** Tokyo only. Marunouchi, Otemachi or Shibuya, where office lunch demand is dense and app pickup is already a habit.
- **Channel:** One or two small-format, pickup-heavy stores run by a local partner under a license or master-franchise agreement, backed by a shared commissary. Not a hotel tie-up, not a food-court trial without a partner.
- **Customer:** workers aged 25-45 who already pay ¥1,000-plus for a healthy lunch at a salad chain, plus their teams for group orders. Not tourists.
- **Price:** ¥1,200-1,800 per bowl, above konbini and below sit-down, as scoped in the original CAVA Japan project. Set it in yen from landed cost, not by converting dollars.

## 3. Entry strategy
**First 90 days (find the partner, fix the rules)**
- Shortlist operators who have already run a US fast-casual or coffee brand in Japan. Shake Shack entered Tokyo in 2015 with Sazaby League, the group behind Starbucks Japan, and deliberately planned only about ten stores by 2020 to protect quality [W2]. That is the partner profile and the pace to copy.
- Appoint an importer of record and file the food import notification with the MHLW quarantine station for every shipment of dips, tahini, olive oil and spice blends; without the Certificate of Notification the goods cannot be sold [W3]. Secure the restaurant business licence and a food hygiene manager through the local health centre [W4].
- Run three blind taste panels with Tokyo office workers on sweetness, portion and salt before the menu is final.

**Months 3-9 (open one store, earn Tabelog)**
- Open store one in an office tower or food hall near Marunouchi or Shibuya with app pre-order and self-checkout, the format Crisp Salad Works uses across its 55 Tokyo-area stores [W5].
- Launch a LINE official account for coupons and a Japanese-language ordering page with PayPay and konbini payment. Seed 15-20 Japanese food creators on Instagram; no paid ads until reviews exist.
- Import the signature ingredients, buy produce and proteins locally, and hold stock at a Tokyo commissary with small daily deliveries, because urban stores have almost no storage.

**Months 9-18 (second store, then decide)**
- Open store two only if store one hits the partner's unit economics and holds a Tabelog score you would show investors.
- Add delivery through Uber Eats Japan and Demae-can last, not first. Delivery is where Japanese operators are losing money [W6].

## 4. Consumer psychology
- **They want the real CAVA, not a Japanese costume.** A 2022 interview study found Japanese customers valued McDonald's primarily as a global brand and gave little weight to its localization efforts [W7]. Keep the core unmistakably CAVA; localize the edges.
- **Lunch is a time and value problem.** In a 2023 Asahi survey, 56% of workers said they could not take a full lunch break and 52% bought lunch at a convenience store at least weekly, with "value for money" the top priority [W8]. Pre-order and a two-minute pickup are the product, not a feature.
- **They research first and judge online.** Tabelog, Instagram and LINE decide the first visit; people are reserved in person and candid online. Monitor reviews weekly and answer in Japanese within a day.
- **Mediterranean already means health and longevity here.** Sell the category's local meaning, not the American origin.
- **Rice and seasonality are expected.** A rice base and a rotating seasonal bowl are not extras; their absence is a reason to skip you.

## 5. Brand adaptation
- **Keep:** Crazy Feta, the dips, the spice blends, build-your-own, the warm hospitality, the app.
- **Change:** add curated set bowls next to build-your-own (choice paralysis is real for a first visit); cut sweetness in sauces and desserts; add a rice base; Japanese-language menu, nutrition and allergen labels; smaller formats with more takeout; packaging to Japanese standard.
- **Add:** Japan-only items from the original project, such as matcha drinks, miso-glazed vegetables and a less-sweet yuzu dessert, released as seasonal limited editions.
- **Drop:** "American import" framing, superlatives like "best" or "#1", and US portion sizes as the default.

## 6. Competitive reality
- **Crisp Salad Works:** 55 stores, app pre-order, takeout-first, weekday office trade [W5]. It owns the exact lunch slot CAVA wants. CAVA's edge is warm proteins, dips and flavor; its disadvantage is zero local trust.
- **Guzman y Gomez:** a build-your-own Mexican fast casual that has run in Tokyo since 2015 through a master-franchise partner and is still opening stores [W9]. Proof that the format travels here when a partner runs it.
- **Convenience stores:** the price anchor for every lunch decision. CAVA cannot win on price; it wins on freshness, protein and customization.
- **Mediterranean fast casual at scale:** none confirmed in Tokyo. Independent Greek and Turkish restaurants exist, but no chain owns the category yet.

## 7. Risks
| Risk | Type | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wrong partner, slow stall | Operational | Medium | High | Carl's Jr. sat at four Japanese stores under a non-restaurant parent before the rights were handed to another operator in 2024 [W10]. Choose a food-service group with a US brand track record and shared targets |
| Import non-compliance on dips and spices | Regulatory | Medium | High | Importer of record, MHLW notification per shipment, Japanese allergen and additive labels checked before the first container [W3] [verify] |
| Delivery drags margins | Cost | High | Medium | Wolt left Japan in 2026 and Demae-can expects an eighth straight annual loss [W6]; Uber Eats is pushing store-price parity [W11]. Build on pickup first; treat delivery as marketing |
| Street-level rent in prime Tokyo | Cost | High | Medium | Prime 1F asking rents in Shibuya ran about ¥45,500 per tsubo in early 2026 [W12]. Use upper floors, office-tower food halls and the partner's lease relationships [W13] |
| Taste misfit on first visit | Cultural | Medium | High | Taste panels, curated sets, less sugar, rice base |
| Yen swings move landed cost | Cost | Medium | Medium | Price in yen from landed cost with a buffer; source perishables locally |

## 8. Rewards
- A Tokyo store that Japanese diners rate well is the most credible proof CAVA could carry into the rest of Asia, and into its US story.
- Chain restaurants gain more from delivery platforms than independents do, mainly through incremental takeout [W14], so once pickup trust exists, delivery can add volume cheaply.
- The partner's leases, hiring and regulatory know-how transfer when CAVA later decides whether to go independent.

## 9. Validate before you spend
1. Interview three Japanese operators who have run a US food brand here; ask what killed earlier US entrants. Taco Bell and Carl's Jr. both left Japan in the 1980s after failing to build recognition [W15].
2. Price-check 10 lunch items in yen at Crisp Salad Works, Guzman y Gomez Tokyo and two konbini near your target block.
3. Quote landed cost for one container of dips and spice blends, including notification, duties and a Tokyo commissary [verify].
4. Run a Japanese-language waitlist and a two-week creator seed; set a sign-up number in advance and respect it.
5. Hold the three taste panels before you sign a lease.

**Confidence:** Medium. High on route and consumer logic, backed by the original CAVA Japan work; low on partner availability and unit economics until a Japanese operator shows real numbers.
