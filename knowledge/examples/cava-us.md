# CAVA → United States

**Brand:** CAVA - fast-casual Mediterranean. Build-your-own bowls, pitas and salads, signature dips like Crazy Feta, digital ordering and pickup. Founded by Greek-American friends; NYSE-listed since 2023. No international stores.
**Customer today:** US office workers and suburban families who want a fresh, customizable meal fast.
**Target market:** United States (home market)

---

## 1. The call
**Go, and keep deepening.** This is not an entry plan. CAVA is already the category leader at home: 500 restaurants in 30 states and D.C. as of September 2026, ahead of its own schedule toward 1,000 by 2032 [W1], with same-restaurant sales up 9.0% on 5.3% traffic growth in the latest quarter [W2]. The question for the next five years is not whether to be in the US but how to fill the map without becoming Sweetgreen: a coastal, lunch-only, overpriced salad brand whose comparable sales fell for a full year [W3]. The plan is density in the metros you have, careful entry into the ones you don't, a dinner and family habit, and a hard line on price.

## 2. Recommended entry point
- **Where next:** finish the Midwest you started (Chicago in 2024 [W4], then Cincinnati, St. Louis, Columbus and Minneapolis in 2026 [W2] [W1]) and treat the remaining 20 states as a 2027-2030 sequence, one metro cluster at a time. Never one store in a new state.
- **Channel:** company-owned restaurants of 2,000-3,000 square feet with 30-60 seats [W5], digital pickup lanes, and catering. No franchising; that is how you keep the standards that earned the comps.
- **Customer:** keep the office-lunch core, and grow the suburban family dinner occasion, where a bowl replaces a drive-through meal rather than a salad.
- **Price:** hold the line. You have kept menu price increases below inflation while peers raised prices and lost traffic [W6]. That discipline is the moat.

## 3. Entry strategy
**Next 90 days (lock the playbook)**
- Write down what made Chicago and the 2026 Midwest entries work: number of sites signed before opening one, local hiring lead time, awareness spend, time to break even. That document is the real estate thesis for every new metro.
- Protect throughput. Digital was 37.9% of revenue in fiscal 2025 [W5]; every new unit needs a pickup flow that does not slow the walk-up line.

**Months 3-9 (density first)**
- Add units in existing metros until each market can share a regional supply hub, local marketing and a bench of trained general managers.
- Grow dinner with bundles for two and four and with the loyalty tiers launched in 2025 [W5]. Measure dinner mix per store, not just sales.
- Keep the grocery dips business as a brand billboard in markets you have not entered yet [W5].

**Months 9-18 (new metros, in clusters)**
- Enter each new region with three to five signed sites, a trainer team, and a local creator plan, the way the Minneapolis entry was announced with two Twin Cities sites at once [W1].
- Say no to the one-off airport or stadium deal that puts a weak store in a city you have not committed to.

## 4. Consumer psychology
- **Health is the reason to try; flavor and value are the reasons to return.** Research on adopting a Mediterranean diet finds cost, unfamiliar ingredients and not knowing how to prepare pulses are the main barriers, while taste, pleasure and ready availability in catering outlets are the facilitators [W7]. CAVA removes the barriers; make sure the menu board sells the flavor, not the diet.
- **Americans buy people and stories.** The founders' story, told in long form on How I Built This, is a channel in itself [W8]. Keep founder and crew faces in content, not stock photography.
- **New regions have less Mediterranean fluency.** A first-time Midwest guest does not know what harissa is. Curated bowls with plain-English descriptions outperform build-your-own on a first visit.
- **Convenience expectations are set by Amazon.** Pickup, delivery and catering are table stakes, and delivery platforms add more incremental takeout for chains than for independents [W9]. Use them for volume; keep the app for loyalty.
- **Value is judged against Chipotle, not against a salad.** Protein portion and visible freshness per dollar decide repeat.

## 5. Brand adaptation
- **Keep:** Crazy Feta and the dips, build-your-own, the warm Mediterranean hospitality, the founders' voice, the pace of 75-77 openings a year [W2].
- **Change:** menu descriptions written for first-timers in new regions; dinner-first merchandising after 4 pm; store design tuned for families (more four-tops, kids' options) in suburban trade areas.
- **Add:** a protein-forward dinner platform (your CEO has already said a seafood item is coming [W10]) and catering sales teams in metros with corporate density.
- **Drop:** any temptation to franchise domestically, to raise prices to protect margin, or to chase a one-store presence in a new state.

## 6. Competitive reality
- **Chipotle:** the scale benchmark at more than 3,700 units and the only fast casual that has proven a company-owned model nationwide; its international growth runs through development partners such as Alsea and Alshaya [W11]. It sets the value and throughput bar you are judged against.
- **Sweetgreen:** 281 units, comparable sales down 11.5% in Q4 2025, openings cut to 15 for 2026, and an admission that it was seen as overpriced [W3]. The cautionary case, not the competitor.
- **Regional Mediterranean and halal fast casuals:** present in many metros, usually without digital scale or a dip business. Watch them for menu ideas, not for market share.
- **Foreign entrants:** Guzman y Gomez, a strong Australian fast casual, exited the US in May 2026 after its Chicago restaurants missed targets, taking a US$30-40 million hit [W12]. The US rewards density and punishes sub-scale experiments, including yours if you spread thin.

## 7. Risks
| Risk | Type | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comps slow as new units cannibalize old ones | Operational | Medium | High | Track transfer sales per new opening; space density by trade-area data, not by ambition |
| Price creep turns CAVA into a lunch luxury | Cultural/competitive | Medium | High | Sweetgreen's path [W3]; keep increases below inflation [W6]; publish protein portion standards internally |
| Imported inputs hit by tariffs | Cost | Medium | Medium | The US tariff regime changed repeatedly in 2025-2026 [verify current rates with a broker]; your CFO has said most of the supply chain is domestic [W13]; lock multi-source contracts for olive oil, tahini and feta |
| Labor and GM bench cannot keep up with 75-plus openings | Operational | High | High | Hire and train managers a market ahead; slow a metro before you slow the quality |
| Delivery and third-party fees compress margin | Cost | High | Medium | Platforms grow chain takeout [W9]; steer repeat orders to the app with loyalty tiers [W5] |
| Dinner never becomes a habit | Strategic | Medium | Medium | Dinner bundles, family seating, protein launches; measure dinner mix monthly |

## 8. Rewards
- Filling the remaining 20 states with the same unit economics is the path to the 1,000-restaurant goal and past it [W1].
- A dinner habit roughly doubles the usable hours of every store you already pay rent on.
- Dominance at home is also what makes any future international test credible to a partner. The Asia plans in this set assume CAVA earns that right here first.

## 9. Validate before you spend
1. For the next three new metros, write the entry criteria in one page before site selection starts: daytime population, lunch competitors, median income, distance to a supply hub, and the minimum number of signed sites.
2. Run a dinner test in 20 suburban stores: bundles, protein launch, family seating. Set the dinner-mix target first.
3. Hold a price-perception study in two mature and two new markets against Chipotle and a local salad chain.
4. Model tariff scenarios for imported ingredients with a customs broker [verify].
5. Listen again to the escape-velocity advice from your own CEO: "what you say no to is usually more important than what you say yes to" [W14]. Write the list of nos for 2027.

**Confidence:** High on direction, because it is working; medium on the dinner and new-region bets, which the data has not proven yet.
