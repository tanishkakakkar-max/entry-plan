# Kalo Ceramics → Singapore

**Brand:** Kalo Ceramics (fictional) - handmade stoneware tableware made in Porto, Portugal. Slow, crafted, quiet earth-tone glazes. Premium, about $30-60 a piece. Sells in Portugal and the EU through DTC, Etsy and 12 concept stores.
**Customer today:** design-minded couples and young professionals, 28-45, who buy a few good pieces rather than a set.
**Target market:** Singapore

---

## 1. The call
**Test first, cheaply, and treat it as a Southeast Asia showroom.** Singapore is the lowest-friction market in this set for a European tableware brand: English content works as-is, most goods including ceramics enter duty-free, and Portuguese tableware already has a reference price on local shelves through Costa Nova at a lifestyle store [W1]. It is also small, rent is brutal, and its best-known design retailer collapsed owing its brands money [W2]. So the test is cross-border DTC plus one stockist on firm wholesale terms, aimed at wedding and housewarming gifts. If it works, Singapore is the proof that opens Hong Kong and Kuala Lumpur conversations. If it does not, the cost was a few shipments.

## 2. Recommended entry point
- **City:** Singapore, one market.
- **Channel:** cross-border DTC in Singapore dollars with duties and GST shown at checkout, plus one design or lifestyle stockist on wholesale (not consignment), plus a short pop-up at a wedding or home fair.
- **Customer:** couples 28-40 furnishing a first flat and friends buying them a gift; expats and returning Singaporeans who already know Portuguese ceramics.
- **Price:** working band of S$45-90 a piece, positioned above the Costa Nova lines sold locally from S$25-38 [W1] and in line with local studio pottery [verify].

## 3. Entry strategy
**First 90 days (set up the cheap test)**
- Switch on Singapore in the existing DTC store with SGD pricing, and open Etsy shipping to Singapore; 26% of Etsy's marketplace sales already come from buyers outside the US, so the storefront Kalo runs today reaches this market with no new build [W3]. Check whether the GST rules for low-value imported goods require overseas-vendor registration at Kalo's Singapore sales volume; GST is 9% and applies to imported goods sold to consumers above the registration thresholds [verify with IRAS].
- Shelf-check the local tableware scene: Costa Nova at House of AnLi from S$25-38, local studios like Padme Hum at roughly S$50 a plate, and mass sets at Table Matters around S$135 for 20 pieces [W1]. Set the SGD price from that, not from euros.
- Shortlist two stockists that buy wholesale. Naiise, once the biggest platform for local designers, sold on consignment with a 30-40% commission and closed its last store in 2021 after years of late payments to brands [W2]. Firm orders only.

**Months 3-9 (one stockist, one pop-up, creators)**
- Place a wholesale order with one stockist and run a weekend pop-up at a home or wedding fair timed before a festive peak (Chinese New Year, Hari Raya, Deepavali, Christmas).
- Seed 10 pieces with Singapore home and food creators on Instagram and TikTok; ask for table-setting content, not unboxing.
- Add a gift registry option on the DTC site; weddings and housewarmings are the occasion.

**Months 9-18 (regional read)**
- If repeat orders and gift purchases appear, use the data to approach one stockist each in Hong Kong and Kuala Lumpur. Singapore does not predict those markets, but it does prove the pitch.

## 4. Consumer psychology
- **Shoppers are global and compare prices.** They know what a Kalo plate costs in Lisbon or on Etsy. Show the SGD price with duties and GST built in, and explain the difference in shipping and breakage protection, or they will do the math against you.
- **Foreign is not a novelty.** The market already stocks Portuguese, Japanese and local handmade ceramics [W1]. Kalo's reason to exist is Porto specifically, the glaze palette, and the gift story.
- **Handmade means love, especially for gifts to close people.** Research on the handmade effect shows consumers see handmade products as carrying the maker's love, strongest for gifts for loved ones [W4]. Weddings and housewarmings are where that lands.
- **Small homes, fewer pieces.** Most Singaporeans live in flats with compact kitchens; sell pairs and small sets, not twelve-piece services.
- **Promotions shape the calendar.** Sale days and festive peaks drive buying; a premium brand should show up with gift sets, not discounts.

## 5. Brand adaptation
- **Keep:** Porto origin, earth-tone glazes, the slow and quiet brand voice, handmade process.
- **Change:** SGD pricing with landed cost included; care notes for humid climates and dishwasher use; creators and styling that reflect Chinese, Malay and Indian tables; gift packaging to Asian wedding standard.
- **Add:** a wedding and housewarming registry; a two-piece gift box; festive-season drops.
- **Drop:** euro prices on the site, "sustainable" as a headline, and any consignment deal.

## 6. Competitive reality
- **Costa Nova and other Portuguese lines at lifestyle stores:** already sold locally from S$25-38 a piece [W1]. They set what "Portuguese ceramics" costs here; Kalo must justify the premium with the handmade and small-batch story.
- **Local studio pottery (Padme Hum, Suterwala's, Suns):** handmade, local, with classes and community around them [W1]. They own authenticity and proximity.
- **Mass online tableware (Table Matters and marketplace sellers):** full sets at low prices [W1]. Not Kalo's buyer, but the price anchor for a first flat.
- **Japanese craft ceramics:** also present through importers like KURIĒTO [W1]; they compete for the same design-minded shelf space.

## 7. Risks
| Risk | Type | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rent and consignment terms eat the margin | Cost | High | High | Prime Orchard retail rents averaged S$31.90 psf a month in 2Q2026 and recent closures were mostly F&B and small retail [W5]. No own store; wholesale or firm orders only; pop-ups over leases; Naiise is the warning [W2] |
| GST or import handling surprises at checkout | Regulatory | Medium | Medium | Check overseas-vendor GST registration thresholds with IRAS; show landed price up front [verify] |
| Breakage in transit | Operational | Medium | Medium | Bulk ship to a Singapore 3PL for the stockist and pop-up; parcel only for DTC with insured packaging |
| Priced against Costa Nova and local studios | Competitive | High | Medium | Position as small-batch and gift; shelf-check prices before launch [W1] |
| Treating Singapore as Southeast Asia | Strategic | Medium | Medium | One market at a time; separate cases for Hong Kong and Kuala Lumpur |
| Creative that ignores the multicultural market | Cultural | Medium | Low | Three-community styling and festive calendar from day one |

## 8. Rewards
- A cheap, English-language proof that Kalo's pitch works outside Europe, with real gift-season data.
- A stockist relationship and 3PL that can serve the region later.
- Wedding and housewarming gifting creates repeat buyers who did not buy for themselves first.

## 9. Validate before you spend
1. Shelf-check 10 tableware pieces across House of AnLi, two local studios and Table Matters; record SGD prices [W1].
2. Confirm GST and overseas-vendor registration obligations for Kalo's expected Singapore sales [verify].
3. Pitch two wholesale stockists; the test is a firm order, not a consignment offer.
4. Run a 30-day Instagram and TikTok creator seed pointing to the SGD store; measure gift-related orders.
5. Quote insured parcel and bulk shipping from Porto to Singapore and compare landed cost per piece.

**Confidence:** Medium. Low cost to test and a clear gifting logic; stockist appetite and the landed price against Costa Nova are the open questions.
