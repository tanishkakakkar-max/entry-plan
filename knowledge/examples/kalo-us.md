# Kalo Ceramics → United States

**Brand:** Kalo Ceramics (fictional) - handmade stoneware tableware made in Porto, Portugal. Slow, crafted, quiet earth-tone glazes. Premium, about $30-60 a piece. Sells in Portugal and the EU through DTC, Etsy and 12 concept stores.
**Customer today:** design-minded couples and young professionals, 28-45, who buy a few good pieces rather than a set.
**Target market:** United States

---

## 1. The call
**Go, through wholesale to independent boutiques first.** The US already buys Portuguese ceramics: Portugal exported about US$100.7 million of ceramic products to the United States in 2025 [W1], and American buyers know the category. Kalo's Etsy store already reaches US shoppers, who account for 74% of Etsy's marketplace sales [W2]. The missing piece is not demand; it is a way to sell $30-60 plates to Americans without losing the margin to parcel shipping and breakage. The answer is Faire, the wholesale marketplace built for exactly this brand and buyer, feeding 20-30 independent home and gift boutiques, with a US 3PL and a DTC site in dollars behind it. Pick one region, not "America."

## 2. Recommended entry point
- **Region:** the Northeast corridor (Boston to Washington) first, where independent home boutiques, wedding registries and design press concentrate; California second.
- **Channel:** Faire for wholesale to independents, Etsy and DTC for consumers, with a US 3PL holding stock so neither channel ships from Porto. No big-box retail in the first 18 months.
- **Customer:** couples 28-45 registering for weddings or furnishing a first home, and the gift buyers around them; shoppers who already follow a handful of American studio potteries.
- **Price:** $30-60 a piece, as today, but set from US landed cost including the 15% tariff on EU goods [W3] [verify], 3PL fees and free-shipping thresholds; wholesale at roughly half of retail.

## 3. Entry strategy
**First 90 days (compliance, 3PL, Faire)**
- Confirm glaze compliance. The FDA sets limits on leachable lead and cadmium in ceramic ware; California goes further, bans sales above those limits, requires Proposition 65 warnings above safe-harbor levels, and requires tableware containing lead or cadmium to carry a permanent maker's or importer's mark [W4]. Test the glazes to US and California methods before the first container.
- Model the tariff. The US and EU agreed a framework in 2025 capping tariffs on most EU goods at 15% [W3]; confirm with a customs broker how it applies to HS 6912 and whether it replaces or stacks on the normal duty [verify].
- Open a Faire storefront. Faire reports more than 100,000 brands and 700,000 independent retailers, pays brands up front while giving retailers net-60 terms, and takes a 15% commission on standard orders, with 0% on a new retailer's first order brought in through Faire Direct [W5]. Convert the 12 EU concept stores into the proof points on the listing.

**Months 3-9 (region, registry, creators)**
- Target 30 independent boutiques in the Northeast through Faire; aim for 10 stockists and two reorders.
- Ship a first bulk order to a US 3PL and switch Etsy and DTC fulfillment to domestic shipping with a free-shipping threshold.
- Build a wedding registry page and seed 15 American home and food creators with table-setting content; registry season and holiday gifting are the two peaks.

**Months 9-18 (second region, then decide on retail)**
- Open California through Faire once the Northeast reorders. Decide on a national chain only if independents prove the price and the glazes hold up to American dishwashers and reviews.

## 4. Consumer psychology
- **Handmade means love, and Americans buy it for weddings.** Research on the handmade effect shows consumers see handmade products as containing the maker's love, with the strongest effect for gifts to close relationships [W6]. Registries are the channel built on that feeling.
- **Convenience is set by Amazon.** Slow cross-border shipping kills conversion; a US 3PL and clear delivery dates are the price of entry.
- **Story and specificity win.** Shoppers are skeptical of vague claims; "stoneware fired in Porto, glaze mixed by hand, dishwasher safe" beats "sustainable" or "artisan."
- **They compare with American studio potteries.** East Fork, founded in 2009 in Asheville with its own factory, built a cult following through limited color drops that sell out quickly [W7]. Kalo's Porto origin and quieter palette are a different proposition, not a cheaper version.
- **Reviews and social proof decide.** Faire reviews from retailers and Etsy reviews from buyers are the trust layer; seed both before paid ads.

## 5. Brand adaptation
- **Keep:** Porto origin, earth-tone glazes, the slow and quiet voice, the handmade process, the $30-60 price band.
- **Change:** dollar pricing from landed cost; US-standard care claims (dishwasher, microwave) tested and stated; packaging engineered for US parcel networks; a permanent maker's mark if any glaze contains lead or cadmium [W4].
- **Add:** a wedding registry, a gift box, a two-piece "starter pair," and Faire-ready wholesale line sheets.
- **Drop:** euro prices, shipping from Porto per order, and "sustainable" as a headline; American shoppers have heard it from everyone.

## 6. Competitive reality
- **East Fork and American studio potteries:** the aspirational set; East Fork's own factory, limited drops and cult community set the bar for storytelling, with reviewers noting that non-core items are "pretty steep" [W7]. Kalo competes on origin and price restraint, not on hype.
- **Portuguese factory brands already in the US:** the category Kalo's buyers already know, supported by Portugal's US$100 million-plus annual ceramics exports to the US [W1]. They set the "Portuguese plate" price; Kalo's handmade, small-batch story must justify sitting above it.
- **Etsy makers:** thousands of US potters selling direct; Kalo's advantage is consistency and a wholesale channel they lack [W2].
- **Mass home retailers:** full sets at low prices; not Kalo's buyer.

## 7. Risks
| Risk | Type | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glaze fails FDA or California limits, or lacks the maker's mark | Regulatory | Medium | High | Test to US methods before shipping; permanent mark; Prop 65 review [W4] [verify] |
| Tariff and landed cost squeeze the $30-60 band | Cost | High | High | Model the 15% EU tariff and broker fees per piece [W3] [verify]; bulk import to a 3PL; adjust assortment toward higher-margin pieces |
| Breakage and returns in US parcel networks | Operational | High | Medium | Domestic fulfillment from the 3PL; packaging tested to drop standards; insured shipping |
| Wholesale margin leaves too little | Cost | Medium | Medium | Faire's 15% commission plus wholesale pricing modelled before launch [W5]; minimum order values |
| Spreading nationally too early | Strategic | Medium | High | One region, 30 boutiques, reorders before the next region |
| Lost among Etsy makers | Competitive | Medium | Low | Lead with Porto, consistency and reviews; use Etsy for discovery, DTC for repeat [W2] |

## 8. Rewards
- The US is the largest market for Portuguese ceramics outside Europe [W1], and independents plus registries are a scalable, low-capital route into it.
- A Faire reorder base is proof that national home retailers respect; it opens that conversation on Kalo's terms.
- Dollar revenue diversifies Kalo away from a single European season and currency.

## 9. Validate before you spend
1. Test three glazes to FDA and California methods and confirm whether a permanent maker's mark is required [W4] [verify].
2. Get a customs broker's landed-cost quote on one pallet to a Northeast 3PL, including the 15% EU tariff [W3] [verify].
3. Launch the Faire listing and pitch 30 Northeast boutiques; the test is 10 stockists and two reorders in 90 days [W5].
4. Price-check 10 pieces from East Fork and two Portuguese factory brands sold in the US [W7].
5. Switch Etsy to domestic shipping from the 3PL and measure the conversion change [W2].

**Confidence:** Medium-high. Demand and channels are documented; landed cost after tariffs and the breakage rate in US parcels are the two numbers that decide the margin.
