# Hero Cosmetics → United States

**Brand:** Hero Cosmetics - hydrocolloid acne patches (Mighty Patch) plus a small acne-care range. Friendly, science-backed, fun. Sold in the US through Amazon, Target, Walmart, Ulta and DTC. Owned by Church & Dwight since its $630 million acquisition in 2022 [W1].
**Customer today:** US teens and twenty-somethings who treat a breakout as a normal, fixable thing.
**Target market:** United States (home market)

---

## 1. The call
**Go, and deepen before the category commoditizes.** Hero is not entering the US; it built the US pimple-patch category. At acquisition it was the number two acne brand with 14% share and held 63% of the patch subcategory, yet its distribution was only 8% ACV against 99% for acne treatments overall, and Church & Dwight's stated goal was 80-90% [W1]. That gap is the plan. The next three years are about getting Mighty Patch into every store that sells acne wash, owning the TikTok Shop shelf where beauty is now bought, and widening from "patch" to "acne care" before cheaper patches turn the hero product into a commodity.

## 2. Recommended entry point
- **Where:** national distribution breadth, with the open white space in drug, grocery and convenience, not more depth in the beauty specialists that already carry Hero.
- **Channel:** keep Amazon as the volume engine and review base, add TikTok Shop as the discovery engine, and use the parent's sales force to open mass and drug doors that an independent brand could not.
- **Customer:** keep the Gen Z core; add the two adjacent buyers who already use hydrocolloid at home, parents buying for teens and adults 30-45 with occasional breakouts.
- **Price:** hold the premium. The original Amazon price of $12.99 was the accident that unlocked Prime eligibility and sold out in 90 days [W2]; discounting into private-label territory would undo the brand.

## 3. Entry strategy
**First 90 days (lock the regulatory footing)**
- Confirm how each product is classified. Under MoCRA, cosmetic manufacturers must register facilities and a responsible person must list each product, with registrations renewed every two years and listings updated yearly; products regulated as drugs or devices are exempt from the cosmetic rules but carry their own [W3]. A hydrocolloid patch can sit on either side of that line depending on claims, so the claims set and the listing should be decided together [verify].
- Audit every retail door and marketplace listing for consistent pricing, pack architecture and review health.

**Months 3-9 (breadth and TikTok Shop)**
- Use the parent's distribution to add drug and grocery doors in the regions where Hero's ACV is lowest; measure incremental households, not just shelf count.
- Build the TikTok Shop storefront and affiliate program. TikTok Shop US beauty sales reached $980 million in Q2 2026, up 82% year on year, with skincare the largest category at $844 million over twelve months [W4]. The peel-off video that made the brand is native to this platform.
- Keep the founder's "break the taboo" story in content; the brand's own CEO credits social media and peel-off videos for its education and growth [W2], and has said on record that Amazon was chosen as the "fastest, cheapest and easiest" way to test product-market fit while the brand stayed profitable from day one [W5].

**Months 9-18 (widen the franchise)**
- Grow the Surface body patch and the balm, cleanser and moisturizer range through the patch's reviews and bundles, so the brand is "acne care" rather than one SKU.
- Revisit evidence. The published hydrocolloid acne trial was a 20-patient pilot showing faster improvement in severity and redness than plain tape [W6]; fund a larger study so the "science-backed" claim stands up to scrutiny as competitors multiply.

## 4. Consumer psychology
- **Reviews and social proof decide.** US shoppers, especially on TikTok and Amazon, buy what they see work. At acquisition Hero's Amazon review count was five times the next competitor's [W1]; that moat erodes if new buyers never see it, so point every ad to the review base.
- **Founder story and personality matter.** Americans buy into people; Ju Rhyu's Korea-to-Amazon story is a channel, not a footnote [W2].
- **Specific beats vague.** Shoppers are skeptical of wellness claims; "absorbs fluid overnight" with the evidence stated beats "clinically proven."
- **Convenience is Amazon-level.** Fast shipping and in-stock at the corner store matter for an impulse, problem-solving product; a breakout does not wait for a Prime delivery.
- **Teens and parents are different shoppers.** Teens discover on TikTok; parents buy at Target and the drugstore. Both need the same pack on the shelf and the same message.

## 5. Brand adaptation
- **Keep:** Mighty Patch Original and Invisible+ as the heroes, the friendly and fun tone, the peel-off content, the Korean manufacturing quality, premium pricing.
- **Change:** claim language aligned to the product's regulatory classification [W3]; distribution weighted to mass, drug and grocery; TikTok Shop as a first-class channel.
- **Add:** a larger clinical study [W6]; adult and parent-facing creative; value multipacks for mass without touching the single-pack price.
- **Drop:** any temptation to chase private-label prices or to launch products outside acne care that dilute the name.

## 6. Competitive reality
- **ZitSticka, Peace Out and Starface:** the named hydrocolloid patch rivals at Ulta, Target, Sephora and Walmart [W1]. Starface owns the "fun" design territory among younger teens; Peace Out owns Sephora. Hero's edge is scale, reviews and parent distribution.
- **Neutrogena and the legacy acne brands:** still number one in acne overall [W1]; they can add patches to existing shelf space at any time.
- **Private label and Amazon multipacks:** the real long-term threat. Patches became 18% of the acne category because shoppers switched from lotions [W1]; a commodity patch at a third of the price is the next switch unless Hero's brand holds.
- **Korean beauty brands on TikTok Shop:** Medicube led Q2 2026 beauty sales on the platform at $62.8 million [W4]; they are the pace-setters for the shelf Hero needs to own.

## 7. Risks
| Risk | Type | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Misclassification or non-compliant claims | Regulatory | Medium | High | Decide classification and claims together; MoCRA facility registration and product listing kept current [W3] [verify] |
| Commoditization by private label | Competitive | High | High | Distribution breadth, review moat, evidence investment [W6], bundles into acne care |
| TikTok dependence | Strategic | Medium | Medium | Build TikTok Shop hard, but keep Amazon and retail as the base; diversify creators |
| Tariffs on Korean-made patches raise landed cost | Cost | Medium | Medium | The US tariff regime changed repeatedly in 2025-2026; model scenarios with a customs broker and consider second sourcing [verify] |
| Over-extension into unrelated skincare | Strategic | Medium | Medium | Launch only products that patch buyers already ask for |
| Retail chargebacks and slotting at mass | Cost | Medium | Low | Parent's existing retail relationships; phased door openings |

## 8. Rewards
- Closing the ACV gap from 8% toward the parent's 80-90% target is the single largest growth lever the brand has [W1].
- A defended premium position at home funds the international distributor model already running in Canada, Australia and Greece [W7].
- Owning "acne care" rather than "the patch" protects the franchise when the patch itself becomes ordinary.

## 9. Validate before you spend
1. Map ACV by channel and region; rank the 10 largest retailers that do not carry Hero.
2. Get a written regulatory opinion on classification and the allowed claim set for each patch [W3] [verify].
3. Run a 60-day TikTok Shop test with 50 affiliates; compare cost per new household with Amazon ads.
4. Commission a design for a larger, independent hydrocolloid acne study; budget it before competitors do [W6].
5. Price-test a mass multipack in two regions without changing the single-pack price.

**Confidence:** High on direction, because the distribution gap is documented; medium on timing, because category commoditization is already visible on Amazon and TikTok Shop.
