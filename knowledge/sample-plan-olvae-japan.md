# SAMPLE PLAN - the format and quality bar
# OLVAE -> Japan

> This is the gold-standard example. Every plan the tool writes should match this structure, this length (roughly 900-1,300 words), and this level of specificity.

**Brand:** OLVAE - clean apparel: 100% organic cotton, linen and hemp daily-wear basics and loungewear, made in India. Positioning: confidence in your own skin. Mission: every purchase helps feed or house an animal.
**Customer today:** upper-middle-class Gen Z women (US), inclusive sizing. Pre-launch, community-first on Instagram.
**Target market:** Japan

---

## 1. The call
**Test first, don't launch yet.** Japan is a strong long-term fit for OLVAE's materials and craft story, but the obvious positioning - "organic cotton basics" - is already owned at a lower price by MUJI and in loungewear by domestic roomwear brands. OLVAE should enter Japan 12-18 months after US launch, through a curated pop-up and a Japanese-language DTC test, leading with how the fabric feels and how it is made in India - not with organic or mission claims.

## 2. Recommended entry point
- **City:** Tokyo (one test city; Osaka later).
- **Channel:** Japanese-language DTC storefront plus one 1-2 week pop-up with a select shop or department store event space.
- **Customer:** women in their 20s-early 30s who already buy premium roomwear and natural-fiber basics, and follow lifestyle creators - not the broad "Gen Z" US definition.

## 3. Entry strategy
**First 90 days (prove there's demand)**
- Translate and localize the product pages, size chart (cm + Japanese sizes), and returns policy with a native copywriter.
- Seed 10-15 pieces with Japanese lifestyle and "slow living" creators on Instagram; measure saves, DMs and waitlist sign-ups from Japan.
- Run a Japan-only waitlist. Go/no-go threshold: [set a number].

**Months 3-9 (earn credibility)**
- Pitch a pop-up to one select shop or a department store event floor, using creator results as proof.
- Ship from India in bulk to a Japanese 3PL or the partner rather than single parcels, and check if Japan-India CEPA lowers duty [VERIFY].
- Collect Japanese-language reviews; they matter more than any ad.

**Months 9-18 (scale what worked)**
- If the pop-up sells through: open a Rakuten or ZOZOTOWN store, but keep pricing disciplined (no deep points promotions in year one).
- Add a gift-packaged line for gifting seasons.

## 4. Consumer psychology
- **Feel beats virtue.** Japanese shoppers buy textiles on hadazawari (how it feels on skin) and kodawari (care in the making). -> Lead every product page with fabric weight, weave, hand-feel and construction detail. "Organic" becomes a supporting fact.
- **Trust is borrowed, not bought.** A select-shop buyer or trusted creator is worth more than paid reach. -> Spend the first budget on seeding and a pop-up, not ads.
- **Quality is inspected.** Loose threads or weak packaging will show up in reviews. -> Japan-standard QC and packaging before the first order ships.
- **Quiet confidence over loud empowerment.** "Confidence in your own skin" translates well as an idea, but US-style bold empowerment copy can feel pushy. -> Express it through ease, comfort and self-care framing.

## 5. Brand adaptation
- **Keep:** natural fabrics, Indian craft and making story, the tulip-garden visual identity (soft palette fits Japanese aesthetics well), the animal mission.
- **Change:** headline message from materials-as-values to materials-as-feel; casting and styling to Japanese creators; sizing to Japanese fits; packaging to gift standard.
- **Drop:** comparison-style or superlative claims ("best", "cleanest"); any US-centric slang in copy.

## 6. Competitive reality
- **MUJI:** organic cotton basics at accessible prices, everywhere. OLVAE cannot win on "organic basics" alone - it must win on design, fabric feel and story.
- **Domestic roomwear brands (e.g. gelato pique):** own the loungewear-as-self-care category with a strong brand world. OLVAE's opening is a more natural, less sugary aesthetic.
- **Imported natural-fiber labels in select shops:** the real comparison set for a pop-up. [name 3-5 of these]

## 7. Risks
| Risk | Type | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Positioned as "pricier MUJI" | Cultural/competitive | High | High | Lead with feel, design and craft; never with organic alone |
| Landed cost too high in a weak-yen market | Cost | Medium | High | Price in JPY from landed cost; bulk shipping; check CEPA |
| Labeling non-compliance | Regulatory | Medium | Medium | Japanese quality labels per the Household Goods Quality Labeling Act before shipping [VERIFY] |
| QC defects damage early reviews | Operational | Medium | High | Extra QC pass for Japan orders |
| Mission feels like marketing | Cultural | Medium | Low | Share the animal mission through real stories and photos, as proof, not a slogan |

## 8. Rewards
- A Japanese select-shop or department-store stamp is credibility that travels - it helps OLVAE's story in the US and the rest of Asia.
- Japanese shoppers are loyal once trust is earned; repeat purchase on basics can be strong.
- Creates a second market for OLVAE's India production beyond the US.

## 9. Validate before you spend
1. Do 5 conversations with Japanese women in the target age range about how they choose loungewear (feel vs brand vs price).
2. Check the Japanese import duty and CEPA eligibility for OLVAE's HS codes [VERIFY].
3. Price check: list 10 comparable items at MUJI, gelato pique and two select-shop labels, in JPY.
4. Run the 90-day creator seed and waitlist test above.

**Confidence:** Medium. Strong on consumer psychology and channel logic; low on landed-cost math until duties and shipping are quoted.
