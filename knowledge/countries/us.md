## 0. Thesis (the non-consensus point of view)
[TANI] Draft to react to: The US is not one market - it's a set of regional and community markets with one shared checkout. Foreign brands fail by launching "nationally" with a national budget that buys nothing. The winning move is to pick one city or one online community and become the obvious choice there first.

## 3. Consumer psychology (each insight -> so what)
- Convenience expectations are set by Amazon: fast shipping, free returns, easy checkout. -> Slow cross-border shipping is a conversion killer; plan US fulfillment early.
- Founder story and brand personality matter; shoppers buy into people and communities. -> Founder-led content is a real channel, not a nice-to-have.
- Reviews and social proof are decisive, especially on TikTok and Amazon. -> Seed reviews and creator content before scaling paid ads.
- Ethical and wellness claims are common and shoppers are skeptical of vague ones. -> Specific, provable claims (fiber %, certification name, factory) beat broad ones.
- Huge regional variation in climate, taste and price tolerance. -> Pick a beachhead region and audience.

### Section 301 tariffs from July 24, 2026
Source: USTR final notice, 91 FR 47318 (Jul 28, 2026), checked economy by economy in the notice text: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15181/notice-of-actions-in-section-301-investigations-of-acts-policies-and-practices-of-various-economies. The tariff is added on top of the normal MFN duty, except where a combined cap applies. All 60 investigated economies are listed; an economy not listed here is not covered by this action.

| Rate | Economies |
|---|---|
| **10%** (17) | Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Trinidad and Tobago, United Kingdom |
| **10% combined cap** (2) | European Union, Taiwan |
| **12.5% combined cap** (3) | Japan, South Korea, Switzerland |
| **12.5%** (38) | Algeria, Angola, Australia, Bahrain, Brazil, Chile, China, Colombia, Costa Rica, Dominican Republic, Egypt, Guyana, Hong Kong, Iraq, Israel, Kazakhstan, Kuwait, Libya, Morocco, New Zealand, Nicaragua, Nigeria, Norway, Oman, Peru, Philippines, Qatar, Russia, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Africa, Thailand, The Bahamas, Türkiye, United Arab Emirates, Uruguay, Venezuela, Vietnam |

- **Combined-cap rule (EU, Taiwan, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland):** the Section 301 tariff tops the normal MFN duty up to the cap (10% for the EU and Taiwan, 12.5% for Japan, South Korea and Switzerland). If a product's MFN duty is already at or above the cap, the Section 301 tariff is zero. Example: an EU product with 4% MFN duty pays 6% Section 301 (10% total); one with 16.5% MFN pays no Section 301.
- **Exempt for all 60 economies (Annex II, Part A, no scope limitation):** smartphones (8517.13.00), computers (all of 8471, including laptops 8471.30.01), computer parts (8473.30), routers (8517.62.00), semiconductors (8541, 8542), bulk vitamins and vitamin mixes (2936) and vitamin medicaments in dosage form (3004.50). See `research/us-technology.md` and `research/us-health-wellness.md`.
- **Not exempt, so they pay the rate above:** finished supplements under 2106.90.99 (that line is exempt only for açaí preparations for beverages), botanical extracts (1302), protein concentrates and isolates (2106.10, 3504), and consumer tech accessories: chargers (8504.40), lithium-ion batteries and power banks (8507.60), earbuds and headphones (8518.30.20) and speakers, which are exempt only as civil-aircraft parts. See the same two notes.
- Also exempt: goods already under Section 232 tariffs; some countries have extra exemptions in their own part of Annex II. Older China Section 301 lists (2018-19) may still stack on Chinese goods. [VERIFY the HTS code and stacking with a customs broker]
- De minimis: the $800 duty-free exemption for low-value shipments was suspended for all countries from Aug 29, 2025 [VERIFY current status]. -> Shipping single orders cross-border into the US now carries duties and brokerage; bulk import to a US warehouse is usually cheaper.
- (Apparel) Textile labeling: Textile Fiber Products Identification Act - fiber content, country of origin, manufacturer identity; care labeling rule [VERIFY].
- Environmental claims: FTC Green Guides govern claims like "eco-friendly" or "organic"; vague claims are risky [VERIFY latest update status].
- (Beauty) FDA MoCRA - facility registration and product listing [VERIFY]; California has additional ingredient laws; Prop 65 warnings in California.
- (Food, Restaurants) FDA registration, labeling, allergen rules; restaurants also need state and city health permits [VERIFY].
- Sales tax: varies by state; economic nexus thresholds after Wayfair [VERIFY].

### Section 232 tariffs on wooden furniture, cabinets and vanities (checked October 2026)
Sources: Proclamation 10976, 90 FR 48127 (Oct 6, 2025), https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/10/06/2025-19482/adjusting-imports-of-timber-lumber-and-their-derivative-products-into-the-united-states ; amendment, 91 FR 1039 (Jan 9, 2026), https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/09/2026-00327/amendments-to-adjusting-imports-of-timber-lumber-and-their-derivative-products-into-the-united

- (Home) Since Oct 14, 2025: **25%** on certain upholstered wooden products (upholstered furniture) and **25%** on kitchen cabinets and vanities, plus **10%** on softwood timber and lumber. Only products listed in the proclamation's Annex I are covered; non-upholstered wooden furniture such as a solid-wood dining table is not named [VERIFY the HTS line against Annex I].
- (Home) The increase to **30%** (upholstered furniture) and **50%** (kitchen cabinets and vanities) was moved from Jan 1, 2026 to **Jan 1, 2027** by the December 31, 2025 amendment, except for countries that reach a wood-products agreement with the US.
- (Home) Caps: for **EU and Japan** goods, the Section 232 duty plus the normal MFN duty may not exceed **15%** in total; for **UK** goods the Section 232 duty may not exceed **10%**. Other countries pay the full rate on top of MFN duty.
- (Home) Goods under these Section 232 tariffs are not subject to the reciprocal (IEEPA) tariffs, and the Section 301 action from July 24, 2026 exempts goods already under Section 232 (see above) [VERIFY stacking per HTS line with a customs broker].
- **So what:** upholstered furniture from outside the EU, Japan and the UK lands with 25% on top of MFN duty now and 30% from January 2027; model both in pricing. EU and Japanese makers face a 15% all-in ceiling, which narrows the gap with US-made furniture.
