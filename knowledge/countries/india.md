## 0. Thesis (the non-consensus point of view)
[TANI] Draft to react to: "India" is not one market. A foreign brand that enters "India" usually means it enters metro, English-speaking, Instagram-native shoppers in a few cities - a market of real size, but one that behaves very differently from the rest of the country. The winning move is to pick that slice deliberately and price for it, not to chase national scale.

## 3. Consumer psychology (each insight -> so what)
- Price-value scrutiny is intense, even among affluent shoppers; discounts and sale events (e.g. festive season around Diwali) drive a large share of buying [VERIFY]. -> Plan launch pricing with festive promotions in mind; don't launch premium into the middle of a discount season without a reason.
- Trust signals: cash on delivery, easy returns, reviews, and recognizable marketplaces lower first-purchase risk [VERIFY current COD share]. -> Offer COD or marketplace presence early; DTC-only with prepaid can stall.
- Foreign brands carry aspiration, but local D2C brands have raised the bar on price and relevance. -> "Imported" is not enough; the brand needs India-specific relevance.
- Family and community influence purchase decisions more than in the US. -> Content and offers that work for gifting and family can widen the funnel.
- Regional and language diversity is huge. -> Start with one or two metros and English plus Hindi; expand language only with demand.
- Climate: hot most of the year in much of the country. -> Breathable natural fabrics have a functional, not just ethical, story.

## 5. Regulations, tariffs, costs
- Import duties on finished apparel are high (often 20%+ effective once all levies are added) [VERIFY per HS code, current year]. -> Imported goods start at a big price disadvantage vs local brands; local manufacturing changes the math entirely.
- GST applies on top; apparel GST rates depend on price band [VERIFY current slabs after the 2025 GST rationalization].
- FDI: single-brand retail allowed with conditions, including local sourcing requirements above certain foreign ownership levels [VERIFY].
- Cosmetics import requires registration with CDSCO [VERIFY].
- Food: FSSAI licensing and labeling [VERIFY].
- Legal Metrology rules: packaged goods need MRP and specific declarations on labels [VERIFY].

## 6. Cultural fit risks
- Western-only casting and styling.
- Ignoring festive calendar (Diwali, wedding season, regional festivals).
- Pricing set by converting USD.
- Messaging that sounds preachy about values to a market that has long-standing traditions around natural fibers and craft.

## 7. Category notes
- Apparel: India has deep textile heritage and is a leading organic cotton producer [VERIFY]. -> An "organic cotton" claim alone is weak in India; craft, fit and design have to carry it.
- Skincare: ingredient-led D2C brands grew fast; Ayurveda-inspired positioning is crowded.
- Food: strong local palates, vegetarian demand, price sensitivity.
