## 0. Thesis (the non-consensus point of view)
[TANI] One or two sentences that the tool should lead with for any brand entering Japan.
Draft to react to: Japan rewards foreign brands that win on craft and material proof, not on values messaging. Brands that lead with "eco" or mission claims read as marketing; brands that lead with how the product feels, how it's made and how long it lasts earn trust - and trust in Japan is slow to build and very sticky once earned.

## 2. Tanishka's own observations (from her CAVA -> Japan project, 2026)
- Sell what the category means in Japan, not where the brand is from. CAVA should enter as "a Mediterranean lifestyle brand, not an American import": Mediterranean food already signals health, longevity and quality there. -> Position on the category's local associations; home country only helps if it is itself a quality signal.
- Keep the core nobody can copy, adapt the edges (transnational strategy). CAVA keeps its spice blends, dips and Crazy Feta, and adds Japan-only items: matcha drinks, miso-glazed vegetables, a less-sweet yuzu panna cotta, and curated set bowls next to build-your-own. -> Every plan should say what stays, what changes and what's added.
- Enter through a local partner, not a hotel. Hotel tie-ups reach travellers looking for familiar food, not locals. A partner bridges language, regulation and specialty supplier networks, and its knowledge transfers when the brand goes independent.
- Hybrid supply: import the soul, source the rest. Import signature ingredients (olive oil, tahini, spice blends); buy perishables locally, where quality and safety standards are high. Tokyo hub or commissary kitchen, cold chain, small frequent deliveries because urban stores have little storage; small-format stores.
- Japanese diners research before they try: Instagram, LINE and Tabelog matter most. LINE had about 96M users in early 2024 (DataReportal) and suits loyalty and coupons. People are reserved in person but candid online. -> Monitor Tabelog and social reviews monthly; use creators in the first six months.
- Gen Z attention can be earned through Japanese IP collaborations (Sanrio-style or anime limited editions) that make a foreign brand feel local faster than ads.
- Teams: expat trainers for launch plus local hires; adapt management to horenso (report, contact, consult) with clear reporting lines.

## 3. Consumer psychology (each insight -> so what)
- Quality and defect intolerance: Japanese shoppers inspect finish, packaging and presentation closely; small defects drive returns and bad reviews. -> QC and packaging must be at Japanese standard before launch, not after.
- Kodawari (particular attention to detail / obsessive care in making): products with a clear "why it's made this way" story sell at a premium. -> Lead with the making story: origin, method, why each detail exists.
- (Apparel) Hadazawari (how a fabric feels on skin) is a buying vocabulary in apparel and bedding. -> For textile brands, sensory language beats ethical language.
- Trust is built through third parties rather than brand-owned ads. (Retail products) select shops, department store buyers and magazines; (Restaurants) Tabelog reviews, food creators and a credible local partner. -> Borrow credibility rather than buy reach.
- Risk aversion with unknown brands: shoppers want reviews, a Japanese-language site, and clear return/after-sales info. -> Localize the full purchase path, not just the product page.
- Aging market: about 29% of people are over 65 (Santander Trade). Older shoppers are more price-conscious and very loyal to a brand or store once won. -> Loyalty is the prize; health and wellness demand keeps growing.
- After decades of flat prices, inflation and wage growth are returning slowly (Bank of Japan, 2025), so shoppers are value-conscious about new price levels. -> Justify the premium clearly.
- High uncertainty avoidance and long-term orientation: buyers and partners want proof and decide slowly. -> Plan long timelines and lead with evidence.
- Seasonality matters: distinct seasons and seasonal menus or collections. (Retail products) gift seasons (ochugen in summer, oseibo at year end) [VERIFY timing] can be a real channel. -> Time launches to season changes.
- "Eco" claims are not a strong standalone premium driver for most mass shoppers [VERIFY with interviews]. -> Treat values as a supporting reason to believe, not the headline.

## 4. Entry routes (when each one fits)
| Route | Fits when | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-border DTC (Japanese-language Shopify, JPY pricing, local payment options) | Testing demand cheaply; brand has strong content | Shipping cost/time, duties paid at delivery hurt conversion; need Japanese customer service |
| (Retail products) Marketplaces: Rakuten Ichiba, Amazon.co.jp | Proven product, competing on search and price | Rakuten is promotion- and points-heavy; can cheapen a premium brand early |
| (Apparel) Fashion platforms: ZOZOTOWN | Apparel with existing demand signals | Selective; usually needs a Japanese entity or partner [VERIFY] |
| (Apparel, design goods) Select shops (e.g. BEAMS, United Arrows, Journal Standard) | Brand with a strong design/craft point of view | Long buying cycles, consignment or wholesale margins |
| (Retail products) Department store pop-ups (e.g. Isetan, Hankyu) | Testing in person, building legitimacy fast | Short runs, high cost, need staff who speak Japanese |
| Importer / distributor / agent | Brand wants Japan without building a team | Lose control of pricing and brand; contract terms must protect positioning |
| Licensing / JV | Large brands with proven global demand | Not a v0 recommendation for small brands |
| (Restaurants) Local partner, franchise or JV operating small-format stores | Restaurant chains; partner handles leases, hiring, licensing and suppliers | Profit share and control; pick a partner with food-service experience |
| (Restaurants) Food hall stall or short-term kitchen in an office district | Testing demand before signing a long lease | Limited menu and capacity; still needs a food business licence |

Default for small retail-product brands: earn credibility first (curator, pop-up or creator seeding), then scale through DTC and marketplaces. Don't lead with Rakuten discounting.
Default for restaurants: enter Tokyo through a local partner with one or two small-format stores near office demand, backed by a commissary kitchen; restaurants do not use marketplaces, select shops or gift seasons.

## 5. Regulations, tariffs, costs
- (Apparel) Labeling: Household Goods Quality Labeling Act requires Japanese-language fiber composition, care and labeler info on textiles; care symbols follow JIS L 0001 (aligned to ISO) [VERIFY].
- (Restaurants) Restaurants need a food business licence from the local health centre and a food hygiene manager [VERIFY].
- (Apparel) Sizing: Japanese sizing runs smaller; size charts need cm and Japanese size equivalents.
- Payments: credit card, convenience-store payment (konbini), and QR wallets like PayPay are common. -> Card-only checkout loses buyers.
- Operating costs: labor, Tokyo real estate and compliance costs are high versus other Asian markets, and strong employee protection makes downsizing slow and costly. -> Start small, lean on partners and 3PLs before hiring a large local team.
- Currency: the yen can swing sharply, which moves landed cost and margins. A weak yen makes imported goods expensive in yen terms [VERIFY current rate]. -> Pricing must be built in JPY from landed cost, not converted from USD.

### Import duties, taxes and entry rules (checked October 2026)
From the Oct 15, 2026 import-duty research (official customs and regulator sources where available; third-party lookups labeled).

#### How landed cost is built
- No country-wide extra tariff tier: duty depends on the tariff line, the product's characteristics and its origin. [Japan Customs, tariff system](https://www.customs.go.jp/english/summary/tariff.htm)
- **Import consumption tax: 10%, or 8% for qualifying food and non-alcoholic drinks**, on customs value plus duty and excise. Medicines and quasi-drugs do not get the food rate. [Japan Customs, calculation](https://www.customs.go.jp/english/c-answer_e/imtsukan/1111_e.htm)
- "Free" means zero duty, not zero tax or zero regulatory work.

#### Trade agreements: the rate depends on where goods are made, with proof
- A lower EPA, RCEP or CPTPP rate applies only if the goods qualify as originating and the importer holds the proof the agreement requires; goods transiting a third country may also need direct-consignment evidence. [Japan Customs, EPA guidance](https://www.customs.go.jp/english/epa/index.htm), [origin procedures](https://www.customs.go.jp/english/c-answer_e/imtsukan/1524_e.htm)
- Example, (Apparel) cotton dresses, HS 6204.42-200: general 11.2%, WTO 9.1%. **Free** under the EPAs with India, Vietnam, ASEAN, Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, the Philippines, Australia, Switzerland, the EU and the U.K., under CPTPP, and under RCEP for ASEAN/Australia/NZ. **5.7%** from China under RCEP. No U.S. preference listed, so U.S.-made dresses pay 9.1%. Free for least-developed countries. [Kanzei, HS 6204.42](https://www.kanzei.or.jp/statistical/tariff/detail/index/e/6204422000)
- Example, (Health & wellness) protein preparation, HS 2106.90-296: 12.5%; **free** under most EPAs (India, ASEAN, Australia, EU, U.K., CPTPP, Japan-US Trade Agreement); 7.8% under RCEP for ASEAN/Australia/NZ. [Kanzei, HS 2106.90-296](https://www.kanzei.or.jp/statistical/tariff/detail/index/e/2106902965)
- Under RCEP, Japan phases out apparel duties over about 21 years and is slowest for China; its Asian agreements generally use fabric-forward origin rules. [Sheng Lu Fashion, 2022](https://shenglufashion.com/tag/rcep)
- **So what:** specific qualifying apparel lines made in India, Vietnam, the EU, the U.K. or Australia can enter Japan duty-free with origin proof. Not every apparel line does, so check each product's HS line and the agreement's origin rule (OLVAE: check each India-made garment under the Japan-India CEPA). Technology is already duty-free, so origin brings no duty advantage there.

#### Small parcels and cross-border online sales
- Shipments with a total customs value of **¥10,000 or less** are exempt from duty and consumption tax (not from liquor or tobacco tax). **Excluded:** leather bags, handbags and gloves; knitted apparel (T-shirts, sweaters); ski boots; leather shoes and leather-soled footwear. Values combine per invoice, per package and across parcels sent together, so don't split consignments to qualify. [Japan Customs FAQ 1006](https://www.customs.go.jp/english/c-answer_e/imtsukan/1006_e.htm)
- The 2026 tax reform withdraws the ¥10,000 **consumption-tax** exemption from **April 1, 2028**: overseas sellers will need Japanese consumption-tax registration, and platforms with over ¥5bn in sales become deemed suppliers. [VATCalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/japan/japan-consumption-tax-low-value-consignment-threshold/)

#### Entry rules by category (non-tariff)
- (Beauty) A prefecture-issued **marketing license** (qualified supervisor, GQP/GVP, domestic office and storage), a **manufacturing license** for any repacking or labeling in Japan, a marketing notification to the prefecture, and a foreign manufacturer notification to PMDA before import. Ingredients follow the Standards for Cosmetics (30-substance negative list; positive lists for preservatives, UV filters and others). Japanese labels with full ingredients. **Quasi-drugs** (e.g. medicated whitening or acne products) need MHLW approval and GMP inspection, about 6 months. [ChemLinked Cosmepedia](https://cosmetic.chemlinked.com/cosmepedia/japan-cosmetic-regulation) Foreign beauty brands almost always enter through a Japanese license holder.
- (Food, Health & wellness, Restaurants) Every commercial food shipment needs an **import notification** to an MHLW quarantine station (Food Sanitation Act, Art. 27); only approved additives; Japanese labels before clearance. [MHLW, imported food](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/kenkou_iryou/shokuhin/yunyu_kanshi/index_00017.html) Supplements are food unless they contain ingredients on the drug list; health claims only through FOSHU, Foods with Function Claims or Foods with Nutrient Function Claims. [ChemLinked Foodpedia](https://food.chemlinked.com/foodpedia/japan-food-regulation) Therapeutic claims can move a product into drug or quasi-drug regulation. [PMDA FAQ](https://www.pmda.go.jp/english/about-pmda/0004.html)
- (Technology) Products under the Electrical Appliances and Materials Safety Act need importer notification, conformity work, inspection and the **PSE mark** before sale. [METI, PSE for importers](https://www.meti.go.jp/english/policy/economy/consumer/product_safety/pse_procedure/index.html) Wireless devices need Radio Law technical conformity [VERIFY with a local compliance specialist].

#### Worked examples
- **Smartphone:** customs value ¥100,000, HS 8517.13: duty ¥0; consumption tax 10% = about ¥10,000.
- **Food supplement:** customs value ¥100,000, a 12.5% line, food-rate tax: duty ¥12,500; 8% tax on ¥112,500 = about ¥9,000; total about ¥21,500. Invalid if the product is a medicine or quasi-drug or falls in a milk or sugar line [VERIFY with a customs broker].

**So what:** Japan is duty-light for tech and beauty but never tax-free. Apparel depends on origin, and supplements depend on formula and claims. Don't quote a supplement landed cost until ingredients, claims, pack size, HS line and origin are reviewed.

## 6. Cultural fit risks
- Overclaiming ("best", "#1", "revolutionary") reads as untrustworthy.
- Translation that is technically correct but unnatural kills premium perception. Use native copywriters.
- Imagery: casting, styling and tone should be adapted; loud US-style lifestyle ads often underperform quieter, product-detail-heavy content.
- Customer service expectations are very high; slow replies damage reputation.

## 7. Category notes
- (Apparel) Apparel / loungewear: strong roomwear culture (gelato pique is a well-known domestic roomwear brand). MUJI sells organic cotton basics at accessible prices, so "organic cotton basics" alone is not differentiated [VERIFY current MUJI range].
- (Beauty) Skincare: very competitive domestic market, ingredient-literate shoppers, sunscreen and gentle formulations big. Regulatory route needed before selling.
- (Food, Restaurants) Food / fast casual: one of the world's largest, most demanding food-service markets. Healthy, customizable, locally grown options already exist, so differentiation is hard. Rice is a staple; portions, flavor (less sweet), seasonality and presentation matter; build-your-own needs curated sets alongside it. Matcha and ready-to-go meals are everywhere; konbini set the low price anchor. Price reference: CAVA planned about ¥1,200-1,800 per bowl, above konbini and below sit-down (Tanishka's CAVA project). Food Sanitation Act and an importer of record apply.
- Cities: Tokyo (finance, startups, about 75% of foreign companies' Japan HQs), Osaka (trade, food services, pharma, electronics), Nagoya (manufacturing, bold-flavored local food). Launch where your category is concentrated; for most consumer brands that is Tokyo first.
