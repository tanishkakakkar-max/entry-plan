# Tara Silver → United States  

## 1. The call  
**Test first, then go.** The U.S. market is large enough to sustain an “accessible‑premium” recycled‑silver line, but the category is already crowded with both domestic indie brands and established retailers. Tara Silver’s differentiators – Jaipur artisan hand‑finishing and 100 % recycled sterling – are compelling, yet they will only win if they reach the right micro‑community first and prove demand without a costly national rollout. Start with a single‑city DTC test and a focused Instagram/creator push; expand nationally only after a clear sell‑through benchmark is met.

## 2. Recommended entry point  

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **City** | Los Angeles (West Coast) – high concentration of ethically‑oriented shoppers, strong Instagram creator ecosystem, and a logistics hub for quick U.S. fulfillment. |
| **Channel** | Brand‑owned Shopify store with U.S.‑based 3PL (e.g., ShipBob) **plus** a 2‑week pop‑up in a curated boutique (e.g., The General Store) to generate press and social proof. |
| **Customer** | Women 22‑35, fashion‑forward, follow “small‑brand” Instagram accounts, value transparency and recycled materials, and are willing to spend $35‑$120 on everyday pieces. |
| **Goal** | Reach 300‑unit sell‑through in the pop‑up and 150‑unit DTC orders in the first 90 days; collect ≥150 email sign‑ups for a wait‑list. |

## 3. Entry strategy  

### Phase 1 – 90 days (validation)  
1. **Logistics** – Set up a U.S. fulfillment center (ShipBob) to receive a bulk shipment from Jaipur (see tariff note below). This avoids paying duty parcel by parcel now that the US has ended the $800 de minimis exemption [verify current rules].  
2. **Localization** – Hire a U.S. copywriter to adapt product copy, size/fit guidance (ring sizes US), and “Made in India” labeling to meet the Textile Fiber Products Identification Act requirements.  
3. **Creator seeding** – Send 30 pieces (rings, hoops, pendants) to micro‑influencers (10‑30k followers) who regularly feature ethical jewellery on Instagram and TikTok. Track saves, DMs, and link clicks to a U.S.‑only landing page with a “Notify me when we launch in LA” form.  
4. **Paid micro‑test** – Allocate $2,500 to Instagram story ads targeting LA zip codes 90001‑90089, directing to the wait‑list page.  

**Go/no‑go metric:** ≥120 wait‑list sign‑ups + ≥30 % click‑through on creator posts = move to Phase 2.

### Phase 2 – 3‑9 months (credibility & scaling)  
1. **Pop‑up execution** – Partner with a boutique that already stocks sustainable accessories. Offer an exclusive “LA‑only” design (e.g., a small charm with a local motif) to drive foot traffic. Capture on‑site email sign‑ups and sell‑through data.  
2. **Full‑fill from U.S. warehouse** – Ship a second bulk container from Jaipur to the 3PL. This reduces per‑order landed cost versus direct‑to‑consumer parcels.  
3. **Retail‑partner outreach** – Use the pop‑up sales deck and creator‑generated UGC to pitch to boutique chains (e.g., Nordstrom Rack’s “Local” program). Aim for 2‑3 wholesale accounts in the first year.  
4. **Community building** – Launch a monthly “Craft Story” newsletter featuring the Jaipur artisans, reinforcing the “Everyday heirloom” narrative.  

### Phase 3 – 9‑18 months (national rollout)  
If Phase 2 yields ≥70 % sell‑through and positive press, open a permanent DTC storefront with U.S.‑wide free shipping (minimum order $100) and list on Etsy’s “Handmade Jewelry” category for broader discovery. Consider a limited partnership with a major ethical‑goods marketplace (e.g., Thrive Market) for additional reach.

## 4. Consumer psychology  

- **Story over label.** U.S. shoppers of recycled jewellery care about provenance, but they need tangible proof. Hand‑finished videos and artisan portraits convert better than “recycled” wording alone.  
- **Instant gratification.** Amazon’s fast‑shipping benchmark means a 2‑day U.S. delivery expectation is now standard; bulk‑import to a local 3PL satisfies this.  
- **Social proof is king.** Instagram carousel posts showing “stacked looks” and real‑customer photos generate the “I can wear this daily” mindset that drives repeat purchases.  
- **Price framing.** Position $35‑$120 pieces as “premium everyday” rather than “luxury” – use comparative phrasing (“same price as a single pair of designer earrings”).

| Keep | Change | Drop |

## 6. Competitive reality  

- **Recycled-metal DTC jewellery brands** – Recycled‑metal, strong Instagram presence, similar price tier. Tara must differentiate with Jaipur craft details and a “made in India” narrative rather than generic recycled metal.  
- **Premium personalised jewellery brands** – Offers personalization and higher‑price range; Tara can occupy the “affordable premium” gap.  
- **Local indie silver brands on Etsy** – Compete on price and design; Tara’s advantage is verified artisan wages and a clear supply‑chain story.

| Consumer skepticism of “recycled” claims | Cultural | High | Medium | Use FTC Green Guides to substantiate “100 % recycled” with third‑party certification; feature artisan video proof on product pages | 
| Returns cost for jewellery (size mismatch) | Operational | High | Medium | Offer free US returns via the 3PL; provide clear US ring‑size conversion chart; pre‑sell limited‑edition sizes to reduce mismatches | 
| Limited brand awareness in a crowded market | Market | High | High | Front‑load creator seeding and pop‑up exclusivity; capture UGC for organic reach; postpone national paid media until sell‑through validates demand | 

## 8. Rewards  

- **Differentiated positioning** – Being the first U.S. brand to highlight Jaipur artisan hand‑finishing on recycled silver gives a unique story angle that can be leveraged in PR and influencer content.  
- **High‑margin repeat purchases** – Stackable rings and pendants encourage multiple‑piece buying; once a customer trusts the brand, average order value can climb toward the $120 tier.  
- **Supply‑chain scalability** – Jaipur artisans can increase output without major capital expense, allowing Tara to meet U.S. demand once the beachhead proves profitable.  

## 9. Validate before you spend  

1. **Customs check** – Confirm the exact HTS code and duty rate for each product type (rings, hoops, pendants) with a customs broker, including any additional tariff on Indian goods [verify].  
2. **Cost model** – Build a landed‑cost spreadsheet including MFN duty, any additional tariff on Indian goods, freight, 3PL fees, and returns handling; ensure the $35‑$120 price range retains ≥40 % gross margin.  
3. **Consumer interview** – Conduct 8‑10 short video calls with LA‑based women (22‑35) about jewellery buying triggers, preferred Instagram accounts, and willingness to pay for recycled metal.  
4. **Creator pilot** – Ship 20 pieces to selected micro‑influencers, track engagement, and set a minimum 5 % click‑through to the landing page as the go/no‑go threshold.  
5. **Pop‑up feasibility** – Negotiate a short‑term lease with a boutique, confirm foot‑traffic stats, and calculate projected sell‑through vs. rent and staffing costs.  

**Confidence:** Medium. Consumer psychology and channel logic are solid; tariff and landed‑cost calculations need verification before full‑scale investment.
